# Chiplog implementation architecture

## Status and authority

This document records the implementation architecture accepted through architecture interview round R06. It is subordinate to [`VISION.md`](../VISION.md), `VisionVersion: 2026-08-22.2`, whose assurance status is **bounded-incomplete**. Where this document selects a mechanism, that mechanism is a realization of the product contract, not a new product-semantic source. A stage that does not yet implement a long-term capability does not weaken or waive that capability.

The architecture inherits, and cannot clear, the VISION's explicit assurance holds: the repaired Plan/Fact/Journal and commitment/reliance material has not been independently re-cleared; natural-language interaction, external-information handling, and institutional/governance design have not received dedicated terminal attacks; and neither a whole-document canonical-parent coverage audit nor a full regression sweep exists. Acceptance of an architectural mechanism, a passing local fixture, or a row in the cross-reference below does not independently verify the VISION or change `HOLD_ADOPTION`. Only a successor VISION acting as product authority, or the exact independent readiness and release processes governed by the current VISION, may change those statuses.

The architecture targets the long-term product and supplies an evolutionary path whose first executable slice is the historical four-hour MVP. The MVP is evidence for only the exact capability and provider conditions it exercises. It does not make Telegram, Google Calendar, explicit confirmation, single-user operation, or any MVP omission permanent.

R01 began with an empty target document. R02 preserved its decisions and closed seven implementation choices. R03 replaced checkpoint-centered coordination with the authoritative durable event graph. R04 closed that graph's frontier protocol and lease/evidence split, and added the tenant conversation, delivery, composition, concurrency, typing, SQLite writer, dashboard, and journal architecture. R05 resolved the apparent deletion contradiction by adopting the product authority's strict logical-deletion boundary rather than physical erasure, made channel visibility non-confidential for the initial single-principal tenant, reserved writer capacity for authenticated late evidence, and bound each Turn workspace to one authoritative read frontier. R06 realizes those boundaries with typed provenance and an authoritative deletion fence, monotone disclosure-label propagation with separately authorized narrowing, and durable-inbox materialization as the sole boundary for local evidence-follow-up authorization. Atomic invariant islands such as domain revision plus effect intent or the indivisible publications required by `VISION.md` remain transactions local to the node handling the relevant input.

R04's stronger premise—that conformance required a physical transformation making payload bytes irrecoverable—was superseded after grounding against `VISION.md`: version one must prevent ordinary retrieval, processing, and replay from reconstructing deleted content, but does not claim erasure from the SQLite file, WAL, filesystem snapshots, or backups. Physical non-reconstructive deletion remains a desired future capability and cannot be cited as version-one evidence.

## Goals and non-goals

### Goals

- Preserve the product boundaries for human authority, Plan/Fact/Journal, external effects, privacy, affected-party rights, and release governance.
- Build one production agent loop used unchanged by Telegram, CLI, scheduler, and evals.
- Make every consequential transition durable, attributable, tenant-scoped, replay-safe, and inspectable.
- Separate authoritative local state from asynchronous provider convergence.
- Support recovery without treating uncertainty as failure or silently repeating an effect.
- Make architecture claims executable through tests, scenario evals, and end-to-end traces.

### Non-goals

- Service decomposition, distributed transactions, or a control-plane tenant catalog in the first version.
- Transparent portability between SQLite and PostgreSQL.
- Database migration support in version one.
- Production exposure or adoption readiness. `VISION.md` currently requires `HOLD_ADOPTION` until its independent readiness and release gates pass.
- Replacing the product-semantic registries and invariants in `VISION.md` with simpler implementation enums in this document.

## Architectural decisions

### Deployment and tenancy

The normative startup authority is not the database file, a deployment file, or an ordinary configuration value. Initialization creates immutable `DatabaseGenesis { tenant_id, database_instance_id, genesis_version }` in the tenant database plus a platform-secured, authenticated, non-rollbackable `TenantDecisionJournal` and `TenantTrustHeadRegistry` outside the tenant database, WAL, snapshots, backups, deployment files, and restore domain. The journal is the source of truth for every authority, replay, credential/session, evidence-local-follow-up, and issued-work head advance. The trust-head registry is the source of truth for the one current deployment-binding and journal-root epoch. Their canonical monotone lineages are independent of the materialized database bytes and of any caller-presented binding.

Before a tenant database transaction may commit one of those advances, the journal irreversibly publishes exactly one `DECIDED_COMMIT { tenant_id, database_instance_id, command_id, command_fingerprint, predecessor_journal_head, predecessor_materialized_head, predecessor_materialization_commitment, complete_canonical_event_write_batch | authenticated_recovery_payload, resulting_heads, resulting_materialization_commitment, schema_version, canonicalization_version }`. The payload is sufficient to reproduce and authenticate the complete exact database transaction without consulting lost database/WAL bytes. Publication is CAS-linearized on the exact predecessor, command fingerprint, and predecessor commitment. Once `DECIDED_COMMIT` exists it cannot abort, be replaced, or choose another batch: the SQLite commit is an idempotent materialization of that decision, after which the journal records `MATERIALIZED` and, for authenticated evidence ingress, may record `LOCAL_ACK_AUTHORIZED`. That last state means only that the exact inbox decision is durably materialized and its registered transport follow-up is locally authorized; it never asserts network emission or remote receipt. Identical replay returns the one recorded lineage; changed or rival reuse conflicts.

The authority-bearing storage universe is defined independently of its commitment rows. A build-generated, operator-authenticated, immutable `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest` enumerates every physical SQLite base table and column, classifies each as authority-bearing or non-authority-bearing, and enumerates the closed executable broker/repository authority-read graph down to the exact operation-to-table/column edges. Its artifact identity/digest, schema identity/version, read-graph identity/version, canonicalization version, and runtime owner/capability fingerprints are bound by `DeploymentTenantBinding`; the exact current manifest head/digest is also included in the predecessor and resulting materialization commitments and their already-present `resulting_heads`. Build, initialization, every startup/restore, clean replay, and replacement-build admission independently enumerate the actual schema and registered executable authority-read graph and require exact equality with this authenticated manifest. An unknown table/column, unclassified storage member, unregistered authority-read operation or edge, runtime-reachable authority read outside the graph, or manifest/version mismatch fails before broker or database admission.

The journal-authenticated `resulting_materialization_commitment` covers a separately generated closed versioned `AuthorityMaterializationRegistry`: every non-rebuildable canonical authority-bearing base-record kind and exact canonical row content, together with its ordered decision prefix, resulting heads, schema, canonicalization version, registry fingerprint, and exact `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest` head/digest. Completeness is a bidirectional equality proof: every authority-bearing storage member and every storage dependency reachable from an executable authority-read edge has exactly one AMR entry, every AMR entry maps to exactly one manifested authority-bearing member/read dependency, and no member or edge may be silently classified away by the AMR generator. Unknown, omitted, duplicated, non-canonical, extra, or orphaned members/edges/entries cannot produce a commitment. The database stores the selected journal head and commitment only as a cache. On every startup and restore, a broker-owned verifier first re-proves manifest/schema/read-graph equality, then independently enumerates and canonicalizes the actual base records, recomputes the complete commitment from raw stored content rather than projections or the self-reported head, and byte-compares it with the exact current authenticated journal commitment. A same-head content substitution or newly reachable omitted field therefore rejects. Any mismatch, unverifiable record/edge, or unsupported version admits no existing byte; the only recovery fallback is deterministic replay of the complete decided lineage into a fresh empty database followed by a second manifest-equality and full-commitment check before the old database is discarded or any work is admitted.

`PREPARED` may precede decision only to reserve bounded journal resources. It grants no authority and may abort only after a positive authenticated journal observation proves that no `DECIDED_COMMIT` exists for the exact preparation/command/predecessor. Absence, timeout, unavailable journal state, ambiguous response, or rival observation holds admission. A crash at any cut therefore yields exactly no decision, one irrevocable decision awaiting materialization, one materialized decision awaiting any applicable local follow-up authorization, or an authorized transport-specific follow-up whose attempt or external observation may still be absent or unknown. If database bytes or WAL are destroyed, truncated, modified, or restored to any snapshot, recovery verifies genesis, authenticates the complete journal prefix, verifies the actual authority-bearing materialization through the claimed head, replays every later `DECIDED_COMMIT` in lineage order, and recomputes the final commitment before broker epoch, adapter, cache, ingress, scheduler, evidence, credential, replay, or authority admission. No database observation may overrule, skip, abort, fork, or authenticate a decided batch or its resulting content.

`DeploymentTenantBinding` is a canonical record signed by a distinct operator deployment key held in the protected OS credential store. It binds only immutable deployment identity and trust roots: exact tenant/database/genesis, operator signer-key identity/version, the platform journal authority and journal identity/version/root key and epoch, the exact `TenantTrustHeadRegistry` authority/identity/version/root, the exact `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest` identity/digest and schema/read-graph/runtime versions, expected lineage rules, binding schema/version, predecessor binding head, and binding digest. It does not sign mutable per-operation heads. Canonical assembly begins from the registry's protected monotone current trust head, retrieves and authenticates that exact binding, root, and authority-surface manifest, and rejects a caller-presented authentic predecessor as stale. It then authenticates the journal lineage and current materialization commitment under that root and admits work only after the database passes manifest equality, full content verification, and replay. Missing/unavailable registry or journal state, wrong authority/key/version, stale/forked binding or journal lineage, unresolved transition/preparation, impossible materialized head or commitment, unknown authority surface, or mismatch fails closed.

Binding-key or journal-root rotation is one closed cross-root successor protocol whose sole linearizer is one registry-owned monotone `TrustTransitionHead`. Its closed state union is `CURRENT { current_binding/root, generation } | PREPARED { transition_id, predecessor_current, generation, readiness_fence, manifest_digest } | READY { exact_prepared_head, generation, manifest_digest, old_ready_attestation_digest, final_old_journal/materialization_commitments } | ACCEPTED { exact_ready_head, generation, manifest_digest, new_acceptance_digest } | ABORTED { exact_prepared_head, generation, manifest_digest, abort_reason }`; a successor `CURRENT(new)` or restored `CURRENT(old)` is published only from the exact applicable terminal transition head. A `PREPARED` CAS occupies the unique active slot and rejects a second/rival transition. `READY` and `ABORTED` are mutually exclusive CAS successors of the same exact `PREPARED` head and generation. The old root may produce a signed readiness attestation over the complete manifest and final old commitments, but it is a non-authoritative candidate: only the registry `PREPARED → READY` CAS publishes `SUCCESSOR_READY`, consumes the exact readiness fence, and freezes ordinary old-root authority. An `ABORTED` winner rotates that fence/generation; the journal and broker reject any late readiness candidate or ordinary command carrying the spent generation. Timeout, absence, stale negative observation, or a readiness record outside the selected registry head never authorizes abort or recovery.

`PREPARED → READY` enters the same broker-owned common order as every old-journal decision/materialization invalidator. Inside that non-awaiting critical section, its single CAS re-reads the exact selected `PREPARED` head/generation and live readiness fence, the exact current final old journal decision head, and its exact current materialization head/commitment; it byte-compares all of them with the signed readiness attestation and manifest before publishing `READY`. An ordinary `DECIDED_COMMIT` or materialization advance that wins first makes the candidate stale, writes no `READY`, and requires a freshly signed readiness attestation over the new final heads. If `READY` wins first, its fence consumption freezes ordinary old-root decision admission in that same order. Neither a prior validation nor a signed stale candidate can satisfy this CAS.

Every transition carries one canonical `TrustSuccessorManifest`, a closed discriminated union of `OPERATOR_BINDING_KEY_ROTATION | JOURNAL_ROOT_ROTATION`. Common fields bind the transition identity/kind, exact predecessor trust/transition head and generation/fence, complete old and proposed `DeploymentTenantBinding` canonical bytes/digests, operator authorization, exact tenant/database/genesis, `TenantTrustHeadRegistry` authority/identity/version/root, journal authority/identity/version, `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest` identity/digest, expected lineage rules, schema/canonicalization versions, and the exhaustive kind-specific permitted-delta set. Both kinds require byte equality for tenant ID, database instance ID, complete `DatabaseGenesis`, trust-registry identity/authority/root, journal identity/authority, authority-surface manifest, lineage rules, and every other field not explicitly mutable for that kind. `OPERATOR_BINDING_KEY_ROTATION` may change only the operator signing-key identity/version and the derived successor binding head/digest/signature/predecessor link. `JOURNAL_ROOT_ROTATION` may change only journal root identity/key/epoch and those same derived binding-lineage/signature fields. Schema/version, genesis, registry, tenant, database, journal-identity, authority-surface, or any unlisted delta is unrepresentable and rejects; version-one import/relabel has no transition kind.

The registry `PREPARED` record binds the full manifest digest. After the exact old-root readiness candidate is authenticated, `PREPARED → READY` binds its digest and final commitments; only then may the new root publish immutable `PREDECESSOR_ACCEPTED` over the same manifest plus exact registry `READY` head and old-attestation digest. The registry authenticates that candidate and CAS-publishes `ACCEPTED`; only `ACCEPTED → CURRENT(new)` admits the new root. Directional signing has no circular digest dependency. Before `READY`, exact old remains current and may operate; from `READY` through `ACCEPTED`, ordinary old and all new work hold while only exact rotation recovery progresses; after `CURRENT(new)`, new is sole current and every old binding/root rejects even if its signatures remain valid. Crash recovery begins exclusively from the one registry transition head and requires every record named by it; it never infers state from a journal candidate or falls back to an authentic predecessor. Post-CAS old-root closure is audit-only. Identical commands resume or return the recorded state; stale generation/fence, rival preparation, late readiness after abort, changed manifest/field/delta, skipped state, unsigned candidate, or partial record conflicts.

Same-tenant restore never rewinds authority and in version one accepts only an exact-current-version snapshot/journal tuple. Its ceremony starts from the protected current trust head, verifies the exact schema/genesis/journal-schema/canonicalization/registry/companion-manifest versions, snapshot provenance, historical head and full authority-materialization commitment, restores the bytes offline, then deterministically replays every later same-version decided batch and recomputes the current commitment before admission. A historical or mismatched snapshot is `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION / HOLD_ADOPTION`, not a restore input. Version one prohibits cross-tenant restore and relabel: a database, snapshot, journal prefix, binding, credential, principal, session, replay identity, evidence state, or issued work of tenant A can never be adopted as tenant B. A new tenant always starts empty with a fresh genesis, trust head, journal root and `BOOTSTRAP_REQUIRED` contour. Any future data movement is a separate held authenticated import/re-enveloping capability with its own complete transformation, provenance, principal/credential disposition and tenant-bound fingerprint contract; it is neither restore nor exact replay and has no version-one command or surface. At no crash or restore/rotation cut can old credentials/sessions, consumed replay identities, locally authorized evidence follow-up, or issued broker work become current. The shorter genesis/path paragraph below is subordinate to this complete contract.

The first stable topology is one Python 3.14 `asyncio` deployable organized as a modular monolith. Service extraction is allowed only in response to a measured operational boundary; it is not assumed in advance.

Tenant and principal are distinct first-class identities. A tenant is a security and data domain; a principal acts inside it. The initial deployment has one process serving exactly one tenant database selected at process startup. That database choice is the deployment-level tenant selection. There is no application-level tenant router, control-plane catalog, cross-tenant query, or cross-tenant transaction in version one.

Database-path selection is not itself tenant authentication. The sole version-one initializing script creates an empty store and writes one immutable `DatabaseGenesis { tenant_id, database_instance_id, genesis_version, schema_version, journal_schema_version, canonicalization_version, registry_version, companion_manifest_version }` with the exact current registered version tuple and complete genesis companion records. Ordinary startup resolves the exact current tenant/database/binding/root from `TenantTrustHeadRegistry`, then authenticates the corresponding `DeploymentTenantBinding`. The canonical assembly opens the file and protected journal read-only enough to decode genesis and version heads before replay, byte-compares every tuple member with the running assembly, and admits no broker epoch, cache, adapter, owner compartment, ingress, scheduler, evidence lane, replay/materializer or authority-bearing operation until the version gate and subsequent content checks succeed. A historical or mismatched tuple deterministically enters typed `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION` under `HOLD_ADOPTION`; it publishes no journal decision, trust transition, database materialization, broker epoch or scheduler/worker record. Version one has no synthesis, adoption or migration path for it.

After exact current-version equality, startup uses one closed partition. Canonical empty genesis may run only the current initializing script. An authenticated current-version `DECIDED_COMMIT` with absent or exact canonical-prefix materialization may complete that exact commitment idempotently; a complete exact state returns replay. Current-version corrupt, extra, rival, non-prefix, mismatched or incomplete-companion state enters typed integrity/recovery hold and publishes nothing. A non-empty database with absent, unknown, duplicate, changed, cross-tenant or unverifiable genesis/content likewise fails closed. Ordinary startup cannot adopt, rename, rewrite, relabel, synthesize companions or infer identity from a path; version one has no cross-tenant restore/relabel path.

Each tenant begins with immutable `TenantPrincipalContour.SINGLE { principal_id? }`. A newly initialized database is `BOOTSTRAP_REQUIRED` and has no principal until the one-shot local bootstrap completes. The canonical process console emits a cryptographically random short-lived single-use token; only the configured OS user may present it over the bootstrap-only local Unix socket. One serializable transaction consumes the exact unexpired token, creates one immutable `principal_id`, binds its first credential, creates an offline recovery-secret verifier and the initial contour/current credential heads, writes the complete audit event, and changes the tenant to `ACTIVE`. The recovery secret itself is returned once and is never stored in the tenant database. Concurrent, replayed, expired, wrong-peer, second-principal, or changed-fingerprint bootstrap attempts reject. While `BOOTSTRAP_REQUIRED`, every Telegram surface, ordinary CLI surface, scheduler, provider/channel evidence adapter, model invocation, tool, delivery, cache publication, owner capability, and non-bootstrap write is absent or denied.

Normal credential rotation and revocation are authenticated principal operations over exact current credential/session heads; successful rotation atomically binds a new credential, revokes the replaced credential and all derived sessions, and appends an audit event. Emergency recovery is a distinct bootstrap-independent operator ceremony available only through its local Unix socket to the configured OS user and only with the offline recovery secret. It never changes tenant or `principal_id`: one transaction revokes every credential and session, binds one new credential to the same immutable principal, rotates the recovery-secret verifier, and records the complete ceremony. Missing, stale, replayed, competing, or partially matching recovery inputs fail closed, and neither ordinary configuration nor possession of the tenant database can create a replacement principal.

Every ingress identity, command, proposal, revision, run, effect intent, receipt, projection row, trace, and eval fixture is tenant-bound even when the physical database contains only one tenant. Tenant identity participates in canonical fingerprints. A repository or adapter call without an explicit tenant context is invalid.

Version-one tenant payload isolation is the database-file boundary: one process opens one tenant file selected at startup. The protected decision journal necessarily retains canonical decided batches or sufficient authenticated recovery payloads for the authority-bearing head families above, but it is a narrow per-tenant platform recovery authority, exposes no tenant routing or cross-tenant query, and is not a general conversation/domain read model. The deployment-key store contains no tenant payload. Tenant-specific payload-encryption keys and backup namespaces remain out of scope until sensitive data or a second tenant makes a stronger boundary necessary; restore authorization itself is already required by the journal ceremony above. This deferral cannot support an adoption-readiness claim for a capability whose applicable vision clauses require the stronger controls.

The single-principal contour is a mechanical capability gate, not a convention. Startup, ingress, context assembly, conversation/history reads, screen and cache keys, tool authorization, evidence correlation, routing, disclosure and delivery each require the exact current contour head and reject an unknown or additional principal, shared endpoint, or contour mismatch. `MULTI_PRINCIPAL` is not a version-one configuration value. A successor release may add it only through one atomic versioned capability migration after provenance-compatible context/history/tool-result/cache isolation, disclosure labels, endpoint ownership, policy and delivery isolation have each been implemented and independently attested. The transaction publishes the complete new contour plus every prerequisite identity together; configuration edits or row insertion cannot activate a second identity or shared surface.

“Independently attested” is a closed mechanism, not a label. `PrincipalContourPrerequisiteRegistry` exhaustively enumerates every required property and complete surface inventory for provenance, context/history/tool-result/cache isolation, labels, endpoints, policy, authority, routing, disclosure and delivery; unknown, omitted, duplicated or extra rows reject. The closed versioned `PrincipalContourAttestation` union is issued only by an attester capability independent of the implementation owner and contour-migration authority. Each value binds registry/prerequisite identity, artifact identity/version/digest, complete surface-inventory identity/digest, test/evidence artifact identities/digests, tenant/database/current contour/deployment-state heads, attester key/head, validity/freshness interval, schema/version, stable command identity and predecessor head. The broker authenticates the attester, recomputes registry completeness and artifact/surface digests, consumes freshness/replay, and transaction-locally revalidates the exact current prerequisite fixed point while publishing the contour and complete accepted-attestation manifest. Self-attestation, stale/current-head mismatch, unknown surface, invalid signer, expiry, changed bytes under one identity, rival predecessor, reordered/partial manifest or a racing state change writes nothing; identical replay returns the recorded disposition.

### Internal modules and dependency direction

The monolith has these logical modules:

| Module | Owns | May depend on |
|---|---|---|
| `ingress` | Telegram and CLI normalization, authenticated channel identity, ingress deduplication | application commands and queries |
| `agent_loop` | Run/Turn orchestration, model calls, tool dispatch, durable orchestration events, suspension, and derived resume cursors | proposals, planning, policy, effects, projections through application ports |
| `interpretation` | immutable interpretation revisions and exact proposals | prompt artifacts, query snapshots, policy reads |
| `planning` | typed commands, append-only planning revisions, Plan authority | domain primitives and persistence ports |
| `policy_authority` | grants, current heads, holds, conflict and state-head validation | domain primitives and persistence ports |
| `effects` | outbox intents, dispatch authorization, provider attempts, delivery attempts, receipts, reconciliation | policy/planning read contracts and provider ports |
| `evidence_journal` | Fact claims, candidate evidence, provenance, trace and receipt linkage | domain primitives and persistence ports |
| `projections` | rebuildable query models and channel-neutral user-visible state | committed revision streams |
| `evals` | scenario arrangement, production-loop assembly, observers, graders, replay manifests | every public application port; never private orchestration forks |
| `platform` | SQLite transactions, clock, IDs, scheduler, model/provider/channel adapters | no domain policy decisions |

The modular monolith is a set of cooperating bounded hexagons. Each capability module owns its domain/application core and typed inbound and outbound ports. Calls into another module enter only through that module's public inbound port. A single composition root wires Telegram, CLI, SQLite, model, scheduler, provider, and eval adapters. Domain/application code does not depend on those adapters; direct adapter-to-adapter calls, foreign-adapter-to-domain calls, and imports of another module's private packages are forbidden. There is no shared executable-code exception: reusable helpers are duplicated and independently versioned inside their owner modules. Only values expressed in the closed inert shared-data language below may cross owner packages.

“Modular monolith” here means one versioned deployable, one tenant database, one logical application/composition contract, and one release and operational lifecycle; it does not require every trust domain to share one OS process. The canonical assembly starts one tenant-scoped `AuthorityBroker` trusted process and one replaceable process-isolated `RuntimeGraphGeneration` compartment for every capability owner—each authority-bearing hexagon and every plugin—behind local allowlisted IPC. These compartments are not independently deployed product services, own no separate business data or public API, and cannot be versioned or operated apart from the monolith. Hexagon ownership and application ports remain the logical dependency boundary; the broker is a narrow platform TCB that enforces owner-partitioned capabilities, public-port routing, and commit fencing but makes no domain-policy decision.

This direction is enforced against a generated, versioned, machine-consumed `HexagonBoundaryManifest`, not inferred from naming conventions. The manifest exhaustively assigns every importable executable package, module, and exported symbol to exactly one owner hexagon and `PUBLIC_PORT | PRIVATE` visibility. Separately, it inventories shared data artifacts as `INERT_SHARED_DATA`, declares their exact canonical bytes/schema identities, declares the exact allowed owner/visibility dependency edges, and declares every public-port parameter, return, exception, generic bound and reachable capability. Generation enumerates the installed distribution, source and test trees, type exports and re-exports, and registered extension metadata and compares that universe with the manifest. An unknown, duplicate-owned, unclassified, newly re-exported, or differently fingerprinted package, module, symbol, signature member, capability, edge, or shared artifact fails the architecture gate.

`INERT_SHARED_DATA` is a closed non-executable data language containing only canonical schema declarations, primitive value types, and literal constants. It contains no function or method bodies, lambdas, custom validators or serializers, descriptors, properties, hooks, factories, executable initialization, domain enums, registries, service locators, owner-dependent branching, imports with side effects, or values whose construction, validation, comparison, hashing, iteration, attribute access, serialization, or decoding can execute user code. The trusted generator parses and re-emits this language without importing its source and rejects unknown syntax or type forms. Generated owner-local Pydantic models may consume the inert declarations, but their executable validators and helpers belong to and are fingerprinted with that owner. Moving any policy or authority discriminator into an otherwise signature-clean shared artifact therefore either makes the artifact executable/owner-dependent and rejects it, or leaves only inert data interpreted by the consuming owner's versioned policy.

A public port may expose only owner-local DTO implementations generated from manifest-declared `INERT_SHARED_DATA` or owner-local types, plus typed inbound/outbound `Protocol`s. Its transitive signature/capability closure cannot contain a foreign repository, event store, database connection/session/transaction, persistence or provider adapter/SDK, container or scope handle, service locator, generic `Callable`/`Any`/opaque execute callback, factory returning an undeclared type, or another hexagon's private concrete type. The gate resolves aliases, generic arguments, annotations and forward references to their canonical owners; an unresolved or dynamically widened type fails closed. Thus a lexically legal public import cannot smuggle a private persistence or provider capability.

The manifest also contains one versioned synchronous `PublicPortCall` request/response routing graph. Its nodes are exact owner/public-port/operation identities and its directed edges are the complete admitted synchronous calls, including callbacks and reverse calls. Generation computes the full graph from source, registrations, generated clients, plugin metadata, eval substitutions, and every bootstrap and rejects any unknown edge or directed cycle before admission. Synchronous cross-owner calls may therefore traverse only this DAG. A logical reverse edge or cyclic workflow is represented instead by independently identified durable command/event choreography: publication commits without waiting for a reverse response, correlation is observational only and cannot lend caller authority, and each consumer emits one closed typed durable success, failure, timeout, unavailable, uncertainty, or recovery disposition under its own owner and identity.

No code may await a cross-owner call while holding any transaction, SQLite writer/admission slot, owner-local serialization lane, scheduler/worker lease mutation guard, mutex, semaphore permit, file lock, or other manifest-listed exclusive resource. Every synchronous routed call carries a finite versioned nested-depth budget, remaining call budget, absolute broker-clock deadline, caller/callee/session/generation identity, and exact timeout policy; the broker decrements/checks them before delivery and response emission. Exhaustion, deadline, owner drain, generation replacement, broker restart, or unavailable callee durably closes the call as a typed `PublicPortFailure` or exact durable uncertainty/recovery obligation and never waits indefinitely or silently retries. Child calls cannot extend their parent's deadline or budget.

The manifest gate combines static dependency and signature analysis with import-time/runtime mutation fixtures. It inventories and rejects undeclared targets reached through `importlib`, module-qualified strings, plugin/entry-point loading, `getattr`, `sys.modules`, registries and service locators, reflection, injected callables and factories, public re-export aliases, and test helpers. Registration and resolution APIs accept only manifest identities and attest the resolved canonical owner, export and capability set; arbitrary module/symbol strings or untyped callbacks are not extension contracts. Static AST cleanliness alone is never evidence for this invariant.

A generated versioned `BootstrapManifest` exhaustively enumerates every executable and composition entry: console scripts, supported `python -m` modules, Telegram/CLI process starts, scheduler and worker launchers, recovery/reconciliation/admin commands, eval and test fixtures, and plugin entry points. Distribution metadata, executable modules, worker registrations, pytest fixtures and extension metadata are enumerated and byte-compared with it. Every entry delegates to one canonical assembly function and binds the same root/provider-manifest identity; it cannot create a container or instantiate an adapter directly. An unknown executable/bootstrap, second assembly function, unregistered plugin root, or incomplete inventory fails startup/tests before work admission.

Static architecture tests, negative and mutant fixtures, bootstrap enumeration, and runtime graph attestation jointly enforce these boundaries. Ingress, evals, and platform may use declared public application ports but cannot obtain planning persistence or private agent-loop orchestration. Provider payloads and model outputs enter as untrusted input and cannot append planning authority without schema, policy, authority, tenant, and current-head validation. The eval harness assembles the same public production ports rather than bypassing a hexagon.

### Persistence and authority

The closed event-kind and batch registry admits `RecoveredToolOutcome` absence-to-presence as the third changed-head class in an `AdmissibleFrontierSuccessor`, in addition to separately authenticated evidence and typed obligation disposition/closure. That zero-to-one transition is legal only in the registered original-stream resolver's owner-local batch that CAS-closes the exact still-open obligation, and it binds the exact original call/obligation identity, authenticated evidence head, reducer/version, and resulting closure head. No other writer or batch, evidence without closure, closure without its required outcome, outcome without closure, one-to-one rewrite, or rival zero-to-one publication is admissible; exact batch identity and complete fingerprint replay, while any changed or rival member conflicts.

Typed append-only domain revisions are authoritative. Current state and presentation are projections that must be fully rebuildable from those revisions. Commands and revision variants remain explicit; the store must not reduce the domain to an untyped generic event stream.

The agent loop additionally has a typed, durable orchestration event graph. It records workflow facts and acknowledgements; it does not replace the domain revision families or grant planning authority. Each node deduplicates a stable input identity and atomically appends only the records owned by handling that input. Cross-hexagon progress is choreography through durable events, never checkpoint authority.

All typed events share one authoritative event-sourced tenant log. It assigns monotone tenant-local `commit_seq` in commit order; `event_id` supplies idempotency, `stream_id` supplies logical grouping, and explicit causal predecessor identities encode dependencies. User/provider timestamps are payload, not ordering authority. A handler may atomically append a small local invariant batch. Equal identity and fingerprint replays the prior result; a changed fingerprint or impossible rival terminal conflicts. `ConversationHistory`, `RunTrace`, domain heads, and screens are separate rebuildable projections over this log, not separate physical journals.

Every sealed response batch binds a versioned `FanOutBound { max_call_count, max_manifest_bytes, max_serialized_batch_bytes, canonicalization_version }`. The writer derives all sizes from the canonical response, manifest, and complete atomic batch bytes and rejects an over-bound, overflowed, non-canonical, or differently versioned batch before publishing any member. Replay requires the same bound and canonical bytes; configuration changes apply only to later response identities.

The event graph versions every event over tenant, Run, Turn, kind, stable subject, full fingerprint, causal predecessors, producer, and canonicalization version. `ModelResponseReceived` seals the ordered tool-call set. A versioned `RecoveryFrontierRegistry` exhaustively declares the scoped head families for recovery: the Run and Turn heads; the sealed model-response head; one head for every sealed tool-call identity; every referenced effect, evidence, delivery, authority/mandate, policy, prompt/tool-schema, recipient, and semantic-binding head; and the execution-lineage head when the Run was scheduler-rooted. For each family it fixes subject-key extraction, cardinality, terminal/conflict rules, canonical serialization and ordering, and canonicalization version.

For every sealed call the registry contains exactly one `CallRecoveryFrontier` value, a closed discriminated union keyed by the immutable original `call_id`. `TERMINAL` contains exactly one exact call-level `ToolTerminalDisposition` head, exactly one acceptance-consistent typed result or exact recovery-obligation head, the exact initialized-or-accepted call head, every required acceptance-batch execution/effect-intent head, and—only when the disposition is `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`—one stable recovered-outcome subject keyed by `(call_id, recovery_obligation_id)` with canonically serialized `ABSENT` or exactly one immutable `RecoveredToolOutcome` head. For every other terminal disposition the recovered-outcome member is canonically `NOT_APPLICABLE`, distinct from `ABSENT`. `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` contains exactly one current pending head, canonically `ABSENT` call-level terminal-disposition and `ReadOnlyCallOutcome` heads, the complete canonically ordered manifest of every initialized call head, accepted-attempt head, per-attempt outcome/result-or-obligation head, the unique shared lineage-counter head, frozen reducer id/version and budget version, and every applicable execution/read-only-proof/snapshot/binding head. `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `ABSENT`, and `PRESENT(head, fingerprint)` are distinct canonical markers; a branch tag precedes fields, fields follow registry order, attempt members follow ordinal then stable identity, and nested fingerprints cover the exact canonical bytes. A call has cardinality exactly one across the two branches: neither zero, both, duplicate, unknown, or hybrid membership can construct a frontier.

One consistent SQLite snapshot enumerates registry rows and actual scoped subjects in registry order, rejects unknown or duplicate families/subjects, invalid cardinality or branch markers, and constructs a membership manifest of `(family, subject, branch, ordered-head-or-marker, fingerprint)` plus the registry/version fingerprint. The canonical frontier digest covers that manifest, not a caller-supplied list. Startup coverage and a second snapshot enumeration prove that every in-scope subject appears exactly once; omission, addition, duplicate, non-canonical ordering, a terminal head beside pending, a pending head beside a call-level outcome, or another impossible cross-branch combination fails construction rather than yielding a smaller valid digest. The only branch changes are exact registered writer transitions from `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` to a new `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` after atomically closing the old pending head and admitting the next attempt, or to `TERMINAL` through the complete-lineage reducer or exact recovery-obligation reduction. An identical complete command replays; changed or rival branch publication conflicts. Every `SuspensionBaseline` and baseline/current or predecessor/successor proof includes the exact branch and all its members and proves each permitted transition against the original call and lineage identities.

SQLite is an intentional first store. Authority, outbox, inbox/deduplication, Run/Turn trace, receipts, and rebuildable projections share one database so that local invariants can commit atomically. Repositories are domain-shaped and may use SQLite semantics directly. PostgreSQL adoption requires a recorded trigger such as concurrent writers, required isolation, replication, or measured load; it is a designed migration, not an adapter swap.

One `AuthorityBroker`-owned `EventAppender` task is the only SQLite writer. It accepts bounded typed write commands through an async `EventStore` Protocol, keeps transactions short, performs generation/session/operation fencing plus CAS/idempotency/lease and semantic ownership validation, rejects any operation or storage edge absent from the exact current `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest`, canonically assembles the complete transaction and resulting authority-materialization commitment, and obtains the exact `DECIDED_COMMIT` before opening the corresponding SQLite commit. The SQLite write byte-compares command, predecessor, batch, resulting heads, manifest head/digest, and predecessor/resulting commitments with that decision; it atomically applies the base records and stores the selected journal head and commitment. Recovery invokes the same idempotent materializer, but startup admission independently re-proves physical-schema/read-graph/AMR equality and recomputes the complete commitment over the actual authority-bearing base state. No authority/replay/credential/session/evidence-local-follow-up/issued-work head may advance through a database-only or unmanifested read/write path.

The writer's admission surface has a separately bounded lane for independently authenticated provider, channel, and reconciliation evidence; its volatile queue is only a duplicate/loss-tolerant wakeup over durable heads and is never custody, acceptance or restart authority. Every `EvidenceIngressSurfaceManifest` row names its exact earliest irreversible source-handoff cut and one of two realizable acquisition mechanisms. A cooperative/redelivering source proves its registered retention/handshake through local exact-byte durability. A destructive read—such as consuming a CLI socket/pipe frame or another delivery whose upstream copy may disappear—first journal-decides and materializes `EvidenceReceiptToken.ALLOCATED_LOSS_SLOT` before the broker issues the receive/read. That token binds tenant/database, source row/class, endpoint/account where known, broker epoch/session, admission epoch/fence, receive-slot and stable receipt/loss identity, authentication stage `PRE_AUTH`, byte-ownership state, exact maximum bytes, transport/handshake version, predecessor and canonical command fingerprint. The bounded read may then exact-CAS the token to `RAW_STAGED { exact inert raw bytes, digest }` before authentication. Both states are no-authority: they cannot acknowledge, advance a cursor, answer CLI success, release custody, publish normalized ingress or prove evidence. A crash before the read leaves an unused enumerable slot; a crash after destructive read but before staging leaves that exact slot as a durable loss obligation; a crash after staging recovers the exact inert bytes.

From `RAW_STAGED`, or from the exact retained-source token under its proven contract, authentication and admission publish exactly one `EvidenceCustodyState` successor for that receipt token. Its closed union remains `ADMITTED_DURABLE | RETRY_WITH_SOURCE_CUSTODY | TERMINAL_REJECT | QUARANTINED_RAW_EVIDENCE | LOSS_OBLIGATION`. Local custody and quarantine contain exact replayable canonical raw bytes plus digest and complete source/tenant/authentication/admission bindings; digest alone is never custody. `RETRY_WITH_SOURCE_CUSTODY` requires exact continuing source retention/redelivery and grants no follow-up. `TERMINAL_REJECT` binds one registered deterministic non-evidence reason. `LOSS_OBLIGATION` binds the exact token/slot and custody-loss cut and never authorizes acknowledgement. Token-to-disposition cardinality is exactly one: identical replay returns the recorded successor; changed/rival/second successor conflicts. Restart and drain enumerate the bidirectional token↔successor join, including unconsumed slots, pre-auth staged bytes and loss slots, before ordinary work.

Evidence admission and shutdown share one broker-owned `EvidenceAdmissionEpoch` CAS order with the journal writer. The epoch stores a fixed canonical class order containing every manifest source class plus `ORDINARY`, positive byte quantum `Q_i`, one-item service quantum, maximum accounted item bytes `M_i`, physical per-class item/byte/quarantine reserves, deficit cap `D_i ≥ M_i`, and exact bounded-integer item/byte accounting. Physical owner headroom is never borrowed or occupied by another class. Only uncommitted scheduling credit may be borrowed and synchronously revoked without moving durable evidence. Every class ready queue is a strict stable FIFO over `ReadyAdmissionGeneration { durable_admission_commit_seq, stable_tie_identity }`; the EventAppender allocates the sequence and timestamps never order it. A later ready generation cannot overtake an earlier still-ready generation. Each scheduler cycle visits every one of the `C` classes exactly once and serves at most one ready head per visit.

Admission of any ready item `x`, including `ORDINARY`, atomically publishes an immutable `AdmissionSelectionBoundSnapshot`. It binds epoch/fairness/formula versions, class/order and `C`, `Q_i`, `M_i`, `D_i`, current capped deficit `d_0`, origin scheduler slot, the complete ordered set of then-ready predecessor generations with their exact heads and accounted byte sizes, `x` identity/head/size and an immutable bound-lineage ID. For the ordered sequence `y_1 … y_k, x`, it computes with checked arithmetic `v_j = min { n ≥ 1 | min(D_i, d_(j-1) + n·Q_i) ≥ size(y_j) }` and `d_j = min(D_i, d_(j-1) + v_j·Q_i) − size(y_j)`. Because every admitted size is at most `M_i ≤ D_i`, every `v_j` is finite. The absolute admission-to-selection deadline is `origin_scheduler_slot + C·Σ(v_j)`; overflow, an incomplete predecessor snapshot or an over-depth/over-byte snapshot is a typed capacity/integrity hold before admission. Later arrivals are absent from that snapshot, enter after `x`, and cannot overtake it. Selection transaction-locally byte-compares the remaining ready prefix, heads, sizes, deficit evolution, current slot, bound lineage and absolute deadline; identical replay returns the bound and changed/rival state conflicts.

Ready FIFO contains only dependency-ready generations. If a predecessor ceases to be ready, its next class visit consumes exactly one recorded skip slot and one common-order CAS moves that generation to a separately durable `BLOCKED_HOLD` queue; while blocked it is outside ready FIFO and has no running bound. The same bounded EventAppender batch advances the actual deficit to `min(D_i, d + Q_i)` without subtracting blocked bytes, closes every affected descendant snapshot as `PREFIX_REBASED_BLOCKED`, and publishes replacement snapshots over the complete remaining prefix. Each replacement preserves the original bound-lineage ID, origin and absolute deadline, records all consumed slots including the skip, recomputes only the remaining recurrence, and must prove `current_scheduler_slot + remaining_bound_slots ≤ original_absolute_selection_deadline_slot`; it never restarts or extends the deadline. Epoch limits ready depth and aggregate descendant-snapshot bytes so this total rebase always fits one registered batch; otherwise the earlier admission was refused. Crash/replay exposes either the entire old prefix or the block plus complete rebase, never a partial set. Unblock preserves stable work/evidence identity but creates a new ready generation at the FIFO tail with a fresh admission snapshot/deadline. If `x` itself blocks, its old bound closes as `BOUND_SUSPENDED_BLOCKED`; no bound runs during the blocked interval.

Every submission linearizes to exactly one durable custody disposition before the transport-facing call can succeed. An oversized or full-reserve submission remains under proven source custody as `RETRY_WITH_SOURCE_CUSTODY`; if custody may already have been lost it becomes `LOSS_OBLIGATION`, never an implicit drop or success. Shutdown CASes the epoch `OPEN → QUIESCING → DRAINING → CLOSED`; allocation of every durable receipt token competes with `OPEN → QUIESCING` in that same order. `QUIESCING` rejects a later token allocation, fences receive/read issuance for rejected slots, waits for every winning token to publish one successor under cancellation shielding, and then publishes immutable `EvidenceDrainManifest` containing the epoch/fence, scheduler parameters/trace, all tokens and every disposition through the fence. `DRAINING` resumes all local durable heads; timeout, cancellation, forced exit and broker crash leave the manifest/remainder restart-enumerable. `CLOSED` requires producer quiescence and total token/successor accounting. Overload stops new Turn/tool work before evidence reserves, while late authenticated evidence uses the same bounded protocol independently of Run/worker state.

After the exact authenticated inbox decision is materialized, the journal may record `LOCAL_ACK_AUTHORIZED { decision_id, inbox_head, transport_contract, followup_subject, authorization_version }`. This asserts only a durable local authorization to execute the registered follow-up. Transport state is separate and closed: push uses `PUSH_RESPONSE_ATTEMPT_ISSUED` before broker-owned socket emission, `PUSH_RESPONSE_LOCAL_COMPLETION_OBSERVED` only when the local stack reports completion, and `PROVIDER_RECEIPT_OBSERVED` only for a separately authenticated provider receipt; CLI uses `CLI_RESPONSE_ATTEMPT_ISSUED`, `CLI_RESPONSE_LOCAL_COMPLETION_OBSERVED`, and no remote-receipt claim; polling uses `POLL_CURSOR_ADVANCE_AUTHORIZED` then locally durable `POLL_CURSOR_APPLIED` for the exact next-poll cursor; reconciliation uses `RECONCILIATION_RELEASE_AUTHORIZED` then `RECONCILIATION_OBLIGATION_RELEASED` for the exact broker-owned source obligation. The version-one polling `transport_contract` is closed to `DURABLE_CURSOR_BEFORE_REQUEST`: `POLL_CURSOR_APPLIED` must commit before the broker may create or emit any polling request carrying that cursor. A provider contract that requires request emission before durable cursor application is unsupported until it defines its own durable pre-emission attempt, typed unknown-outcome, reconciliation, and no-blind-retry states. Each state has a stable subject, exact predecessor, attempt identity, transport/session/contract version, replay semantics, and typed unknown outcome where applicable.

For every polling response, the broker first authenticates the complete canonical raw provider page and, before member parsing, derives one immutable `PollResponsePageManifest { provider/transport_contract, request_attempt, authenticated_session, predecessor_cursor, candidate_next_cursor, raw_page_digest, ordered_complete_member_identities_and_digests, manifest_digest, schema/canonicalization_version }`. The raw-page framing rules, not successfully parsed items or a caller-selected subset, determine ordered membership; missing, extra, duplicated, reordered, partially authenticated or digest-mismatched members reject the manifest. Each member then reaches exactly one durable cursor-safe disposition in the closed union `MATERIALIZED { exact_inbox_head } | DUPLICATE_OF { exact_durable_inbox_subject, head, digest } | TERMINAL_NON_EVIDENCE_REJECT { exact_closed_versioned_reason } | QUARANTINED_RAW_EVIDENCE { exact_custody_head }`. The reject branch is admissible only when the registered deterministic reason proves that the exact authenticated member cannot contain admissible evidence. Quarantine is distinct: before it can authorize page completion it must durably retain the exact authenticated replayable raw member bytes and digest, source/page/member identity, parser/schema/error type and versions, bounded inspection/retry policy, admission epoch and quarantine budget; every later parser version must enter through the single `QuarantineReprocessingHead` algebra below without rewriting those bytes. Unavailable, ambiguous, retryable, unknown, open, oversized-with-source-custody or quarantine-capacity-exhausted outcomes hold the complete page and cursor. A parser failure may advance only through the exact durable quarantine branch; poison redelivery deduplicates to that head and consumes neither a new source reserve nor another cursor disposition.

Every quarantine custody head owns exactly one monotone `QuarantineReprocessingHead`. Its genesis is `RETRY_OR_HOLD { predecessor = custody_head, reason, next_parser_constraints }`. From an exact current `RETRY_OR_HOLD`, one CAS selects one immutable attempt identity, registered parser ID/version and complete original authentication/correlation bindings; no parser assertion can replace those bindings. The attempt publishes exactly one closed result: `MATERIALIZED { stable_inbox_id = derive(custody_head), exact_inbox_head } | VERSION_INDEPENDENT_TERMINAL_NON_EVIDENCE { closed_byte_level_proof_kind/version, proof_digest } | RETRY_OR_HOLD { typed_failure_or_unknown, next_parser_constraints }`. Parser exception, unavailable dependency, ambiguity, unknown schema or interpretation uncertainty can only yield `RETRY_OR_HOLD`. `MATERIALIZED` and `VERSION_INDEPENDENT_TERMINAL_NON_EVIDENCE` are terminal and never superseded; the latter is legal only for a registered deterministic proof over the immutable raw bytes that is independent of parser interpretation. A newer parser may start only from the exact prior retry/hold head. One journal decision and EventAppender CAS select each successor; byte-identical replay returns it, while changed attempt/version/result, rival parser or second terminal conflicts. The original custody/raw bytes remain immutable, and at most one stable inbox subject can ever arise.

`POLL_CURSOR_ADVANCE_AUTHORIZED` transaction-locally re-enumerates the exact manifest and byte-compares a unique terminal disposition for every ordered member before binding that page manifest and its exact candidate cursor. Only then may the exact `POLL_CURSOR_APPLIED` successor durably apply that cursor under `DURABLE_CURSOR_BEFORE_REQUEST`; changed page/manifest/member/disposition/provider contract/session/cursor conflicts. A crash after any subset of member dispositions resumes the same page join, never advances the cursor, and exact replay returns existing dispositions. A crash after complete authorization but before application may apply only that identical authorized cursor; application still precedes construction or emission of the next request.

A push adapter may emit success only after `LOCAL_ACK_AUTHORIZED`; a polling adapter may apply no cursor before `POLL_CURSOR_ADVANCE_AUTHORIZED` and may emit no request carrying that cursor before durable `POLL_CURSOR_APPLIED`; reconciliation may release no obligation before `RECONCILIATION_RELEASE_AUTHORIZED`. A crash before cursor application therefore emits no request with the new cursor; a crash after application but before emission safely reuses the same durable cursor on restart under the exact transport contract. A crash after local authorization or attempt issuance can otherwise leave remote observation unknown and can cause provider redelivery; identical redelivery deduplicates to the one inbox event and resumes only the transport-specific safe follow-up, never a second material evidence event. No local state claims that a remote peer received a response. If the source offers no redelivery contract and either journal decision or durable materialization is unavailable, the broker enters `DEGRADED` and surfaces possible evidence loss rather than authorizing success. Worker lease generation never controls this evidence admission. Reads use the broker-owned closed `ReadOperation` protocol below, separate raw connections and explicit snapshots; WAL is enabled. A blocking SQLite driver is isolated inside the broker adapter boundary.

That physical single-writer rule is not semantic write authority. The shared commit boundary also owns a closed `RecordOwnershipRegistry`, generated and checked against the closed enums of every concrete event and revision kind. Each kind has exactly one entry derived from the module ownership above: its sole authorized application writer, admissible typed source-command or evidence family, required tenant/principal and authority/current-head bindings, allowed causal-predecessor roles, admissible prior-state→next-state transitions, terminal-state behavior, and permitted atomic batch relations. `EventAppender` validates the entry inside the commit transaction before schema-valid data can append; an unregistered kind, duplicate owner, cross-owner command, inadmissible source family, absent or mismatched binding, forbidden predecessor role, illegal semantic transition, transition from a terminal head, or impossible rival terminal fails closed. A concurrent rival that the governing family requires the owner to resolve remains unresolved rather than being selected by timestamp. Public ports cannot waive this check, and local invariant batches validate every member independently as well as the permitted batch relation. Startup fails if a concrete kind lacks exactly one registry entry. Adding or changing a kind therefore requires an explicit registry change owned by its existing semantic module, not inference from caller identity, adapter route, payload shape, or physical writer ownership.

Version one has one initializing script, not a migration framework. It initializes only a canonical empty database/journal pair at the exact current database-genesis, schema, journal-schema, canonicalization, registry and companion-manifest versions and publishes every required genesis record/commitment. It never opens a historical store for conversion. Startup accepts only that exact tuple; any older, newer, missing or mismatched member is `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION / HOLD_ADOPTION` before replay or authority admission. An operator archives/removes and recreates disposable early data. There is no companion synthesis, adoption command, compatibility range or partial migration.

The migration trigger is the first release or operator/public promise that existing tenant data survives a schema change. Before making that promise, the project must adopt a migration framework and demonstrate verified backup/restore rehearsal, projection rebuild, boundary failure injection, and an explicit recovery contract. Every migration declares `backward-compatible` or `forward-only` before execution. A forward-only migration requires a verified backup, restore rehearsal into a separate compatible new database, an explicit point of no return, and a recorded recovery procedure; rollback means restoring that database and running a compatible application, not reverse SQL. Tooling rejects a migration without its declared mode and recovery contract. Until the trigger, no upgrade path may imply preserved data.

### Interpretation, proposal, and authorization

Natural-language interpretation produces an immutable `InterpretationRevision` and an exact `Proposal`. A proposal binds at least:

- source message and channel identity;
- tenant and authenticated principal;
- prompt artifact, model, tool-set, and policy versions;
- proposed typed command;
- ambiguities, assumptions, and expiry;
- exact authoritative state head used to derive it.

Approval targets one exact proposal version. A stale proposal is rejected, rebuilt against current heads, and shown again. Model output is always an untrusted proposal; it cannot grant authority. This realizes the `VISION.md` path `utterance → interpretation revision → proposed typed state → adoption/rejection → active head` and preserves the rule that generic assent is valid only when exactly one displayed current version is salient.

Planning lifecycle imports the staged `VISION.md` contract rather than defining another reducer. `PlanningCommand` is the sole identity-bearing envelope for create, amend, supersede, retract, and every other operation that allocates or advances Plan. It binds the exact authenticated actor and authority, every targeted current source head, the exact adopted displayed proposal when one exists, and the complete proposed atomic result manifest. The planning committer publishes exactly one `PlanningCommittedResult` for that command or nothing: all successor heads, terminal dispositions, allocations, edges, lineage, and children become visible atomically. A changed or stale source head rejects; concurrent or conflicting heads never merge or win by timestamp and remain unresolved until the governing family conflict rule selects one authorized successor. Replay of the same command identity and byte-identical command/result manifest returns the existing result after a lost acknowledgement; any changed binding conflicts, while a different command identity against an old head is stale rather than replay.

The closed `PlanningAuthorityRegistry` exhaustively maps actor, operation, authority source, proposal binding, adoption proof, and any operation-specific stricter predicate. A direct authenticated principal command may omit proposal adoption only when its exact principal-authored act itself supplies the typed command and binds every affected current head. A system-issued command may omit proposal adoption only under an exact current bounded mandate that explicitly admits that operation, scope, consequence, and result manifest. A command derived from natural language requires the exact displayed `PlanningProposal` and authenticated adoption of that proposal. The evidence-policy assessor has one narrower non-Plan row: under the exact current bound evidence policy it may perform only `ALLOCATE_REPRESENTATION` to create a non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate; it cannot allocate a local obligation discriminator, confirm or promote recognition, create an actual commitment, advance an obligation cursor, or change Plan. Root transformation, recurrence materialization, false-closure reconciliation, and every other specialized operation retain their separately registered stricter predicates and cannot borrow a broader row. A model-selected tool or request is never an authority source. Every unlisted actor × operation × authority × proposal/adoption tuple, every unknown predicate result, and every attempt to substitute one row's evidence for another rejects before `PlanningCommittedResult` publication.

Proposal construction and planning commit validation obtain every authority-sensitive value exclusively through a typed `AuthorityReadRecorder` capability. Its closed operations return a typed value together with canonical source identity, head/version/generation, validity and freshness data, and append that read to an immutable ordered trace. This is the sole provenance path not only for repository lookups but also for cached, projected, precomputed, injected, or derived authority values and every transitive input to an authority predicate; those values cannot enter proposal construction or commit validation through constructors, ordinary repositories, projections, caches, or private adapter access. An unavailable direct/untracked path, an unrecordable source, or an authority predicate whose complete inputs cannot be reproduced fails closed. The selected `PlanningAuthorityRegistry` row supplies required registry inputs and allowed typed read kinds, but does not declare away reads that actually occurred.

Natural-language adoption has one canonical exhaustive `ProposalFreshnessBinding`, not a set of independently optional freshness checks. It binds the exact displayed `PlanningProposal` and display digest; authenticated principal and adoption act; source ingress and `InterpretationRevision`; `PlanningCommand` identity and complete proposed `PlanningCommittedResult` manifest; the deterministic canonical ordering and digest of the actual `AuthorityReadRecorder` trace; and every required registry input. The binding is derived from that trace plus those required inputs, and construction rejects any missing, extra, duplicated, disallowed, unreproducible, or untracked authority-sensitive dependency. The planning committer uses the same capability inside the transaction that would publish `PlanningCommittedResult`, reproduces the required reads and predicates, and atomically compares the resulting whole binding; a changed, missing, conflicting, expired, unknown, or newly discovered value or predicate writes no result, invalidates the displayed proposal and adoption, and requires a proposal rebuilt and redisplayed from current inputs plus a new exact adoption. No subset check, declared-only manifest, prior approval, projection snapshot, receipt, timestamp choice, or semantically similar binding can satisfy or refresh it. Direct-principal, bounded-system, and specialized rows remain governed only by their own complete registered predicates and never inherit this proposal-adoption requirement by implication.

### Model and provider trust boundary

The model receives no application or provider credentials, provider tokens, tenant encryption keys, or direct network access. Its complete input is limited to the minimal conversation context required for the Turn, tenant-scoped `DashboardScreen` projections, the bound prompt artifact, and the schemas of tools currently allowed by policy. Tool implementations retain credentials outside the model boundary and independently enforce tenant, authority, policy, and state-head checks; a model-selected tool call is a request, not authorization.

Structured model output is untrusted until schema, tenant, authority, policy, current-head, and semantic-version validation succeeds. Provider payloads are likewise untrusted evidence: they may advance an evidence-driven effect-attempt state through an admitted adapter path, but they never write Plan, grant authority, or become a planning head. Logs and traces must redact secrets before persistence while retaining stable artifact and correlation identities needed for replay.

### External effects and local atomicity

One local transaction commits the authorized domain revision and its `ExternalActionIntent`/outbox record. Provider execution occurs after commit and is asynchronous and idempotent where the provider makes that guarantee. Provider receipts are appended separately and never become planning authority.

R03's smaller per-event atomicity does not split invariant islands that are independently required to publish together. The domain-revision/intent pair and the indivisible state transitions required by `VISION.md` remain atomic inside their owning node. What is rejected is using one cross-hexagon checkpoint transaction as the default coordinator for all planning, tool, effect, delivery, and Run consequences.

The implementation imports the complete normative dispatch protocol and closed reducer from `VISION.md` as one version-bound conformance dependency; this document neither copies nor defines a competing transition table. The import includes the last-local `SEND_COMMITTED` ordering boundary, same-domain authority/hold/freshness and intent-equality revalidation, separate `TransmissionAttempt` children, safe-retransmission guards, conservative parent reduction, terminal consequences, and the deny semantics of `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and `PARTIAL`. No Stage 2 adapter, channel, provider, calendar path, recovery path, or worker may produce any external effect until the full imported reducer and its ordering/fencing conformance suite pass for that surface. The shorthand lifecycle used in channel projections—`pending`, `delivered`, `retrying`, `reconciliation required`, `failed`—is presentation only and must not collapse those normative states.

Every durable `ExternalActionIntent`, `DispatchAuthorization`, and `TransmissionAttempt` carries one immutable canonical `DispatchSemanticBinding`. The binding names the exact normative dispatch manifest and reducer version, transition-registry version, canonicalization/fingerprint version, and relevant adapter-contract version; its canonical fingerprint is copied unchanged from intent through authorization and transmission. At every action-authorizing transition—initial dispatch, child allocation plus `SEND_COMMITTED`, retransmission, duplicate-risk authorization, and compensation dispatch—the owning transaction atomically requires exact equality between every bound component and the versions supported for that operation. A missing, unavailable, unsupported, or unequal component produces a typed `DISPATCH_VERSION_DENIED` before the transition or `DISPATCH_VERSION_HOLD` for already durable work; partial equality, compatibility inference, or adapter-selected fallback is rejection, not authorization.

Compensation is a fresh `ExternalActionIntent`, never a continuation, mutation, replay, or rival disposition of the original. It has its own stable identity and complete current actor/principal, authority or bounded mandate, affected-party rights and constraints, hold/conflict/ordering, dependency/evidence, consequence and scope, proposal/adoption when required, freshness, endpoint/provider, and `DispatchSemanticBinding`; separately, it references the exact original effect identity and binding plus the exact ambiguity/reconciliation head and consequence it addresses. Creation rejects stale, revoked, expired, changed, conflicting, missing, or unknown authority, mandate, affected-party, hold, dependency, consequence, proposal/adoption, freshness, dispatch, or original-head input. At its last reversible boundary the owning transaction revalidates that complete compensation binding and the referenced original effect's exact current ambiguity/reconciliation head before child allocation plus `SEND_COMMITTED`; late evidence or any changed/resolved original head rejects or holds dispatch and requires a new authorization decision. Compensation never closes, rewrites, weakens, or makes retryable the original `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `PARTIAL` record; only authenticated evidence reduced by the original bound protocol changes that record.

Upgrade recovery is explicit and state-sensitive. Before `SEND_COMMITTED`, a held pending intent may proceed only through a recorded compatible migration that preserves its identity and complete semantics or through a new exact re-authorization against the migrated record. At or after `SEND_COMMITTED`, migration or re-authorization cannot cross the last-local boundary, reset uncertainty, reinterpret the record, allocate a replacement/rival intent, or make an ambiguous outcome retryable: `SENT`, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, and `PARTIAL` remain under their bound semantics and require bound-version reconciliation or the normative separately authorized duplicate-risk or compensation path. Separately authenticated late provider evidence remains admissible despite a dispatcher-version mismatch, but it is reduced only by the record's exact bound reducer/transition registry or held for an explicit compatible reconciliation implementation; evidence ingestion never authorizes transmission. No startup, worker, replay, migration, reconciliation, or adapter path may automatically reinterpret or replay a record under another binding, weaken ambiguity, synthesize compatibility, or allocate a rival intent.

Stable identities exist for ingress items, commands, committed results, proposals, intents, transmissions, runs, turns, and deliveries. Reuse of an identity with the same complete fingerprint returns the original result; reuse with a changed fingerprint is a conflict. Lost acknowledgements and duplicate deliveries are ordinary replay cases. An ambiguous provider outcome opens typed reconciliation and is never blindly retried.

### User-visible consistency

After local commit, queries expose committed local truth immediately and show provider convergence separately. No channel claims provider success before authenticated evidence supports it. Telegram and CLI consume the same query projection and differ only in authentication, transport, and presentation.

Every consequential result preserves the `PLAN`, `FACT CLAIM`, `CANDIDATE EVIDENCE`, and `PROPOSED PLANNING CHANGE` distinctions required by `VISION.md` and states whether planning changed. Aggregate UI states are rendered as scoped language with owner, authority, current/historical status, consequence, blocker, and bounded next actions; raw internal enums are insufficient.

### Ingress and evidence authentication

Telegram ingress is one broker-owned two-phase protocol. In phase one, the broker allocates a stable `TelegramCandidateRef`, retains the exact raw transport record and secrets in broker-private storage, and performs schema-only candidate normalization into `TelegramNormalizedCandidate { candidate_id, canonical_raw_digest, parsed_update_identity, candidate_fields, normalization_version }`. This record is explicitly untrusted: it consumes no replay, creates no witness or channel binding, grants no principal authority, and cannot publish ingress. The ingress owner can receive or return only closed inert candidate/classification references through its public port; raw transport handles, credentials, secrets, live objects, and authority-bearing witnesses never cross.

In phase two, the broker reacquires the exact private raw transport record by candidate reference, byte-compares its digest and normalized fields, and authenticates transport under current broker-owned credentials. The broker-only closed `TransportOriginWitness` has two variants: `TELEGRAM_POLLING` proves an authenticated TLS response obtained by the broker-owned polling adapter under the exact current bot credential/account; `TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK` proves receipt at the exact registered TLS endpoint with the exact current webhook secret token and bot credential binding. Both bind candidate identity, tenant/database/current trust/materialization heads, bot/account, endpoint, broker epoch/transport session, current bot/endpoint credential heads, exact canonical raw update and digest, Telegram update/replay identity, authentication/canonicalization versions, freshness, and witness identity. In one broker-owned journal-decision/materialization transaction, the broker revalidates those fields, constructs the complete Telegram `ChannelAuthenticationBinding`, consumes the exact replay identity, stores the private witness and binding, and publishes exactly one normalized ingress item plus inert `AuthenticatedIngressRef { ingress_id, candidate_id, witness_head_digest, channel_binding_head_digest }`. No intermediate authenticated candidate is visible. Plausible metadata, direct DTO submission, ingress-owner assertion, absent reference, changed bytes, or credential/account/endpoint/epoch substitution cannot qualify; identical replay returns the same reference and changed or rival reuse conflicts.

`ChannelAuthenticationBinding` is a closed versioned union that must exist before an ingress item can commit. Its Telegram variant is broker-constructed inside the phase-two transaction and binds the exact `bot_id`, private `chat_id`, `telegram_user_id`, tenant-registered endpoint identity, immutable tenant/principal/contour heads, current trust/materialization/endpoint/credential-binding heads, candidate and witness identities, canonical raw-update digest, Telegram update identity, freshness window and replay head. Version one admits private chats only. Groups, channels, shared chats, administrators, delegates, bot membership in another chat and unknown update forms have no authority. Forwarded, quoted and replied-to material is untrusted payload text; neither its attributed author nor the replied-to message lends authority. Account deletion, reassignment or replacement requires local authenticated re-enrollment, and endpoint revocation makes every old binding, update and session inadmissible. Application code receives only `AuthenticatedIngressRef`; every direct-principal semantic commit asks the broker to revalidate that reference against the exact current private binding and heads rather than accepting an owner-supplied binding DTO.

The CLI variant binds the local Unix-socket endpoint, authenticated OS peer identity, exact immutable tenant and principal, current contour/credential/session heads, a short-lived authenticated CLI session, canonical command bytes, freshness and replay identity. Socket-path or copied-configuration possession alone grants nothing. Automation and privilege escalation are explicit registered policy variants rather than implicit inheritance from the launching process; session expiry, credential rotation/revocation, OS-peer change or policy-head change requires fresh authentication and authorization. The normalized request carries the complete binding, and every direct-principal authority path revalidates its current heads at the owning commit rather than trusting adapter labels.

Incoming provider, channel and reconciliation material becomes durable evidence only through `EvidenceAuthenticationBinding`, a closed variant selected by the registered source contract. It binds canonical raw bytes and digest, transport and provider identity, exact account/endpoint, signature or polling credential/key identity and version, audience, immutable tenant/database/principal contour where applicable, current provider/endpoint credential heads, freshness, source replay identity, correlation/intent/delivery/obligation subject, and authentication-contract/canonicalization versions. The broker authenticates and consumes this binding before the durable inbox commit that makes the material evidence; syntax, correlation identity or possession of a plausible payload is never source proof. Wrong account, tenant, endpoint, key, audience, freshness, correlation or replay state rejects without acknowledgement or cursor advance. Independently authenticated late evidence remains admissible across worker lease or Run terminal state, but only under its exact current source contract and original subject binding.

An independently generated closed `EvidenceIngressSurfaceManifest` is byte-compared bidirectionally with executable adapter/public-port reachability, record kinds and broker registrations at build, startup and every runtime-generation publication. It has exactly one row for each Telegram webhook push, Telegram polling response, CLI request, provider callback, provider poll, reconciliation observation and tool-result evidence path. Every row binds canonical path/owner and source class; the exact earliest irreversible handoff instruction/adapter cut; `PROVEN_SOURCE_RETENTION` or `PREALLOCATED_LOSS_SLOT_THEN_RAW_STAGE`; stable receipt-token and source identity derivation; authentication fields/current heads and pre-auth no-authority rules; tenant/principal/correlation; raw/page maxima; dedup/order; physical reserve and fairness parameters; custody successors; acknowledgement/redelivery/cursor/release; quarantine/reprocessing; shutdown fence and restart handler. Mutation fixtures execute the actual handoff and compare an independent external transfer log with the durable union of unused tokens, raw-staged tokens, exact successor dispositions and loss obligations. Any transfer without exactly one token, or token with zero/multiple successors after resolution, rejects drain completeness. Unknown, omitted, duplicated, extra, aliased, rerouted or post-initialization substituted path/row/record kind rejects before ingress and at runtime generation replacement. The generated executable-path inventory and separately generated manifest must agree; caller labels or a generic DTO cannot select a row.

`agent-dashboard` screens and their refresh observations are projections of the tenant event journal only and have no evidence-ingress row, adapter capability, custody command or broker route. A dashboard may display external state already obtained through journalled evidence, but cannot turn screen state, refresh results or user-visible projection bytes into evidence. Any external state not already in the tenant journal enters only through a registered tool-result, provider callback/poll or reconciliation adapter row and then follows the ordinary authentication, durable custody, bounded admission, acknowledgement and replay protocol. Bidirectional negative reachability tests require the dashboard/screen evidence-path set to be empty.

### Conversation and channel delivery

Each tenant has one canonical agent conversation spanning Telegram, CLI, and scheduled prompts. Agent-facing context is channel-independent, but human-visible projections remain channel-specific: content originating in CLI is not automatically rendered into Telegram history. Full conversation means durable access, not unbounded prompt inclusion. Every Turn receives a bounded conversation projection containing recent messages, active threads, unresolved items, and provenance-bound summaries; read-only history tools expose search, ranges, and message/thread/Run lookup over originals. Evals verify both availability and required retrieval before consequential action.

For the initial single-principal tenant, channel-specific human visibility is a presentation boundary, not a confidentiality boundary. The agent may use tenant-wide history in any authorized reply channel and does not mirror old messages automatically. Absent an explicit restriction, cross-channel use therefore remains permitted and needs no extra principal approval; this never exempts model-derived bytes from the disclosure mechanism below. Explicit sensitive/disclosure labels remain enforceable restrictions. Stronger per-principal isolation becomes mandatory before any multi-principal or shared-channel capability is enabled; the initial simplification cannot be used as evidence for that capability.

An explicit sensitive/disclosure label may be assigned by the authenticated principal or by an explicitly versioned policy rule. `DisclosureLabel` is one closed versioned join-semilattice with a canonical `join` and endpoint-admissibility table. Its explicit bottom value means “unrestricted under this exact current policy”; an absent label/envelope, unknown value/version, incomparable member for which the registered join is undefined, or unavailable head is not bottom and fails closed. Canonical set ordering, duplicate elimination followed by byte-equality, and tenant/source identity prevent order, alias or duplicate-dependent joins.

The runtime owns one monotone durable `TurnVisibilityAccumulator` per Turn and exactly one durable `ModelCallAttemptLineage` per `Turn/call_slot`. The lineage contains immutable `ModelCallAttempt` generations, a monotone `retry_generation`, and one selector that permits exactly one generation to be emittable or acceptable at a time. Before each generation, the runtime derives its immutable `ModelVisibilityManifest` from the complete ordered set of content-bearing bytes actually exposed by conversation originals and summaries, prompt/policy/instruction/tool-schema material, retained screens and attention tail, history/search/index/tool reads, prior tool results, prior accepted or rejected trace-derived content, and every other registered context edge. Each member binds exact record/revision/head, canonical visible bytes or digest, provenance-envelope head, disclosure-label head, producer/surface row and schema/canonicalization version; the manifest binds tenant/principal/contour, Run/Turn/call slot/attempt generation, prompt/context/tool-schema heads, complete membership and canonical joined label. The accumulator may only add newly exposed members between calls. A tool result obtained after call N affects call N+1, never retroactively relabels call N, and no caller or model may remove a member or declare a smaller dependency set.

Model request emission uses one broker/EventAppender journal-decision protocol. Its first CAS publishes `PREPARED_NOT_EMITTED` with the exact accumulator seal/head, immutable visibility manifest, canonical request bytes/digest, provider adapter/contract and exact authenticated provider-recipient tuple, current worker owner/generation/session, applicable Run/current physical-root/work epoch/lease fence, stable lineage/call-slot/attempt identity and retry generation. The next durable CAS to `EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` must commit before the first request byte can become externally observable; only then may the broker-owned adapter emit those exact bytes. Consequently restart may treat a still-current `PREPARED_NOT_EMITTED` as proof of non-exposure, while every `EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` is a crossed no-retry boundary even if no response or local send acknowledgement exists. Worker-local preparation, socket state or an unenforced “last reversible” callback cannot substitute for either journal decision.

The attempt-generation lifecycle is the closed union `PREPARED_NOT_EMITTED | EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | RESPONSE_CAPTURED | TERMINAL_REJECTED | TERMINAL_ACCEPTED`. Only the selected exact generation may advance. `RESPONSE_CAPTURED` durably binds provider response identity, exact raw bytes/digest, receipt/session evidence and the predecessor emission/manifest heads before parsing; parse errors, malformed or schema/semantic rejects, accepted responses, `RunTrace`, summaries, projections, replay and eval records join that same exact attempt and visibility manifest. One CAS selects `TERMINAL_REJECTED` or `TERMINAL_ACCEPTED`; changed/rival response, predecessor, manifest or terminal conflicts. A late response is admissible only against the still-exact emitted generation and never against a reconstructed current projection.

Possible emission forbids hidden retry. A replacement generation is legal only when a registered provider-specific proof establishes no external model exposure for the exact selected attempt under the existing retry policy. It remains in the same `ModelCallAttemptLineage`, increments `retry_generation`, CAS-advances the one selector and preserves the superseded attempt immutably; it cannot allocate a fresh call slot or Turn to bypass the old selector. Old and resumed workers compete on the same selector with current owner/session/lease fences, so at most one generation can emit or accept. Absent that exact proof, `EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` remains terminally non-retryable pending late response/reconciliation or explicit recovery disposition.

Every byte produced by one model call—including `NonAuthoritativeText`, `DeliveryAssertion` fields, tool names/arguments, accepted and rejected response bytes, parse/validation errors retaining content, and their `RunTrace` records—inherits that call manifest and its whole joined label regardless of citations, copying, paraphrase or model-declared references. Every derived summary, screen, cache/index/search row, conversation record, later context member, rendering and eval input/output carries an immutable `DisclosureProvenanceEnvelope` that names its exact complete source manifest and label heads; budget truncation may remove content but never the envelope needed to authorize retained content. The model cannot remove or weaken a label.

An independently generated versioned `DisclosureSurfaceManifest` is byte-compared bidirectionally with executable content-bearing reachability, record kinds, serializers and broker registrations at build, startup and every runtime-generation publication. It has exactly one row for every ingress/event/tool request and result, model input/output and rejected trace, conversation/original/summary/history/search/index, screen/cache/context assembler, delivery/render/send, replay/rebuild and eval path. Each row fixes owner, provenance producer, label join/envelope, allowed reader/endpoint class, narrowing/deletion invalidators, replay/rebuild behavior and enforcement cut. Unknown, omitted, extra, duplicate, aliased, dynamically substituted or unenveloped paths and records reject before model exposure, publication or delivery.

Narrowing uses one closed `DisclosureNarrowingDecision` CAS, separately authenticated to the principal's exact authority. It binds the immutable target content revision/bytes, its complete provenance manifest and every exact current source/predecessor label and prior-narrowing head, lattice and policy heads/versions, requested target label, endpoint scope, purpose, expiry, rationale, actor, contour and stable command fingerprint. The transaction revalidates all heads and publishes a new explicitly bound successor revision/narrowing head only when the target is permitted by the lattice/policy; stale, missing, added, rival or changed provenance writes nothing. Narrowing is non-transitive and prospective: existing traces, summaries, caches, indexes, conversation records, renderings, deliveries and other descendants retain their original restriction unless each receives its own independently authorized exact successor revision. Delivery policy fails closed when any required envelope or label is absent, unknown, unavailable, expired or disallowed at the selected endpoint.

Conversation and execution trace remain semantically distinct. `ConversationHistory` contains user messages, scheduled prompts, and accepted completions/deliveries; `RunTrace` contains Turns, `Continue` responses, tool calls/results, retries, obligations, and failures. Stable message/Run/Turn identities cross-link the projections.

`Complete` carries typed `deliveries[]`, each with its own payload and one closed immutable `DeliveryEndpointSelection`: `ORIGIN_EXACT { ingress_binding_head, endpoint_head, provider_recipient_tuple, canonical_address_bytes, credential_binding_head } | MODEL_SELECTED_EXACT { endpoint_head, provider_recipient_tuple, canonical_address_bytes, credential_binding_head }`. Input omission is only shorthand: validation deterministically replaces it with the exact `ORIGIN_EXACT` bytes and heads captured from authenticated ingress before canonical `DeliveryManifest` construction or durable acceptance; scheduler origin uses its separately registered exact delivery binding. Durable state therefore contains exactly one explicit variant, never omission/neither/both. Unknown variants, stale heads, logical-name lookup, alias substitution or fallback reject. A canonical complete ordered `DeliveryManifest` binds every selection, payload/rendered-byte digest, `ModelVisibilityManifest`, provenance/label/narrowing heads and policy/version; duplicate or rival entries resolving to one exact provider-recipient tuple reject rather than send twice. One exact origin delivery remains the default and the model may select another registered endpoint without extra approval, but selection never grants authority and its exact bytes remain unchanged through acceptance and send.

Every channel delivery uses the existing external-outcome lifecycle with a delivery-specific `SEND_COMMITTED` as its exact last local reversible boundary. In one broker-owned total order/critical section shared with every tenant/principal/contour, endpoint ownership/address/credential/binding, disclosure-policy, source-label/narrowing and delivery invalidator, the transition revalidates the accepted payload/render digest, complete delivery/visibility/provenance manifests, exact unchanged provider-recipient tuple and all exact current heads. Only the winning `SEND_COMMITTED` disposition may submit those immutable bytes to that tuple. If an invalidator wins first, the intent enters typed hold and requires a fresh authorization/intent under the same product rules; it may not resolve an alias or replacement endpoint. If send commitment wins first, later local invalidation is later and non-retroactive, and timeout, crash or lost reply retains the existing immutable attempt/`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`/reconciliation semantics. Replay is disposition-only and never re-enqueues; any separately authorized retry binds a new attempt identity but the same exact authorized tuple and current-head proof.

The version-one delivery guarantee is exactly authenticated provider-recipient tuple equality and local routing integrity through that cut; it does not claim that a provider cannot later reassign an address/account, route incorrectly, or be compromised, nor that the eventual human is cryptographically identical to the principal. Such provider-side behavior is external outcome/threat evidence and enters the registered receipt/reconciliation protocol without retroactively converting local submission into a confidentiality proof or definite delivery. A local endpoint/address/credential/binding change remains an ordinary invalidator and suppresses `SEND_COMMITTED` when it wins first.

Each delivery payload is an ordered closed union of `NonAuthoritativeText { text }` and `DeliveryAssertion { assertion_kind, subject_ref, source_record_refs, source_frontier, candidate_disposition_head?, assertion_codes, renderer_version }`. Free model prose is permitted only as explicitly non-authoritative commentary: the deterministic renderer encloses every such segment in an immutable, prominent `UNVERIFIED MODEL COMMENTARY` presentation and never gives it the visual or linguistic form reserved for an asserted Plan state, Fact claim, candidate status, provider outcome, or other consequential result. This does not depend on heuristically recognizing whether the prose sounds consequential. A consequential statement must instead be a `DeliveryAssertion`: its closed `assertion_kind` selects an evidence validator and deterministic renderer. Plan and Fact assertions require the exact admissible authoritative head. A candidate assertion binds the immutable candidate identity and version, the exact unique current candidate `CandidateDisposition | CandidateStatus` head at the accepted source frontier, and one or more closed `assertion_codes`; every code must be entailed by that exact head's registered semantic payload and terminal/nonterminal classification. Candidate attribution is rendered from those records, never from model wording. A stale, superseded, disputed, conflicted, non-unique, absent, or terminally mismatched candidate head, a code not entailed by the head, or a current-head change before durable acceptance rejects the whole completion. Provider success requires an authenticated durable provider receipt or completed reconciliation whose typed outcome entails every supplied assertion code. The validator also rejects unknown assertion kinds or codes, absent/inadmissible evidence, tenant/subject/correlation mismatch, stale or conflicting Plan/Fact heads, a source frontier older than a required current head, and any model-supplied authoritative wording. Only after validation does the renderer derive the authoritative label and text from the typed source record and bound template version. Rejection aborts that `Complete`; neither its deliveries nor its prose enter `ConversationHistory`, and a later Turn receives a typed reason. This validation governs durable acceptance as well as channel dispatch, so a schema-valid trace or free-text completion cannot masquerade as Plan, Fact, candidate, or provider truth.

### Composition, concurrency, and types

Dishka is the composition framework. Scope mapping is `APP` = process/tenant, `REQUEST` = one Run activation from ingress/tick/resume, `ACTION` = one Turn or event-handler invocation, and optional `STEP` = one model/tool/provider call requiring separate cleanup. `SESSION` is not used initially. A durable Run may span many containers; every resume opens a new request scope from the event graph.

Only the canonical assembly creates containers; only manifest-declared runtime boundary adapters may open their declared scopes. Domain/application services use constructor injection and do not import Dishka. Provider functions contain wiring, not business logic. `promptstrings.DishkaContext` is the sole controlled container-handle boundary, is owned by its exact declared integration adapter, and cannot cross a public port; dashboard builders receive typed query ports.

After assembly and before work admission, a closed `RuntimeCapabilityGraphAttestation` walks the realized Dishka graph and the objects it produces under bounded probe scopes. Its canonical nodes and edges cover provider identity/version, declared port, concrete object owner, scope/lifetime, constructor dependencies, returned objects, callback targets, factory products, child containers/scopes, registry/service-locator handles, and declared transitive capabilities. It byte-compares this graph with the `HexagonBoundaryManifest` and provider manifest. Unknown or opaque providers, objects, factories, callbacks, child containers, handles, edges, scopes, or capabilities reject startup. In particular it rejects adapter-to-adapter, adapter-to-foreign-domain, domain/application-to-adapter, public-port-to-foreign-persistence/provider, unapproved child-container, and container/service-locator escape edges even when lexical imports are legal.

Persistent enforcement is governed by one closed, immutable, versioned `RuntimeGraphGeneration` per capability-owner compartment and one tenant-scoped `AuthorityBroker` as the non-bypassable trusted computing base. An owner is exactly one authority-bearing hexagon or one plugin; plugins always have distinct owner compartments. Each generation manifest binds immutable `owner_id` and covers only that owner's provider definitions, realized inert object descriptions and target identities, dependency edges, registries/callbacks, factories and admissible behavior partitions, scope/child-container templates, exact capability set, and boundary/provider/runtime-graph fingerprints. Two mutually distrusted owners never share an address space, proxy membrane, IPC peer/session, or generation. The broker exclusively owns production credentials, the SQLite writer/raw connections, provider SDKs, sockets, native handles, channel transports, clock/lease authority, and every adapter handle capable of an irreversible external, storage, or event commit. Owner workers, plugins, provider/factory-inspection, and quarantine processes receive none of those objects or ambient equivalents.

Every owner generation candidate starts in a new OS-isolated process under a sanitized allowlist environment: no production secrets or inherited descriptors, closed-by-default descriptor inheritance, no production filesystem mounts, no network namespace/route, no subprocess authority except an explicitly empty or allowlisted bootstrap, no broker/provider modules carrying live globals, and one owner-exclusive authenticated local IPC endpoint whose message schema and operation set are generation-manifested. The canonical bootstrap derives the complete owner partition from `HexagonBoundaryManifest`, creates one compartment launch manifest per owner, proves its isolation profile and exact executable/image, `owner_id`, generation manifest, capability set, environment, descriptor table, mount/network policy, IPC peer identity, and code fingerprints, and only then admits that owner. Unknown, duplicate, missing, merged, or split owners and any shared peer/session fail startup. A failed or unproved control prevents generation publication. This process boundary, rather than Python cooperation, makes module/class/global/container/closure mutation, descriptors, native state, foreign threads, cached bound methods, coroutine/Future captures, and callback tables unable to acquire another owner's proxy or raw production authority.

Before trust, providers, plugins, callbacks, and factories are accepted only as inert canonical declarative manifests in a closed data language. Inspection parses canonical bytes with non-executing trusted decoders and may read only allowlisted plain fields; it never imports candidate modules or executes constructors, descriptors, `__getattribute__`, metaclass/import hooks, iterators, hashes/equality, annotations/signatures, lazy initializers, extension getters, or arbitrary Python/native accessors. Unknown executable metadata, arbitrary Python/native constructors, behaviorally opaque code, or a declaration whose closure cannot be established is rejected without execution. Candidate executable code may run only after its inert declaration and isolation profile are accepted, still inside the authority-empty worker; invocation-dependent construction remains confined there and cannot make a production commit.

Quarantine may export only canonical inert data matching a closed allowlist: manifests, fingerprints, schemas, frozen Pydantic DTO values, and serialized code/image identities. The broker decodes these through trusted parsers, rejects object-capability/pickle/foreign-object encodings and unknown types, re-canonicalizes and re-attests every byte and declared dependency, and never imports or retains a callback, thread/task, descriptor, lazy object, bound method, closure, file descriptor, socket, shared-memory pointer, native handle, extension object, or executable object from quarantine. Published generations therefore publish an attested process/image plus inert graph contract, not a Python object graph crossing into the broker.

Agent/domain/application code sees only typed owner-local proxy Protocols. A proxy can form a canonical broker request but holds neither a raw repository, connection, SDK, socket, native handle, callback nor a reusable bearer credential. The launcher injects an unforgeable owner- and generation-bound IPC session endpoint into that owner's trusted proxy membrane. Every token, read, write, commit, issue record, response slot and result DTO binds immutable `owner_id` together with tenant, broker epoch, authenticated process/peer/session, generation, target, capability, operation and canonical payload fingerprint, plus applicable nonce, expiry, verified-snapshot identity and replay mode. The token and broker-private verified-snapshot capability are never returned to application/plugin code, cannot be exported to another owner/session or used for another operation, and convey no service-location or arbitrary-call authority. Native or async code retained by one owner can at most submit through that owner's membrane; owner substitution fails before execution and response emission.

Cross-hexagon invocation is never a local proxy handoff. It is a closed `PublicPortCall` DTO carrying caller `owner_id`, callee `owner_id`, exact public-port/operation identity, bounded strict Pydantic payload, caller session/generation/epoch, correlation/idempotency identity and canonical fingerprint. The broker authenticates the caller owner, verifies the exact `caller_owner_id → callee_owner_id/public_port/operation` edge and DTO closure in `HexagonBoundaryManifest`, records/routes the call to the callee owner's distinct current session/generation, and returns only a closed `PublicPortResult | PublicPortFailure` DTO through the same correlation. It never returns the callee's proxy, session, token, callback, container handle, live object or raw capability to the caller. Callbacks are modeled as new reverse public-port calls whose reverse edge and DTO are independently manifested; a callee cannot act as a confused deputy for an undeclared caller capability.

Every broker start atomically allocates and durably publishes a fresh monotonically unique `BrokerEpoch`, new authenticated IPC endpoint/key identity, and initial `RECONCILING` state before accepting a connection. A process crash never resumes an epoch. Every prior worker, proxy session, token, nonce, cached read result and serialized request is bound to the old epoch and is invalid; all old workers must be observed terminated before the new epoch can enter `ADMITTING`. Reconnection cannot preserve peer/session identity. Before admission, the restarted broker reads the durable issued-operation registry and consumed nonce/replay ledger for every prior epoch and reconciles each exact canonical command identity to `NOT_ISSUED | ISSUED_OR_COMMITTED | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN_WITH_OBLIGATION | DEFINITE_TERMINAL`. It resumes observation or reconciliation of an issued subject but never invokes its raw operation a second time. A changed identity is a different unauthorized command, not replay; an identical pre-issue request from an obsolete epoch is denied and must be freshly authorized under the new epoch.

Capability token replay mode is a closed tagged union: `ONE_SHOT | IDEMPOTENT_EXACT`. Both bind `BrokerEpoch`, peer/session, generation, tenant, target/capability, canonical operation and bounded payload fingerprint, nonce and expiry. For `ONE_SHOT`, durable nonce consumption and exact issued-operation publication are one broker linearization transaction before raw execution; every later byte-identical or changed use denies and lookup returns only the already published disposition. For `IDEMPOTENT_EXACT`, the same transaction publishes the stable command identity and canonical response/disposition slot; byte-identical replay returns that slot or its typed pending/unknown state without raw re-execution, while any changed field conflicts. A crash before that transaction leaves no issued authority and the obsolete epoch cannot execute it; a crash after it but before, during or after the raw call remains the same issued subject and is reduced to a definite result or durable unknown/recovery obligation before new admission. Token validation without this durable publication is not operation admission.

Reads use one closed `ReadOperation` discriminated union whose variant fixes an exact public query identity, bounded canonical payload and wildcard/range limits, required capability and manifested target, tenant, authenticated peer/session, `RuntimeGraphGeneration`, `BrokerEpoch`, and a broker-issued stable read-request/attempt identity. Every authority-bearing variant must name one broker-private immutable `VerifiedAuthorityReadSnapshot { snapshot_id, database_instance_id, sqlite_snapshot_frontier, current_trust_transition_head, journal_head, materialization_commitment, AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest identity/version/read_graph_version, AMR registry/version/fingerprint, storage_mutation_generation, file/WAL observation, verifier_version }`. To issue it, the broker opens one explicit SQLite read transaction, captures its immutable snapshot/frontier and exact current protected heads, independently enumerates all raw authority-bearing base content inside that same transaction, recomputes the full AMR/materialization commitment, and byte-compares it with the bound exact current journal commitment. It also authenticates the manifest/schema/read-graph/runtime fingerprints. A snapshot ID, database-reported head, partial query result, cache value, or prior verification is never sufficient. Failure, unavailable bytes, live file/WAL mutation during verification, or any unverifiable observation yields no verified snapshot and holds the read.

At raw execution the trusted broker adapter prepares the exact query against that same still-open snapshot under a deny-by-default authorizer and emits `PreparedReadEdgeAttestation { read_attempt_id, query_variant, canonical_prepared_query_and_parameters_fingerprint, actual_operation_to_table_column_edges, manifest_head/read_graph_version, snapshot_id }`. The broker requires the prepared query fingerprint and complete actual edge set to equal the variant's exact closed `AuthorityStorageSurfaceManifest` declaration before stepping the statement; an added, missing, substituted, aliased, dynamically selected, or unobservable edge denies. The result is read only from that snapshot. Immediately before DTO emission the broker revalidates the still-open snapshot capability, exact current trust/journal/materialization/manifest/AMR heads, storage-mutation generation and authenticated file/WAL observation against the values proved at issuance. Any intervening journal decision, database/WAL change, query/edge change, closed or unverifiable snapshot, commitment mismatch, or unverifiable live-storage observation publishes `STALE_OR_INDETERMINATE_READ` and suppresses the result. Broker output is only a frozen strict canonical Pydantic DTO from the query variant's closed result union, fingerprinted over the complete request/attempt context, verified-snapshot proof, edge attestation and canonical result bytes. Cursors, lazy iterators, generators, ORM entities, repository/cache handles, callbacks, Futures, descriptors, executable objects and any value with deferred broker access cannot cross IPC.

Verified snapshots and caches are broker-private performance artifacts, never authority sources. Multiple reads may reuse a snapshot only while every bound protected head, commitment, manifest/read-graph version, storage-mutation generation and file/WAL observation remains exact; each read still receives a distinct attempt identity and execution-edge attestation. Cache keys include the complete request context, verified-snapshot identity/proof, edge attestation and result schema/version, and a hit undergoes the same pre-emission revalidation. Any broker write, observed external byte/WAL change, ambiguous observation, snapshot closure or current-head change invalidates the snapshot and every dependent cache entry before authority-bearing delivery.

The broker generates a closed `AuthorityReadInvalidatorRegistry` from the same admitted manifests. It exhaustively names every state whose change can invalidate authority-bearing release: journal decision and materialization heads, trust transition, credential/session, principal contour, endpoint/channel binding, storage mutation generation and authenticated file/WAL observation, owner generation/drain, and `BrokerEpoch`/restart. An unregistered invalidator source or one that cannot enter the broker-exclusive release boundary prevents startup. Each accepted attempt has one broker-private durable `AuthorityReadReleaseHead`: `PENDING → RELEASED { release_id, read_attempt_id, result_digest, exact_recipient_owner_peer_session_epoch, verified_snapshot_and_edge_proof, invalidator_heads_and_generations } | STALE_OR_INDETERMINATE_READ`. Every registered invalidator and this transition execute through one broker-owned non-awaiting critical section and therefore share one total order.

Completeness is not self-certified by that registry. An independently generated, operator-authenticated, closed `AuthorityReadInvalidationSurfaceManifest` enumerates every executable release-validity predicate and current-state read, every code/runtime mutation path capable of changing each value, the exact state identity/version, and a bidirectional mapping of each predicate/read and mutation path to exactly one `AuthorityReadInvalidatorRegistry` row plus its sole common-order owner. Its generator starts from executable read/validation and mutation reachability rather than registry declarations; build, startup and every runtime-graph generation compare its canonical bytes bidirectionally with the separately generated registry and realized broker graph. Unknown, omitted, duplicated, extra, aliased, unowned or non-orderable predicates, reads, mutation paths, rows or owners reject before admission or generation publication. Runtime mutation through any path lacking the exact manifested row and owner is impossible to authorize and invalidates the generation before a release decision.

The release transition re-enumerates the complete invalidator registry and byte-compares every current head, generation, storage observation and exact recipient binding while holding that section. Its linearization cut is the broker-local atomic publication of immutable `RELEASED` together with the canonical DTO bytes into the authenticated nonblocking response slot/queue for that exact bound peer/session; no authority-bearing byte can cross IPC from any other state. If an invalidator wins first, the broker publishes `STALE_OR_INDETERMINATE_READ` and enqueues no DTO. If release wins first, the later invalidation is later in the same order and cannot retroactively revoke bytes already released. An enqueue/publication failure leaves no `RELEASED`, is nondelivery, suppresses the result and requires the ordinary fresh read path; the broker never converts it to success or retries the enqueue.

An accepted read is a broker-issued drain subject governed by that release head. Cache hits, `ADMITTING → DRAINING`, credential/session or contour revocation, endpoint replacement, owner generation change and broker restart all use the same release order. Session revocation and worker termination wait for a terminal release disposition, though no read uncertainty creates an external-effect recovery obligation or permits stale delivery. A `RELEASED` replay or lost-reply lookup returns only the immutable disposition/digest and never re-enqueues bytes; restart invalidates every unfinished old-epoch delivery and never delivers an old response slot into a replacement session. A retry after stale, indeterminate, enqueue failure, crash or lost reply is permitted only by the existing closed finite read semantics with a fresh request/attempt identity, freshly issued fully verified snapshot and new edge attestation; the old snapshot/result/cache/slot can never be rebound or emitted. Tenant, commitment, live file/WAL, snapshot, query edge, recipient, result or cache substitution fails before release.

Every irreversible provider/channel operation and every SQLite/event append linearizes inside the broker. In one broker-owned admission/commit critical section, the broker verifies the exact currently admitted generation, authenticated IPC peer/session, token and nonce state, declared capability and target fingerprint, canonical operation/payload binding, tenant and relevant expiry; it then invokes the sole raw handle or submits the semantic write to the broker-owned `EventAppender`. Storage/event writes additionally pass the existing `RecordOwnershipRegistry`, authority/current-head and batch predicates in the same SQLite transaction. The generation check is therefore inseparable from the actual local storage/event commit. For a remote/native operation whose external linearization cannot share that transaction, the broker first persists the exact issued-operation identity and dispatch semantic binding under the existing effect protocol, owns the raw call, and persists its definite result or typed `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`/recovery obligation; the worker never receives the raw operation handle. A pre-check followed by a worker-side Python/native call is not a valid authority use.

Generation replacement is an owner-scoped broker state transition. The broker atomically changes only the old `(owner_id, generation, session)` from `ADMITTING` to `DRAINING`, so it accepts no new direct or routed operation/token issuance for it, while already broker-accepted direct operations and `PublicPortCall`s remain exact durable issued subjects. Each in-flight subject either reaches its broker-observed definite terminal result within the versioned drain bound or the broker persists its typed unavailable/indeterminate result and, for consequential work, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` plus the exact recovery obligation required by its family; timeout, process loss, cancellation, or native uncertainty never becomes silent success, cancellation, or retry. Callers receive a typed `OWNER_DRAINING | OWNER_UNAVAILABLE | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` result rather than fallback routing to another owner or generation. Only after all issued subjects have a definite result or durable uncertainty/recovery state may the broker revoke that owner's old IPC session and terminate its process. Late authenticated evidence continues through the independent evidence lane. The replacement owner process is admitted only after complete manifest/isolation/capability attestation and atomic broker publication of its new current generation; unrelated owners continue under their unchanged sessions. Broker restart still rotates every owner session, terminates every old compartment, and reconciles all owner-partitioned issued/replay state before admission.

Behaviorally opaque factories and providers remain forbidden. A directly invocable factory is admissible only when its inert declaration denotes pure deterministic worker-local construction over a closed canonically serialized input/state partition whose branches, product data, requested broker operations, and capability envelope are exhaustive. Otherwise each invocation occurs in a fresh or reset authority-empty isolated worker/quarantine and can export only re-attested inert data; it cannot publish a live product graph or perform a side effect. Monkeypatching or mutation within a worker may corrupt that worker's computation but cannot enlarge its broker capabilities; mismatch with the attested image/manifest causes denial or process termination, and every consequential commit still depends on the exact current generation and operation-bound broker grant.

Production and eval bind the same canonical assembly, `BootstrapManifest`, provider-manifest version, owner-compartment partition, owner-to-public-port routing graph, per-owner capability sets, scopes and ownership graph. An eval may replace only explicitly registered leaf adapter implementation identities inside the same owner compartment. For each replacement, realized-graph comparison requires the same provided port, allowed dependency/capability closure, scope and owner boundary as the corresponding production leaf; only the concrete leaf identity may differ. A fake cannot receive or return a private runner/service, repository/event store, raw connection, provider SDK, container/child-container handle, generic callback, cross-adapter reference, or extra orchestration capability, and cannot merge, split or move owners. Production-private capabilities or extra handles in eval, an undeclared replacement, owner-partition drift, or any unknown graph difference fails closed. Both production and eval evidence record the bootstrap, boundary, owner partition/routing, provider and realized-capability-graph fingerprints.

The `AuthorityBroker` is platform TCB, not a replaceable leaf. Production and eval run the same broker implementation, version, durable epoch/replay/read protocols, configuration schema and security profile. Eval may substitute a registered edge adapter behind the broker only when graph attestation proves identical broker-side capability, request/result schema, tenant/session/generation/epoch/snapshot checks, limits, cache isolation and failure dispositions. An in-memory/eval-only broker, relaxed IPC, skipped epoch reconciliation, transferable cache, extra read query or weaker token mode fails equivalence.

The process has one asyncio event loop. I/O and application ports are async; pure domain reducers stay synchronous. All concurrency is structured through `TaskGroup`: no orphan tasks, cancellation is durably recorded, tool fan-out is policy-controlled, blocking SDKs cross explicit thread boundaries, and resources have bounded concurrency. The injected clock and concurrency contract are identical in production and eval.

Pydantic is standard for models and DTOs. Authoritative models are frozen and strict, reject extra fields, use discriminated closed unions, and have versioned canonical serialization; provider boundaries use provider-specific models. Protocol is the default abstraction mechanism, ABC requires explicit justification, cross-hexagon dictionaries are typed models, all code is type-annotated, and strict mypy is a gate.

Each Turn has one controlled dynamic-model boundary. From the ordered, versioned `ToolSpec` set, a schema factory constructs `Continue[union of exact ToolCall variants] | Complete[deliveries of NonAuthoritativeText | DeliveryAssertion]`. It canonicalizes the JSON Schema and binds its digest, generator version, component identities, assertion registry and renderer versions, and prompt artifact to the Turn. Immediately before each call, the broker/EventAppender seals the selected attempt generation's complete `ModelVisibilityManifest` from the durable monotone `TurnVisibilityAccumulator` inside `PREPARED_NOT_EMITTED`, then journal-commits `EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` before any request byte becomes externally observable; prompt emission without both exact heads is impossible. Validation uses only that Turn's class; equal spec sets may share a cache. Schema validation begins only from `RESPONSE_CAPTURED` and establishes received model-response trace evidence whose complete raw bytes and every parsed/rejected derivative already carry the immutable attempt/visibility/provenance envelope and joined disclosure label. Semantic acceptance separately validates the selected attempt generation, assertion evidence and entailment, current heads/frontier, authority and policy, endpoint/routing/disclosure, explicit normalized delivery selection, complete delivery/visibility manifests, and deterministic rendering for every delivery. Persistence stores only the semantically accepted payload and derived rendering in `ConversationHistory`; any rejected response becomes the exact `TERMINAL_REJECTED` immutable equally labeled `RunTrace` evidence and never an accepted completion. Property tests cover reconstruction by the versioned generator.

### Agent workspace and dashboard families

Turn context is assembled from fixed policy/tool/schema material, mandatory `core.conversation`, an optional bounded attention strip, up to three retained domain families, and compact Run status. Conversation is outside the LRU and may vary only in budget. The three-family LRU stores distinct `dashboard_id`s, evicts the least recently used prompt projection on a fourth family, and renders retained families least-recently-used to most-recently-used so focus is last. Eviction does not remove navigation state or authority.

Initial families are `core.planning`, `core.calendar`, and `core.journal`. `attention` is a conditional cross-cutting highlight/navigation projection, omitted when empty; it owns no canonical item or LRU slot. Run status is compact cross-cutting trace/uncertainty context. A future `ops.runs` family is admitted only after sustained multi-Run operational navigation passes the family admission test. Activity labels are task framing, not family IDs.

The complete family contract is:

| Concern | Contract |

| Identity | `dashboard_id` is a stable namespaced family slug and LRU key; `screen_id` is a stable view key; typed parameters hold tenant-independent object/range identity. |
| Location/snapshot | `ScreenLocation` is typed navigation state. Persisted `ScreenSnapshotRef` binds tenant, location, builder/version, source cursor or frontier, clock, policy/config/budget versions, and content hash. |

`core.journal` is a disposable deterministic workspace over fact authority, not an authority itself. The fact subsystem owns `FactClaim`, `ClaimDisposition`, `WorkOccurrence`, provenance, corrections/retractions, and epistemic reduction. Planning and calendar may render labeled read-only fact projections and typed links, but every mutation routes to its owning authority. The only path from fact to Plan is `FactClaim → proposed planning command → validation/authorization → new Plan revision`; neither a claim nor an occurrence directly changes Plan.

The first journal slice shows owner-authored claims and provider/calendar observations as candidate evidence. It ships `record_fact`, `correct_claim`, `retract_claim`, and `confirm_candidate`, plus provenance/lineage/linked-subject reads; replacement and complex correlation/merge wait. Statuses are source-bounded: `user reported`, `provider observed`, `user confirmed provider observation`, `disputed`, `corrected`, `retracted`, and `unknown`. Conflicts preserve both attributed records, never use latest-write-wins, and require direct principal action to change a personal claim.

`record_fact` accepts exactly one of two authority envelopes, never a bare model-produced `FactClaim`: `DirectFactAssertion` or `ConfirmedFactAssertion`. `DirectFactAssertion` binds one authenticated ingress item and version, its exact utterance bytes and digest, tenant and authenticated principal, the target FactClaim schema/version, one complete canonical typed claim (including exact tagged subject, canonical payload, and principal-utterance provenance), canonicalization version, and a stable assertion identity plus complete fingerprint. A deterministic closed-grammar eligibility check—not a model interpretation—must positively prove every conjunct: the principal is the authenticated speaker and endorser of the proposition; the complete principal-authored span is one declarative affirmative assertion speech act; its proposition is asserted in the actual modality rather than asked, requested, commanded, wished, doubted, conditionally or hypothetically entertained, quoted, merely mentioned, attributed or reported as another speaker's claim, corrected/retracted, or negated; and that same span supplies exactly one canonical subject, payload, and principal-utterance provenance without ambiguity, alternatives, omitted or inferred fields, model-added content, split or merge, correlation, or consequential interpretation. The parser returns a closed per-predicate proof vector, and any false, unknown, unrecognized, or multiply applicable speech-act/polarity/modality/quotation/attribution/endorsement result makes the assertion ineligible. Same identity and byte-identical binding replays the original result; any changed binding conflicts.

If that proof fails for any reason, direct append is unavailable. The interpretation service may only create an immutable `FactAssertionConfirmationDisplay` binding the authenticated ingress, exact utterance, every proposed canonical field and provenance edge, consequence text, target schema/version, interpretation/policy/current heads, and a digest of the complete display. `ConfirmedFactAssertion` repeats that exact binding with an authenticated principal confirmation and stable confirmation identity; commit revalidates the digest and every bound head before append. Every ambiguous, inferred, incomplete, multi-claim/split, merged, correlated, or consequential case therefore requires exact displayed confirmation; neither generic assent, a model-generated typed command, nor an otherwise legal `record_fact` caller can substitute for it.

Ordinary fact mutation imports the closed `VISION.md` reducer. Each immutable initial `FactClaim` roots exactly one canonical claim family, and every `ClaimDisposition` targets that family's unique current `FactClaim | ClaimDisposition` head with exactly one owner-authorized operation: `CORRECT`, `RETRACT`, or `REPLACE`. `CORRECT` preserves the canonical family key and exact tagged `ClaimSubject`; `RETRACT` leaves no current positive assertion; `REPLACE` retires the prior family from the current positive projection, allocates the newly identified successor claim in a fresh family, and records exact lineage. A successful disposition is the sole current head. A stale target rejects, while concurrent candidates remain an explicit unresolved conflict until the principal selects one exact successor, never by timestamp. The first slice may defer the `REPLACE` command surface, but it must neither emulate replacement through correction nor narrow this long-term contract.

Every disposition command has an immutable owner-confirmed identity and complete fingerprint binding principal, family, exact predecessor, operation, successor payload/disposition, replacement lineage when applicable, and semantic registry/schema/codec/canonicalization versions. Same identity plus a byte-identical fingerprint returns the complete committed successor after a lost acknowledgement even though the predecessor is no longer current. Reuse with any changed binding conflicts atomically; a different identity targeting that predecessor rejects as stale and cannot be retargeted.

`confirm_candidate` is an authenticated, idempotent adoption command for one immutable candidate revision, never a lookup by mutable subject or screen position. The displayed confirmation affordance binds tenant and authenticated principal; candidate ID and version; exact displayed interpretation; stable subject; canonical payload; complete provenance; consequence text; target `FactClaim` schema and schema version; candidate, claim, policy, and other authority/current heads used to render it; and a digest of that complete display binding. The submitted command repeats the binding and carries a stable confirmation ID and complete fingerprint. Inside one commit transaction the candidate-evidence owner verifies the digest and all bound heads, applies the closed ownership registry, and appends the resulting claim/disposition at most once. The same confirmation ID and fingerprint returns the original committed result after a lost acknowledgement; identity reuse with a changed fingerprint conflicts. A changed, stale, already superseded, disputed, or concurrently conflicted candidate never adopts: the command returns a typed rejection, rebuilds from current authoritative heads, and requires the principal to see and confirm the newly bound display. Generic assent, refreshed screen position, or a projection alone cannot supply this authority.

Every content-bearing derivative and queued action appends typed provenance edges to all semantic sources. A rebuildable reverse-dependency index supports transitive discovery, while a small authoritative deletion-fence registry keyed by deleted source identity prevents replay, import, retained SQLite pages, WAL, snapshots, backups, or quarantine copies from rematerializing fenced content into ordinary state. Missing provenance on a content-bearing derivative fails closed.

Authorized deletion commits the fence before or atomically with exclusion work, immediately blocks new use, traverses the reverse dependencies, and removes semantic payload from ordinary projections, summaries, search, history tools, model context, highlights, and cached snapshots, leaving only policy-minimal, non-dereferenceable tombstone and ordering metadata. Dependent ordinary derivatives are purged or recomputed; inferred state is invalidated; pending actions whose authority or evidence depended on the source are cancelled or held. The reverse index is disposable and rebuildable; the fence is authority. Retention periods remain policy-owned. Version one makes no physical-erasure claim for retained storage media; a stronger non-reconstructive mechanism and its backup evidence are future capability work.

Every evidence-informed planning revision binds its exact material claim dependencies and their effective dispositions at authorization. Later correction, retraction, supersession, dispute, or deletion of a dependency never rewrites Plan. The combined view deterministically becomes `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED`, names the exact affected node and invalidated inference, and may no longer present the planning closure as currently evidenced fulfillment. Non-deleted immutable Plan and claim history remain visible; deleted content is limited to the deletion contract's non-reconstructive residue. Divergence persists until a separate owner-authorized planning command reconciles it. Chiplog raises a separately authorized compensating planning proposal or asks for confirmation under current authority; a journal mutation, projection rebuild, summary, trace, journal view, or provider event cannot perform that reconciliation or clear the divergence.

Any authority-sensitive pending work whose authorization or result depends materially on such a claim revalidates the exact dependency heads and effective dispositions at its last reversible boundary. A mismatch, unresolved conflict, or `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED` dependency holds or cancels the unsafe continuation under its governing protocol and routes to planning reconciliation; it cannot dispatch, close, or compensate by inference. Combined projections reduce only from the unique current claim/disposition, correlation, expectation-lineage, and planning heads. Missing, stale, conflicting, or unordered inputs fail unresolved rather than selecting by timestamp, mixing predecessor and successor state, inferring fulfillment, or silently clearing divergence.

Every initial Turn bundle and every post-tool rebuild batch reads the conversation core, retained-family projections, policy, and internal query state in one SQLite read transaction at one shared authoritative frontier. A genuinely external or lagging source may carry a distinct frontier only when its `ScreenSnapshotRef` marks that staleness explicitly. An authority-sensitive tool never relies on such a screen for authorization or freshness: its preconditions are rechecked against the current authoritative frontier.

## Agent-loop execution model

### Run and Turn

A `Run` begins with one concrete prompt from a user or the scheduler. It contains zero or more `Turn`s. A `Turn` is one model invocation and the response disposition for that invocation. The model—not deterministic orchestration code—directly declares semantic success and supplies the response to deliver. Runtime code validates the declaration, executes tools, enforces policy and budgets, commits trace records, and decides technical continuation or suspension.

The model response is a per-Turn dynamically constructed tagged union:

```text
Continue { tool_calls: [...] }
Complete { deliveries: [...] }
```

A response cannot both request tools and declare success. Tool dispositions are presented in a later Turn; only then may the model complete the Run.

`SealedCallAccountingJoin(run_id, response_id, sealed_manifest_fingerprint)` is the sole authoritative join over a sealed response. In one current SQLite snapshot it enumerates the exact bounded sealed manifest and requires every member exactly once with a `TERMINAL` `CallRecoveryFrontier`: an acceptance-consistent registered call-level terminal disposition plus its required typed result, or terminal `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` plus the exact durable recovery obligation; for a read-only retry lineage this member is the unique immutable `ReadOnlyCallOutcome` and `ToolTerminalDisposition` published by the registered complete-lineage reducer, never an individual attempt terminal. Any `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING`, absent or hybrid branch rejects the whole join. It also requires every initialized/accepted head and applicable execution/effect intent, rejects missing, extra, duplicate, rival, partial, or non-canonical members, and binds the resulting complete accounting manifest and frontier head.

`ModelContinuationReadyJoin(run_id, response_id, sealed_manifest_fingerprint)` is a distinct registered transaction-local predicate over the exact current `SealedCallAccountingJoin` manifest, its frontier head and registry/version fingerprint. It re-enumerates and byte-compares that complete manifest rather than trusting a stored Boolean or projection. Every member must be `TERMINAL` and model-consumable: ordinary terminal results are present and acceptance-consistent; `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` always rejects; and terminal `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` is accepted only when the exact recovered-outcome member is `PRESENT(RecoveredToolOutcome head, fingerprint)`, the exact obligation is closed by the immutable terminal closure head produced in the bound registered original-stream resolver batch, and the registered current `SemanticEvidenceReduction` remains consumable under the same reduction identity. The outcome's authenticated evidence head is an immutable accepted witness, not a claim that it remains the newest raw evidence append. `ABSENT`, evidence-present/obligation-open, outcome-present/obligation-open, closure without its bound outcome, absent or stale current semantic reduction, a changed reduction identity, rival/contradictory/meaning-changing/unclassified evidence, stale outcome/closure/reducer/batch heads, or any manifest/fingerprint mismatch rejects the whole predicate. Its immutable success record binds the accounting manifest and frontier heads, registry/version fingerprint, every exact result or recovered-outcome and accepted-witness head, every exact obligation-terminal head, the bound resolver-batch identity and fingerprint, and the exact current semantic-reduction identity/head; identical complete replay returns it and changed or rival reuse conflicts.

Each appendable authenticated evidence stream has one authoritative registered `SemanticEvidenceReduction { stream_id, reduction_id, reducer_id, reducer_version, current_reduction_head, consumed_evidence_manifest, semantic_class, consumability }`. Only its registered owner may CAS the exact current reduction head using the fixed reducer/version and canonical complete evidence manifest. A compatible authenticated refinement may advance `current_reduction_head` and its consumed manifest while preserving the same `reduction_id`, semantic class, and `CONSUMABLE` status; it neither rewrites the accepted witness, `RecoveredToolOutcome`, obligation closure, nor resolver batch. Rival, contradictory, meaning-changing, unclassified, or reducer/version-changing evidence atomically advances the reduction to a typed non-consumable hold, or conflicts where the registry declares no reduction, and cannot be hidden by selecting an older append or timestamp. No raw evidence append head is itself a continuation credential.

A later model Turn may start only through a registered writer-owned `TurnStarted` CAS. Its immutable command binds the immediately preceding sealed response, exact successful current `ModelContinuationReadyJoin`, exact current `Run: ACTIVE` head, Run id, Turn ordinal, and exact current-Turn head. Inside that same publication transaction the writer re-enumerates the sealed manifest and directly recomputes and byte-compares the continuation-ready predicate, including every immutable accepted witness/outcome/obligation-closure/resolver-batch head and every exact current semantic-reduction identity/head, frontier, registry/version and fingerprint head; checked state from an earlier transaction cannot satisfy it. For scheduler-rooted or otherwise worker-originated work it additionally binds the exact stable-lineage head and lineage-owned `current_run_id`, current-physical-root selector/selected epoch and complete lease identity/live generation/trusted-clock proof. Only then may it atomically append the one next Turn head. Terminalization, suspension, supersession, physical rollover, takeover, lease expiry, generation change, pending retry, open recovery, stale or non-consumable semantic reduction, stale continuation proof, a rival next ordinal, or any Run/lineage/epoch/Turn/join mismatch writes nothing; identical replay returns the existing Turn and changed reuse conflicts. No projection, cursor, per-worker count, earlier check, or fixture substitutes for this transaction-local predicate.

### Run state machine

Successor publication is one indivisible writer transaction. From authoritative rows inside that transaction it re-enumerates and byte-compares the complete frontier manifest and registry fingerprint, recomputes `RecoveryProofDisposition`, and requires exactly `SUCCESSOR_REQUIRED`; no earlier classification can satisfy this precondition. For a scheduler-rooted Run it also compares the exact stable `ExecutionLineage` head, its exact tagged `ExecutionLineageSubject` and byte-compatible root fingerprint, requires lineage-owned `current_run_id` to equal the predecessor, and binds the exact protected physical-root selector, selected `PhysicalExecutionRoot` epoch/head and lease generation as explicit CAS preconditions. Only then may the same CAS publish predecessor `SUPERSEDED`, one successor, edge, complete initialization, and the lineage current-Run advance without changing that matched subject, root fingerprint, selector or physical epoch. Any disposition, subject, lineage-head/fingerprint, selector/epoch, current-Run, lease-head or generation mismatch publishes none of them and restarts from a fresh snapshot.

```text
CREATED ──▶ ACTIVE ──▶ SUCCEEDED
   │          │  └──▶ SUSPENDED ──▶ ACTIVE
   │          │              ├────▶ SUPERSEDED
   │          │              ├────▶ ABORTED
   │          │              └────▶ CANCELLED
   │          ├────▶ ABORTED
   │          └────▶ CANCELLED
   ├───────────────▶ ABORTED
   └───────────────▶ CANCELLED
```

A model `Complete` is only a proposed success transition. `CompleteAcceptance` is the indivisible conjunction of the exact selected `ModelCallAttemptLineage` generation at `RESPONSE_CAPTURED`, its Turn-schema result and immutable per-call `ModelVisibilityManifest`, direct transaction-local recomputation of a successful `ModelContinuationReadyJoin` for every earlier sealed response in the Run lineage, assertion evidence/current-head/code-entailment validation, authority and policy validation, complete disclosure-provenance/label closure, explicit normalized endpoint selection and canonical delivery-manifest validation, deterministic delivery rendering, and creation of the complete durable delivery records/intents. The writer enumerates every earlier response from authoritative lineage rows, recomputes each exact accounting manifest and continuation-ready predicate inside the same acceptance transaction, and byte-compares every bound attempt/response, visibility/provenance/delivery manifest, label/narrowing head, endpoint selection, frontier, registry/version, result/recovered-outcome, obligation-closure and resolver-batch head; no transitive claim, model citation, earlier `TurnStarted`, cached success record, current-projection reconstruction or caller-supplied ancestry can substitute for this direct route. Only after every conjunct passes does that transaction CAS the exact attempt to `TERMINAL_ACCEPTED`, append the accepted semantic result and rendered `ConversationHistory` entry, publish every durable delivery intent, mark the Turn accepted, and advance `Run: ACTIVE → SUCCEEDED`; failure or unknown at any conjunct writes none of those semantic-success records. A definite semantic reject CAS-selects `TERMINAL_REJECTED`; the received response remains immutable equally disclosure-labeled `RunTrace` evidence with typed rejection reasons but cannot itself become accepted completion or `SUCCEEDED`.

Worker-originated `CompleteAcceptance` additionally resolves its exact commit-registry row inside that same transaction. If scheduler-rooted, it rereads and validates the stable lineage and lineage-owned `current_run_id = Run` together with the exact current physical-root selector, selected epoch/head and complete live lease fence; rollover, takeover, expiry, renewal/head change, holder/session substitution or unknown clock writes no accepted result, conversation entry, delivery intent or `SUCCEEDED`. A previously successful Turn or fan-out lease check cannot substitute for this completion-time fence.

Every transition to `SUCCEEDED`, `ABORTED`, or `CANCELLED` is an exact-current-Run-head CAS that transaction-locally enumerates every sealed response and requires its successful `SealedCallAccountingJoin`; it atomically publishes a complete `RunTerminalAccountingManifest` classifying every sealed call by terminal/result or exact recovery-obligation identity. `CANCELLED` closes admission of new Turns and actions, but does not cancel, erase, undo, or claim non-occurrence of already accepted external consequences. Before it commits, every sealed call must be terminal or carry its exact recovery obligation; accepted effect, evidence, delivery, and recovery streams retain their independent lifecycle and may resolve afterward. `ABORTED` and `SUCCEEDED` have the same exhaustive accounting requirement. Missing or unknown accounting leaves the Run nonterminal and held; no terminal label is inferred from worker shutdown or cancellation propagation.

Every worker-originated terminal transition also resolves its exact commit-registry row and, when scheduler-rooted, transaction-locally requires the stable lineage/current-Run plus exact current physical-root selector/epoch/head/lease/generation/expiry/clock fence before publishing the terminal head, accounting manifest or post-terminal work subjects. A stale worker cannot win merely because the Run head has not otherwise changed. Separately authenticated late evidence and later post-terminal work remain governed by their independent owners and leases rather than this physical-root fence.

For every still-open recovery obligation named by a terminal manifest, that same terminal transaction creates or byte-exactly reuses one durable stable `PostTerminalRecoveryWorkSubject { work_id, original_run_id, original_subject_id, recovery_obligation_id, obligation_head, resolver_id, reducer_version }`, one internal initial `WorkLeaseEpoch`, its canonical genesis lease and one protected monotone `PostTerminalWorkCurrentEpochSelector`. The stable subject is operational ownership only: it neither transfers nor replaces the independently owned evidence or recovery-obligation stream, and it never amends the immutable terminal manifest. `PostTerminalWorkLeaseState` is closed and uint64-typed: `UNLEASED { lease_head, generation = 0 } | CLAIMED { lease_head, holder_id, holder_session_id, lease_id, generation:uint64, trusted_expiry, clock_contract_version } | GENERATION_EXHAUSTED_HOLD { lease_head, generation = UINT64_MAX, exhausted_command_id, trusted_expiry, authority_epoch } | CLOSED { exact_obligation_terminal_head }`. Stable fingerprinted `CLAIM | RENEW | TAKEOVER` commands bind the subject, exact current-epoch selector/version, selected epoch/head, work-state head, holder/session, lease ID, generation, expiry, clock proof, original obligation/evidence heads, resolver and canonicalization versions. Claim advances genesis generation once; renewal preserves holder/session/ID/generation while strictly extending a still-live expiry and changing the lease head; takeover requires positive transaction-local expiry proof and advances generation by exactly one. Rival, stale, pre-expiry, unknown-clock or illegal arithmetic writes nothing; no wrap, reset or reuse is legal.

At an expired `CLAIMED` state with `generation = UINT64_MAX`, the exact takeover CAS publishes `GENERATION_EXHAUSTED_HOLD` and admits no old-epoch work. A versioned journal-decided `PostTerminalWorkEpochRolloverDecision` exact-CASes the stable subject, current selector/version, exhausted predecessor epoch/head, hold/lease identity, holder/session, generation/expiry, original obligation/evidence heads, resolver/version and authority proof. One transaction terminally fences the old epoch, creates exactly one fresh non-aliasing `WorkLeaseEpoch` with canonical `UNLEASED(0)`, publishes one reciprocal rollover edge and monotonically advances the selector. It creates no new recovery obligation or work subject, transfers no evidence/obligation/resolver authority, and leaves the open obligation claimable. Lookup, registry admission, replay, traversal, rebuild and dedup resolve the selector; stale old-epoch commands reject. Rival rollover and crash/lost-ack replay select one exact winner.

A worker may act only after transaction-locally joining the authoritative current original-obligation/evidence heads with the stable work subject, exact selector, selected epoch and live lease tuple. Resolver success closes the original obligation under its existing owner and reducer first, then a causally bound work-subject CAS records that exact terminal head; a crash between them leaves replayable closure work, not a second resolver authority. The authoritative current recovery view for a terminal Run joins its immutable `RunTerminalAccountingManifest` to every named original obligation's current head, stable work subject, selected epoch and status, so historical terminalization membership stays frozen while current open/closed/claimed state remains complete. Missing, duplicate, rival, stale or unregistered subject/epoch/selector identity holds and alerts; terminal Run state and epoch exhaustion never suppress recovery admission, and recovery completion never rewrites Run history.

A suspended Run carries a set of concurrent typed causes and recovery obligations. Each obligation binds the exact subject/head, resolver, opening evidence, safe and denied actions, and closure predicate. Approval wait, provider wait, budget exhaustion, delivery reconciliation, and other UI labels are projections over these obligations rather than competing authoritative Run states.

On `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, the same transaction creates an immutable `SuspensionBaseline`. It binds the suspension-time canonical frontier, including the exact `CallRecoveryFrontier` branch and all branch members for every sealed call; every open obligation identity, subject/head, and closure predicate; the objective and requested work; prompt artifact, ordered tool specifications, generated schema and their versions/digests; semantic bindings including recipient and effect fingerprint; authority scope, policy and current authority/mandate heads; and every crossed or still-applicable no-retry boundary. A `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` branch is a valid baselined recovery state but remains nonterminal; a resume cursor is not this baseline and cannot amend it.

Same-Run resume requires an explicit `AdmissibleFrontierSuccessor(SuspensionBaseline, current_frontier)` proof over complete `RecoveryFrontierRegistry` membership. Equality is sufficient but not necessary. For a strict successor, the proof enumerates and causally accounts for every head changed since the baseline, and admits only (a) separately authenticated evidence whose source family, subject/attempt/intent identity, causal predecessor, canonicalization and bound protocol were permitted by the baseline, (b) typed disposition or closure events for baseline obligations, produced by their registered resolver and satisfying the frozen closure predicate, (c) the terminal branch's recovered-outcome subject exact `ABSENT → PRESENT` transition, only in the same registered resolver batch that closes that exact still-open obligation and binds the exact authenticated evidence head and reducer/version, and (d) an exact `CallRecoveryFrontier` transition permitted by its closed branch algebra. Every admitted event must descend from a baseline head or another admitted event; no missing, unrelated, rival, unregistered, or hybrid branch is tolerated. Obligation evolution is monotone: a baseline obligation may remain open or close once through its typed terminal disposition, but cannot be erased, weakened, reopened, replaced, or closed by a projection/cursor. The recovery outcome cannot be published by another writer/batch, without its exact closure and evidence, as a one-to-one rewrite, or as a rival. The proof also requires exact equality of the baseline objective and requested work, semantic bindings, authority/mandate scope and heads, policy, recipient/effect identity, prompt artifact, ordered tool specifications and generated schema, and no-retry boundaries. A pending branch may be preserved in a suspension baseline and predecessor/successor proof, but it cannot authorize same-Run activation: until its exact registered branch transition closes the pending head, `RecoveryProofDisposition` cannot be `SAME_RUN`. A valid strict successor may otherwise accept late provider/channel/reconciliation evidence, close its corresponding obligation, and observe its exact resolver-batch outcome without changing the Run.

`RecoveryProofDisposition` is a total deterministic partition. `SAME_RUN` means equality or a valid admissible successor proof. `SUCCESSOR_REQUIRED` means the complete frontier is well formed and causally accounted, but at least one frozen binding has a valid registered current value different from the baseline—semantic, authority/policy, prompt/tool schema, requested work, recipient/effect, or another successor-permitted binding. `RECOVERY_HOLD` means membership or causal accounting is incomplete, a required head is missing or unknown, rival heads remain unresolved, an obligation state is unaccounted/new/reopened/replaced, or a required fact cannot yet be proved. `TERMINAL_RECOVERY_FAULT` means corrupt canonical bytes or fingerprints, an impossible registered transition or rival terminal, broken immutable ancestry, or another invariant violation. Precedence is fault, then hold, then successor-required, then same-Run; no caller or timestamp chooses the class. Hold writes no successor and preserves the suspended Run; fault appends its typed terminal fault and permits no continuation. Only `SUCCESSOR_REQUIRED` may allocate a successor.

Same-Run activation is published only by one resume writer transaction. The canonically identified resume command and its complete fingerprint bind the Run, exact `SUSPENDED` head and `SuspensionBaseline`, the observed complete frontier-manifest bytes and registry-fingerprint bytes, the recovery-disposition version, and the activation payload. Inside that transaction the writer requires the exact Run head still `SUSPENDED`; re-enumerates from authoritative rows the complete current `RecoveryFrontierRegistry`, including canonically absent recovery-outcome members; recomputes and byte-compares the complete manifest and registry/version fingerprint with the command's observed bytes; recomputes `RecoveryProofDisposition` from that transaction-local frontier; requires it still be `SAME_RUN`; and requires every activation-blocking suspension cause to satisfy its frozen closure predicate. One CAS then atomically publishes the resume activation and advances that exact Run `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. A changed Run head, manifest byte, registry byte, proof input, closure predicate result, or non-`SAME_RUN` reclassification writes nothing and restarts classification from a fresh snapshot; the writer never substitutes its recomputed values into the stale command. Identical command replay returns the published activation, changed reuse conflicts, and a rival resume or successor that wins the exact `SUSPENDED`-head CAS makes the loser stale and subject to fresh classification. No earlier classification, digest-only comparison, cursor, or projection authorizes `ACTIVE`.

Successor allocation uses immutable `RunSuccessorTransition { predecessor_run_id, execution_lineage_subject, execution_lineage_root_fingerprint, observed_execution_lineage_root_head, observed_physical_root_selector_head, observed_physical_root_epoch_id, observed_physical_root_epoch_head, observed_execution_root_lease_head, observed_lease_holder_id, observed_lease_holder_session_id, scheduler_lease_id, observed_lease_generation, trusted_clock_expiry, clock_contract_version, trusted_clock_validity_proof, suspension_baseline_fingerprint, observed_frontier_manifest, observed_registry_fingerprint, recovery_disposition_version, changed_binding_manifest, successor_initialization_manifest }`. In the exact current version, the lineage fields denote stable lineage and the epoch fields form a separate internal fence domain; no historical wire-compatibility claim is made. Its stable identity and fingerprint are derived canonically from every field in the current versioned transition domain. For a non-scheduler Run, every execution-lineage, physical-epoch and lease field carries its registered typed `NOT_APPLICABLE` marker; no scheduler lease predicate is introduced on that branch, and any mixed applicable/non-applicable encoding rejects. For a scheduler-rooted Run, the transition binds the exact stable tagged lineage subject and immutable root fingerprint already referenced by the predecessor, exact lineage head/current Run, exact current selector/version, selected physical epoch/head, lease-state head, holder/session, lease ID, generation, expiry and clock-contract version, plus a registered trusted-clock live-validity proof over those exact bytes, transition identity and submission transaction. The publication transaction rereads and byte-compares every field; physical rollover, acquire, renewal or takeover that changes the selector, epoch or lease head makes an uncommitted older successor stale even when lease ID and generation remain equal.

One SQLite writer transaction first requires the predecessor still at the exact `SUSPENDED` head, then re-enumerates the complete current `RecoveryFrontierRegistry`, recomputes the canonical membership manifest and registry/version fingerprint from authoritative rows, and byte-compares both with the transition's observed values. On the scheduler branch it additionally rereads the stable lineage by the exact tagged subject, requires the exact lineage head and immutable root fingerprint, requires lineage-owned `current_run_id = predecessor_run_id`, resolves and byte-compares the exact current physical-root selector and selected epoch/head, requires the bound lease ID and generation to be the unique current selected-epoch lease holder, and transaction-locally validates from the trusted clock that the bound expiry and validity proof are live at the successor-publication linearization point. Only that same transaction may CAS-select the transition and atomically append the predecessor's `SUPERSEDED` head, exactly one successor Run carrying the same tagged subject/root fingerprint, the `predecessor_run_id` edge, the complete initialization manifest, and the `ExecutionLineage.current_run_id` advance. The selected physical epoch observes the new exact lineage head/current Run through the same internal batch but does not own or rebind Run identity. Lease expiry without takeover, takeover or rollover before submit, lease substitution at the same generation, any lineage-head/current-Run/selector/epoch change, generation mismatch, clock-proof mismatch, or expiry at commit publishes none of the predecessor, successor, edge, initialization, lineage advance or epoch observation; it returns stale/unauthorized and requires a fresh authoritative read and classification. No writer may substitute recomputed lineage, selector, epoch, lease, generation, expiry, clock proof, frontier or registry values into the submitted transition.

Replay is identical only when the complete transition canonical bytes and fingerprint—including exact stable lineage head/subject/fingerprint, physical-root selector/epoch/head, lease identity, generation, expiry and trusted-clock proof—are byte-identical and the existing committed successor was produced by that same fencing proof. Such lost-ack replay returns that one published batch without requiring the old lease to remain live; reuse of the transition identity with any changed field or fencing proof conflicts. Before first publication, however, a now-expired proof never acquires replay status and writes nothing. A rival transition, physical rollover, takeover, lineage-head advance or resume that linearizes first makes the loser stale and subject to fresh classification. There is never a visible `SUPERSEDED` predecessor without its one successor, edge, initialization, lineage current-Run advance and selected-epoch observation, or a successor without the predecessor transition.

Effect, transmission-attempt, delivery, provider/channel/reconciliation evidence, and their disposition streams are owned by their semantic modules and stable subject identities, not by a Run lifecycle. They remain appendable under their own authentication, deduplication, reducer, and binding rules after any observing Run is `SUPERSEDED`, `ABORTED`, `CANCELLED`, or `SUCCEEDED`. Each recovery obligation against such a stream remains authoritative for its whole lifetime under its one original independently owned obligation stream, original Run/call/obligation identity, closure predicate, and resolver binding; Run supersession never changes that authority. Cross-owner recovery is an explicit two-commit causal protocol, never a cross-owner atomic batch: first the evidence owner durably appends authenticated evidence to its independent stream under a stable evidence identity and fingerprint; then the registered original-stream recovery resolver consumes the exact durable evidence stream identity and head, the exact still-open original obligation identity and head, and the bound reducer/version. The resolver CAS-closes that one original obligation head and, when the obligation is for a terminal `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` call, atomically publishes its `RecoveredToolOutcome` bound to the same original call/obligation identity, prior obligation head, authenticated evidence head, reducer/version, and resulting closure head in one permitted owner-local batch. Evidence-present/obligation-open is a legal crash and concurrency state; it forces `RECOVERY_HOLD`, bars the next model Turn and every denied action, and is completed only by resolver replay. An identical evidence append or resolver command and complete output fingerprint returns the existing heads; changed-fingerprint reuse, a rival evidence terminal, rival closure, or rival recovery outcome conflicts and leaves ambiguity held rather than selecting by timestamp. A successor neither moves, copies, transfers, re-parents, reopens, replaces, nor rewrites those records or allocates a rival obligation. Its initialization stores only immutable causal observations of the exact original obligation stream identity/head and exact evidence stream identity/head at an observation frontier; a new immutable observation, never mutation of the old one, records a later original-stream head, and frontier membership follows the original identity and current original-stream head across the Run lineage. Such a reference confers no closure or resolver authority. Late evidence and the registered resolver can close or refine only the independently owned streams and original recovery obligation under the bound reducer; they cannot revive the predecessor, silently mutate successor bindings, authorize a retry, or erase ambiguity.

The successor is initialized as a fresh `CREATED` Run and must independently validate every current semantic, authority/mandate, policy, recipient, prompt/tool-schema, budget, lease, and requested-work binding before `ACTIVE`. Its initialization manifest classifies every predecessor recovery obligation as `REFERENCE_EXTERNAL` to its one original independently owned obligation stream, together with the immutable exact original Run/call/obligation identity, observed obligation head, closure predicate and resolver binding, corresponding evidence stream identity/head, and observation frontier, or as `CLOSED_BEFORE_TRANSITION` with that original obligation's exact terminal head. `TRANSFER_OPEN`, a successor-owned copy, omission, duplication, or any other class rejects the atomic transition. If the original head changes between observation and successor publication, the full-frontier comparison makes the transition stale and writes nothing; if it changes after publication, only a new immutable observation may expose the new original head. The initialization also carries the predecessor's crossed and still-applicable no-retry boundaries as immutable references. Before any tool, model, dispatch, retry, or worker action, a closed action-lineage check proves either that the action is fresh and no referenced boundary was crossed, or that the governing bound protocol explicitly authorizes the exact continuation; unknown proof holds. Thus successor creation never reissues a sealed tool call, unknown model call, `SEND_COMMITTED`/unknown effect, ambiguous delivery, or any other crossed no-retry action.

### Turn and model-call attempt

One permitted invariant batch atomically publishes `ModelResponseReceived` with the complete canonically ordered `ToolCallSubjectInitialized` manifest for every sealed call; neither the response nor any initialized subject is visible alone. The stable response identity, complete manifest, order, and batch fingerprint replay only on byte-exact equality. Omission, addition, duplication, reordering, changed fingerprint, partial allocation, or a rival manifest conflicts and publishes nothing. When worker-originated, the transaction also resolves its exact `WorkerAuthoritativeCommitRegistry` row; on the scheduler-rooted branch it rereads the stable lineage/current Run, current-physical-root selector/selected epoch and complete live `ExecutionRootLeaseState` fence, so stale selector, epoch, holder/session, lease head/ID, generation, expiry or clock proof publishes neither response nor initialized subject. The non-scheduler branch requires exact `NOT_APPLICABLE` scheduler markers and its existing Run/session-generation predicates.

Dispatch has a closed durable acceptance boundary. A consequential call can leave its exact `ToolCallSubjectInitialized` head only through a CAS-selected `ToolCallAccepted` batch whose identity and fingerprint bind the call, initialized head, tool/schema and policy versions, authority/current-state inputs, exact current `Run: ACTIVE` head, and—when worker-originated—the exact live execution-lineage root/current-Run, lease identity, generation, and trusted-clock validity proof, plus dispatch semantics. The acceptance transaction revalidates every bound head and the live lease/generation; a terminal, suspended, superseded, changed-Run, expired-lease, or stale-generation state writes no acceptance or intent. That same transaction publishes the complete applicable `ToolExecutionIntent` and, for an external effect, the authoritative `ExternalActionIntent`; no adapter, worker, provider, or other execution path may dispatch before that batch commits. Identical complete-batch replay returns the accepted head and intents, while a stale initialized or Run head, changed binding, partial batch, or rival accept/cancel conflicts and dispatches nothing. `CANCELLED_BEFORE_ACCEPT` is legal only as a CAS from that exact still-unaccepted initialized head, so it cannot follow either accepted form or stand in for an uncertain accepted execution.

Proven read-only calls use a separate closed `ReadOnlyToolCallAccepted` branch, never `ToolCallAccepted` or a consequential intent. Its registered tool class must positively prove no external or authoritative mutation, stable query identity and snapshot/frontier semantics, and one immutable `ReadOnlyRetryLineage { lineage_id, original_call_id, max_attempts, budget_version, reducer_id, reducer_version }` allocated at the original sealed-call initialization. Every accepted attempt has a unique ordinal and exact predecessor and reaches exactly one immutable registered per-attempt outcome with its typed result or exact recovery obligation. Its acceptance CAS binds the exact current `Run: ACTIVE` head and, when worker-originated, live lease/generation. Another ordinal is admissible only when the immediately preceding attempt has the registered `FAILED_DEFINITE` outcome whose exact error class is explicitly retryable under the lineage's frozen reducer/version, no recovery obligation is open, the call-level outcome is still canonically absent, and the lineage-wide budget CAS has capacity. `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, an open recovery obligation, success, a non-retryable definite failure, exhaustion, or any already published call-level outcome forbids another attempt.

One registered reducer consumes the complete canonically ordered attempt lineage and its exact current heads. It never rewrites attempt outcomes. In one CAS-selected batch it publishes exactly one immutable call-level `ReadOnlyCallOutcome` and its matching `ToolTerminalDisposition` for the original sealed call: first success selects `SUCCEEDED` with that exact typed result; a non-retryable definite failure selects `FAILED_DEFINITE`; a retryable definite failure at the frozen `max_attempts` selects `FAILED_DEFINITE` with typed budget-exhausted provenance; and `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or an exact recovery obligation selects the corresponding `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` hold form without retry. This unique call-level record, not any attempt record, is consumed once by `SealedCallAccountingJoin`. Publication atomically closes the lineage; identical complete-lineage replay returns it, while an incomplete lineage, missing/extra/reordered attempt, changed head, rival reduction, or any post-terminal retry writes nothing. One lineage-wide CAS advances `attempts_consumed`; Turn changes, same-Run suspension/resume, successor Runs, process restart, scheduler takeover, or call re-materialization reference that same counter and cannot allocate or reset a budget. Unknown, unregistered, dynamically mutating, authority-writing, provider-effecting, exhausted, terminal-Run, stale-generation, or no-longer-provably-read-only calls fail this branch and cannot borrow either retry permission or consequential acceptance semantics.

When the latest attempt is an explicitly retryable `FAILED_DEFINITE` below the frozen bound but a frozen/current semantic, authority, policy, tool-schema, snapshot, or execution binding prevents another attempt in the same Run, one registered writer command may atomically publish the `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` branch of the original call's `CallRecoveryFrontier`. Its stable pending identity is derived from the original `lineage_id` and `original_call_id`; its immutable payload binds the unique pending head, exact initialized and accepted heads, complete canonically ordered attempt-head manifest, last retryable failure head, shared lineage-counter head, frozen reducer id/version, budget version, `max_attempts`, current lineage-wide `attempts_consumed`, next ordinal, read-only proof, snapshot and crossed binding heads, and closure registry/version. The same branch canonically records `ABSENT` for call-level `ToolTerminalDisposition` and `ReadOnlyCallOutcome`; it cannot coexist with an open attempt recovery obligation, either call-level head, or a `TERMINAL` branch. It is an exact pre-suspension recovery state, so it may satisfy the suspension/baseline subject-classification gate and successor proof, but it can never satisfy `SealedCallAccountingJoin`, a later Turn gate, `CompleteAcceptance`, or a Run terminal manifest.

Successor initialization may carry only an immutable reference to that exact pending branch and all its heads under the ordinary suspension/successor CAS; it cannot copy, transfer, replace, re-key, or partially reconstruct it. After the successor independently reaches `ACTIVE`, the next attempt is admissible only through one writer transaction that revalidates the current registered tool as read-only, validates every current semantic/authority/policy/tool-schema/snapshot binding, matches the exact pending branch, pending head, complete attempt manifest, original logical `call_id`, next ordinal and shared counter head, and has remaining frozen budget. That transaction atomically closes the old pending head as `NEXT_ATTEMPT_ACCEPTED`, advances the same lineage-wide counter, publishes the next `ReadOnlyToolCallAccepted`, and publishes the uniquely derived successor `READ_ONLY_RETRY_PENDING` branch containing the extended ordered manifest and new counter head; it allocates neither a new logical call nor a new lineage. If the attempt immediately yields a reducer-terminal result, the same admissible frontier transition may instead atomically close pending and publish the complete `TERMINAL` branch. The other legal pending-to-terminal closures are `CALL_REDUCED_TERMINAL`, bound to the unique complete-lineage reducer batch, and `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`, bound to the exact registered recovery-obligation reduction; both replace canonical terminal/output absence with exactly one complete terminal branch, never a hybrid. Same-Run resume cannot consume pending, failed validation cannot silently discard it, and suspension/baseline/successor publication, restart, takeover, or repeated materialization cannot reset the counter, mutate prior attempts, publish a second current pending head, strand a branchless call, double-count a terminal, or bypass the exact branch transition. Thus every pending state has a durable owner and one next-attempt-or-terminal transition while all model-Turn and Run-terminal joins remain blocked until the original call's frontier is `TERMINAL`.

A logical Turn advances through preparation, active call, response availability, disposition, and durable commit. Its one `ModelCallAttemptLineage` per call slot contains immutable attempt generations under the closed durable lifecycle:

```text
PREPARED_NOT_EMITTED ──▶ EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN ──▶ RESPONSE_CAPTURED
                                                       ├──▶ TERMINAL_REJECTED
                                                       └──▶ TERMINAL_ACCEPTED
```

The emission transition is durable before the first externally observable request byte. A pre-emission failure can advance the same lineage only through an exact registered proof-of-no-exposure and monotone retry-generation selector; possible emission is never automatically retried. A late response joins only its exact selected `EMITTED_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` attempt and manifest. A response to an already terminal or superseded generation is recorded as separately authenticated late evidence without changing its disposition. Retry never allocates a new Turn/call slot to evade the lineage selector, because two semantic responses could otherwise compete.

Crash-safe continuation is anchored by the durable event graph. Each sealed call reaches exactly one closed `ToolTerminalDisposition`: `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED_DEFINITE`, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, `CANCELLED_BEFORE_ACCEPT`, or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`. `SUCCEEDED` requires a typed success `ToolResultRecorded`; `FAILED_DEFINITE` requires a typed error result; `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` requires a typed unknown-outcome result and no-retry boundary; `CANCELLED_BEFORE_ACCEPT` requires a typed not-executed result and the exact still-unaccepted initialized predecessor; `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` is itself terminal, requires no `ToolResultRecorded`, and instead requires an explicit durable `ToolResultRecoveryObligation` naming owner, evidence sought, safe/denied actions, closure predicate, and bound reducer/version. After either accepted head, a crash or lost acknowledgement without a definite registered result reduces only to the applicable `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` path; it never reduces to cancellation, deletes the acceptance/intents, or permits consequential reissue. No later event mutates that disposition or recasts the original uncertain execution as `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED_DEFINITE`, or another terminal result.

Successful recovery of a `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` call publishes a separate immutable `RecoveredToolOutcome { call_id, terminal_disposition_head, recovery_obligation_id, recovery_obligation_head, authenticated_evidence_stream_id, authenticated_evidence_head, reducer_id, reducer_version, semantic_outcome }`. The registered model-consumable tool form is exactly the pair `(RECOVERY_REQUIRED, exact RecoveredToolOutcome)`; neither member alone supplies recovered semantics, and the pair preserves rather than rewrites the original uncertainty. Each obligation permits one CAS-selected recovery-outcome head. The resolver command and outcome fingerprint cover every field plus the resulting obligation-closure head: identical replay returns the existing outcome/closure, while a changed fingerprint, different evidence head or reducer, or rival semantic outcome conflicts and remains held. When disposition and result are locally known, one permitted atomic invariant batch publishes both against the exact call head. When an adapter boundary prevents that batch, the first durable terminal fact atomically publishes `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` and its matching obligation; the cross-owner evidence/resolver protocol later publishes closure plus `RecoveredToolOutcome`, and a later model Turn remains barred while evidence is absent, evidence is present but the obligation remains open, or the exact registered pair is unavailable. Equal call/result identity and fingerprint replay; changed content or rival terminal conflicts. Recovery follows these open obligations rather than inventing a result or relying on a cross-hexagon transaction.

Recovery closure also initializes the evidence stream's registered semantic reduction with the same reducer/version, accepted witness, semantic class, and a stable `reduction_id`. Thereafter raw evidence append and semantic reduction are separate ordered transitions. For `e1 closure/outcome -> e2 compatible refinement`, if reduction CAS wins before `TurnStarted` or `CompleteAcceptance`, that consumer observes the advanced head with the same identity and may proceed; if the consumer commits first, its immutable join records the exact preceding current reduction and the later compatible advance does not invalidate the committed Turn or acceptance. For rival, contradictory, meaning-changing, unclassified, or reducer-changing `e2`, a reduction hold that commits first bars the consumer; a consumer that commits first remains historically valid, while the later hold governs every subsequent consumer and recovery workflow. No ordering permits reclosure, a second outcome, witness substitution, reducer reinterpretation, or timestamp-based winner selection.

A resume cursor, if materialized, is disposable and derived from one consistent read of the authoritative graph. It grants no authority, cannot prove closure merely by existing, and must be rebuildable. Resume recomputes the current canonical frontier and open obligations, loads the immutable `SuspensionBaseline`, and validates the complete `AdmissibleFrontierSuccessor` proof; restart never infers effect commitment or obligation closure from a cursor, projection, or incomplete trace.

### Recovery policy and budgets

Before `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `SuspensionBaseline` publication, every subject in the sealed complete initialization manifest must already have exactly one registered terminal/result form or an exact durable recovery obligation, and its initialized/accepted head plus every acceptance intent must be present in the complete frontier. An exact still-unaccepted initialized call that never started cannot disappear: it uses the registered `CANCELLED_BEFORE_ACCEPT` never-started terminal form with its typed not-executed result. An accepted call without a definite result instead materializes `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`; missing, partial, duplicate, rival, or acceptance-inconsistent terminal/result/obligation materialization bars suspension and baseline publication.

Version one permits exact crash resume only at the baseline frontier or at a frontier proven to be its admissible monotone successor, plus the distinct bounded `ReadOnlyToolCallAccepted` retry branch for operations positively proven read-only. A non-`SAME_RUN` proof never falls back to partial replay: only a well-formed, fully accounted valid frozen-binding change is `SUCCESSOR_REQUIRED`; incomplete, rival, missing, or unaccounted state—including durable evidence whose exact obligation has not yet been CAS-closed—is `RECOVERY_HOLD`; corruption or an impossible transition is `TERMINAL_RECOVERY_FAULT`. Same-Run and successor publication each revalidate the complete canonical frontier manifest and registry fingerprint, including call acceptance and execution/effect-intent heads, inside their publishing writer transaction: the resume writer must transaction-locally recompute `SAME_RUN`, satisfy activation-blocking closure predicates, and CAS `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, while the successor writer must transaction-locally recompute `SUCCESSOR_REQUIRED` before its existing atomic supersession publication. A stale comparison or reclassification writes nothing and restarts classification. Cross-model failover is disabled. Unknown model calls, accepted consequential tool calls without definite results, and unknown external effects are not retried. A recovered tool outcome becomes model-consumable only through the registered `(RECOVERY_REQUIRED, exact RecoveredToolOutcome)` pair and never rewrites the terminal call fact. Approval, budget extension, and duplicate-risk recovery require explicit decisions, and any decision that changes a frozen baseline binding belongs to the atomically allocated successor Run with fresh validation, immutable `REFERENCE_EXTERNAL` observation of each still-applicable original obligation, and inherited no-retry references.

A Run binds either:

- an explicit versioned budget policy; or
- an explicit `NO_POLICY` marker.

The default budget policy limits only `max_turns`. Token, cost, wall-time, retry, and landing-reserve limits are opt-in. A landing reserve may be configured, but correctness never depends on a final model call. Exhaustion suspends for explicit extension or aborts according to the bound policy.

Scheduler-triggered Runs notify the user only when human action is required or uncertainty cannot safely remain backgrounded: required approval, unknown/partial external effect, terminal abort, exhausted recovery policy, or semantic change requiring a successor. Fully recovered transient faults remain visible in traces and metrics.

Each scheduled occurrence has the stable identity `schedule_id + schedule_revision + canonical_due_coordinate`. The scheduler transactionally claims that identity through the common ingress/inbox deduplication path and binds the allocated Run to the exact schedule and policy revision. A versioned missed-occurrence policy chooses `skip`, `coalesce`, or `materialize each`, and its decision is durable. Restart does not recompute a past disposition from current wall time or a newer schedule revision.

`ScheduleDefinitionHead` and `MissedOccurrencePolicyHead` are typed exact-current-head references to the existing authoritative schedule-definition and missed-occurrence-policy revision streams; they are not new event families or projections. Every scheduler command that can publish an individual, coalesced, skipped, or boundary-only decision binds both observed heads and their exact revision-record fingerprints. Inside the same EventAppender transaction that would publish the decision, the writer rereads both current heads and byte-compares them with the command before any branch CAS. An amendment or policy replacement that wins first makes every old-head command stale and writes none of its disposition, boundary, aggregate, root, Run, or decision records; the command must be rebuilt from the new authoritative heads. If an old-head branch wins first, the later amendment observes that durable decision under its ordinary current-head transition rules. Version one has no implicit pending-old-occurrence survival or cutover branch: retaining old pending occurrences would require a future explicit durable cutover manifest that proves a non-overlapping old/new due-set partition.

`OccurrenceDispositionRegistry` is a closed three-branch registry: `CLAIMED_INDIVIDUALLY | COALESCED_INTO | SKIPPED`. These and only these are occurrence-disposition branches; `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK` below is an interval-decision branch and consumes no occurrence head. All three disposition branches consume the exact canonical `UNDISPOSED` occurrence head by CAS and in the same transaction match the exact current `ScheduleDefinitionHead` and `MissedOccurrencePolicyHead`. The ordinary branch transaction must prove the occurrence is still canonically undisposed, outside every committed or concurrently matched coalescing or skip decision boundary/manifest, and at the exact current decision-stream boundary/frontier before it atomically publishes `CLAIMED_INDIVIDUALLY` as part of the complete scheduled-occurrence materialization batch defined below. The coalescing transaction requires the same `UNDISPOSED` head for every manifest member and atomically changes each to `COALESCED_INTO` in its corresponding complete batch. A committed skip is therefore rejected by both ordinary and coalescing admission; skip likewise rejects an earlier individual claim or coalescing membership. Whichever branch linearizes first makes every conflicting ordinary, aggregate, or skip command stale; identical replay returns the whole winning batch, changed reuse conflicts, and there is no intermediate claimed-without-root-or-Run, aggregate-without-all-member-dispositions-or-Run, or policy-decision-without-skip state.

Every missed-occurrence interval is decided by the closed internal `ScheduledIntervalDecisionBatch` union `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK | SKIP_ALL | COALESCE_SINGLE | COALESCE_MULTI | MATERIALIZE_EACH_SINGLE | MATERIALIZE_EACH_MULTI | OVERFLOW_HOLD`. It binds one immutable `SchedulerEligibilityBoundary { schedule_definition_head, missed_occurrence_policy_head, schedule_id, schedule_revision, policy_revision, policy_kind, previous_due_boundary, cutoff_due_coordinate, enumeration_frontier, canonicalization_version }`; the half-open interval is `[previous_due_boundary, cutoff_due_coordinate)`, so a due coordinate at the cutoff belongs to the next interval. Inside the one writer transaction, the writer rereads and byte-matches both current heads, independently enumerates every eligible still-undisposed occurrence at the authoritative frontier, canonically orders the complete `SchedulerEligibilityManifest`, and byte-compares every submitted member/head. The previous boundary must be the exact terminal boundary of the preceding durable interval decision or its registered genesis. No branch may advance the boundary unless every manifest member's exact `UNDISPOSED` head is consumed into the policy-required disposition and, when applicable, complete materialization sub-batch in that same transaction; a missing, stale, extra, rival or still-undisposed member makes the whole decision write nothing.

`SchedulerIntervalBoundHead { head_id, predecessor_head_id, generation, bound, owner_id, authority_epoch, broker_generation, runtime_graph_generation, canonicalization_version }` is the authenticated monotone current-head state owned by the scheduler's existing EventAppender authority; its `bound` is `SchedulerIntervalBound { max_member_count, max_manifest_bytes, max_serialized_batch_bytes }`. Bound genesis and replacement are stable fingerprinted journal decisions in the same order as interval decisions, hold creation and resolution. Replacement CAS-matches the exact current head/generation, advances generation by one, binds the current scheduler assembly fence and never rolls back, reuses or aliases an old head. Startup reconstructs the current head from the protected decision journal before scheduler admission. Every ordinary interval command, hold and resolution binds and transaction-locally rereads the exact current bound head, not merely configuration bytes. The writer derives count, manifest bytes and proposed serialized whole-batch bytes from the independently enumerated canonical eligible set before choosing an ordinary branch. Within all three bounds, the exact policy algebra is:

- `SKIP`: zero members publishes `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK`; one or more members publishes one atomic `SKIP_ALL` batch containing the durable policy decision, `SKIPPED { policy_decision_id, decision_fingerprint }` for all and only manifest members, and then the exact boundary advance. It publishes no aggregate, root or Run.
- `COALESCE`: zero publishes `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK`; one publishes `COALESCE_SINGLE`, containing one `INDIVIDUAL` disposition/root/initial-Run batch and boundary advance; `2..max_member_count` publishes `COALESCE_MULTI`, containing all-and-only member `COALESCED_INTO` dispositions, one aggregate/tagged root/initial Run and boundary advance.
- `MATERIALIZE_EACH`: zero publishes `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK`; one publishes `MATERIALIZE_EACH_SINGLE`, containing one `INDIVIDUAL` disposition/root/initial-Run batch and boundary advance; `2..max_member_count` publishes one atomic `MATERIALIZE_EACH_MULTI` interval batch containing all and only `N` independently identified `INDIVIDUAL` disposition/tagged-root/initial-Run/init/reciprocal sub-batches plus the one boundary advance. Each sub-batch retains its own occurrence subject, root fingerprint and `run_id = initial_run_id = current_run_id`; no aggregate or shared Run is created.

`BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK` binds the independently reproduced empty manifest and otherwise complete interval decision. In one EventAppender journal decision and SQLite transaction it publishes the durable missed-policy decision and exact boundary advance, with no occurrence disposition, aggregate, tagged subject, root, Run or initialization. `SKIP_ALL` and both `MATERIALIZE_EACH` multi-member branches are whole-interval atomic: no prefix disposition/root/Run or intermediate boundary is visible, and version one has no automatic chunking or continuation frontier. Identical replay returns the whole selected interval batch; changed or rival policy/head/member/order/boundary/frontier/bound/command conflicts. Restart consumes the durable decision rather than reselecting a branch from wall time.

If member count, canonical manifest bytes or proposed serialized whole-batch bytes exceeds its exact registered limit under any policy, the sole branch is durable `OVERFLOW_HOLD`; no ordinary disposition, materialization or boundary advance may publish. Its stable identity and fingerprint bind policy, exact current schedule/policy heads, schedule/revisions, previous boundary, cutoff, frontier, bound/version, exceeded dimension, actual measured value and limit, operator/recovery owner and stable command. The writer derives it from the same independently complete eligible-set enumeration: when complete manifest bytes fit, the hold stores the full canonical manifest and all member heads; when they exceed the manifest limit, it stores an authenticated streaming digest/count plus bounded first/last/order and enumeration-completeness evidence sufficient to reproduce the exact digest from the same authoritative heads/frontier—never a caller assertion or truncated-success subset. Byte-identical replay/restart returns the same active hold and performs no new interval decision; changed reuse conflicts; retry under unchanged heads cannot bypass the hold or chunk automatically.

`SchedulerOverflowHoldState` is closed in version one: `ACTIVE | RESOLVED_BY_OPERATOR(exact_resolution_decision_id)`. While an exact `ACTIVE` hold exists, every schedule-definition or missed-policy amendment for that schedule is rejected; there is no supersession, retention, carry-forward or cutover branch. The operator must first publish an exact current successor `SchedulerIntervalBoundHead` whose bound admits the independently re-enumerated complete set, then fully resolve the held interval. Only after the resolution's boundary and all member dispositions/materializations are durable may an amendment compete under ordinary current-head rules.

`SchedulerIntervalResolutionDecision` is the sole terminal successor of one exact active hold and the canonical parent named by every emitted occurrence sub-batch. Its stable identity/fingerprint binds the hold identity/head/fingerprint, original interval identity/boundary/frontier and old bound head, exact current successor bound head, exact current schedule/policy heads, complete independently re-enumerated manifest/member heads, selected policy branch, resulting boundary, stable command and schema versions. One EventAppender journal decision/SQLite transaction CAS-consumes the active hold, publishes `RESOLVED_BY_OPERATOR`, the complete policy-required branch and exactly one boundary advance. It is not a second `ScheduledIntervalDecisionBatch`; no ordinary rival for the same predecessor/cutoff can publish. Missing, stale or insufficient bound, changed membership, partial/rival parent, chunk or prefix writes nothing. Exact replay returns the one resolution lineage and batch.

For `COALESCE_MULTI`, only after exact manifest equality, `2..max_member_count` cardinality, byte-bound validation and exact current-head equality does the transaction derive `CoalescedOccurrenceAggregateId` and publish the aggregate, immutable eligibility/member manifest, every and only manifest member's `COALESCED_INTO { aggregate_id, manifest_fingerprint }` disposition, boundary, tagged root and initial Run. Omission, addition, duplicate membership, overlap, changed order/fingerprint/frontier/boundary/head/bound, already disposed member, partial publication, aggregate/root/Run mismatch or rival aggregate writes nothing.

`ExecutionLineageSubject` is a closed tagged union: `INDIVIDUAL(occurrence_id) | COALESCED(aggregate_id, manifest_fingerprint)`. The discriminant is the first field of its canonical bytes; variant payloads have disjoint registered namespaces, and equal payload bytes under different tags remain different subjects. Any cross-variant namespace alias, collision, untagged value, unknown tag, or subject whose tag disagrees with its occurrence disposition is rejected before publication. The individual subject is derived from the exact canonical occurrence identity. The coalesced subject additionally binds the exact complete `SchedulerEligibilityBoundary`, the complete canonically ordered `SchedulerEligibilityManifest`, and its fingerprint; its `aggregate_id`, `manifest_fingerprint`, and subject canonical bytes are derived from those same registered bytes. Member permutation, omission, addition, duplication, representative-member substitution, boundary/frontier change, or reuse of an aggregate/root identity with a changed manifest therefore conflicts rather than selecting another root.

The non-skipped occurrence sub-batches are the closed `ScheduledOccurrenceMaterializationBatch` union `INDIVIDUAL | COALESCED`. It is an internal bounded Pydantic composition over existing durable record types, not a new tenant event kind. Its versioned canonical derivation graph is strictly acyclic: (1) `ScheduledBatchPrimitiveDomainV1` contains only primitive command, parent decision/resolution, heads, manifest, subject, root and initial-Run inputs and explicitly excludes every lease field, initialization/reciprocal derived field, every commitment field and every commitment storage slot; (2) `genesis_lease_head = H("execution-root-lease-genesis-v1", PrimitiveDomainV1)` and `UNLEASED(generation=0)` are derived; (3) `ScheduledBatchFinalizedMembersV1` serializes initialization and reciprocal root/Run/lease bindings using that lease head but contains no envelope commitment field or slot; (4) `batch_fingerprint = H("scheduled-batch-envelope-v1", FinalizedMembersV1)` is stored only in the envelope slot canonically excluded from its own input. No initialization or member embeds a fingerprint that covers itself; references use the parent decision/resolution fingerprint and finalized member identities. A generated schema-dependency manifest must be a DAG and two independent encoders must byte-match every domain before admission. Unknown, omitted, extra, duplicate, unbounded, differently ordered, differently headed, cyclic or differently fingerprinted members reject before publication.

The single broker-owned `EventAppender` interval command first rereads and CAS-matches the exact current schedule-definition and missed-policy heads, then selects the policy-discriminated interval branch and every required occurrence-disposition CAS. In the same journal `DECIDED_COMMIT` and SQLite writer transaction it publishes the parent interval decision, all occurrence sub-batches and the boundary advance where permitted. Each `INDIVIDUAL` sub-batch publishes exactly one `CLAIMED_INDIVIDUALLY` disposition, `INDIVIDUAL(occurrence_id)`, immutable root, one immutable initial `CREATED` Run, complete initialization, reciprocal Run/root reference and exactly one canonically derived genesis `UNLEASED` state reciprocally bound to that root and Run. A `COALESCED` sub-batch publishes exactly the `2..max_member_count` manifest dispositions plus aggregate, tagged subject/root, one initial `CREATED` Run, initialization, reciprocal reference and exactly one matching genesis `UNLEASED`. In either occurrence sub-batch `run_id = root.initial_run_id = root.current_run_id`; the Run, root and lease state carry the same tagged subject/root fingerprint, and initialization byte-binds the parent interval decision, exact current heads, schedule/revision/policy, occurrence or aggregate inputs, genesis lease head/generation, bound, versions, stable command and whole-batch fingerprint. `SKIP_ALL` publishes its separate all-member skip batch and no lineage or lease state. `MATERIALIZE_EACH_MULTI` canonically orders and publishes N complete independent individual sub-batches and therefore exactly N independent root-bound genesis `UNLEASED` states under one parent decision; no sub-batch or lease genesis can replay, conflict or become visible independently of the whole interval command.

No disposition, aggregate, boundary, subject, root, Run, initialization, genesis lease state, or reciprocal reference from a non-skipped branch is visible unless the whole batch reaches `DECIDED_COMMIT`; a crash before that decision leaves none visible, and a crash after it leaves all visible. Byte-identical replay returns the entire immutable winning batch without allocating another Run or lease genesis. Reuse of the command or any member identity with a changed field/fingerprint, or a rival disposition, aggregate, root, Run, initialization, lease state, or reciprocal reference, conflicts and writes nothing.

Startup, replay, projection rebuild, and recovery enumerate these records from one consistent journal/materialization snapshot and enforce interval- and occurrence-batch bidirectional joins. Every non-skipped occurrence disposition reaches exactly one `ScheduledOccurrenceMaterializationBatch`, tagged root, complete initial `CREATED` Run/init, exactly one canonical genesis `UNLEASED` state and exactly one parent interval decision; every scheduler-rooted initial Run, root and genesis lease state reaches exactly one occurrence sub-batch and parent interval decision through reciprocal root/Run/lease bindings and the same whole-batch fingerprint. `COALESCE_SINGLE` and `MATERIALIZE_EACH_SINGLE` each map bijectively to one `INDIVIDUAL` disposition/root/Run/genesis-lease tuple. `COALESCE_MULTI` maps bijectively to all and only its `N` ordered `COALESCED_INTO` dispositions and one aggregate/root/Run/genesis-lease tuple. `MATERIALIZE_EACH_MULTI` maps bijectively to all and only its `N` ordered `CLAIMED_INDIVIDUALLY` dispositions and exactly `N` independently identified individual root/Run/genesis-lease sub-batches. `SKIP_ALL` maps bijectively to all and only its `N` ordered `SKIPPED` dispositions and no root/Run/lease state. `BOUNDARY_ONLY_NO_WORK` and `OVERFLOW_HOLD` reach no occurrence disposition, root, Run or lease state and validate only through their interval-decision/hold lineage. Missing, extra, duplicate, orphaned, partial, rival, mismatched, wrong-genesis-head/generation, out-of-bound, non-bijective, still-undisposed-behind-boundary or unverifiable membership holds scheduler admission before any lease acquisition, renewal, takeover, Turn, dispatch or successor action; recovery never fabricates a missing member or treats a projection as authority.

The immutable `ExecutionLineage { subject, initial_run_id, current_run_id, schedule_id, schedule_revision, policy_revision, root_fingerprint }` is the stable execution identity permanently referenced by every Run in its chain. Within the one exact current version, its `root_id`, tagged subject and `root_fingerprint` canonical fields mean the stable lineage and derive from complete tagged subject bytes, exact schedule/revision/policy, initial Run identity and registered stable fields. For a coalesced subject the fingerprint also covers the complete eligibility boundary and canonical member manifest through the subject binding. Lineage alone owns `current_run_id`; no physical-root rollover creates, rewrites or rebinds a Run. Batch publication independently re-derives and byte-compares those values; aggregate-ID/lineage-subject mismatch, manifest/lineage mismatch, Run/lineage mismatch, or any individual/coalesced namespace collision publishes nothing. Identical current-version lost-ack replay returns the same whole batch; changed or rival reuse conflicts.

`PhysicalExecutionRoot { epoch_id, epoch_head, execution_lineage_root_id, execution_lineage_root_fingerprint, observed_lineage_head, observed_current_run_id, predecessor_epoch_id, authority_epoch, epoch_fingerprint }` is a replaceable internal lease epoch inside one stable lineage. A protected monotone `ExecutionLineageCurrentPhysicalRootSelector { lineage_id, selector_head, selector_version, current_epoch_id, current_epoch_head }` selects exactly one epoch. The selected epoch observes the exact lineage head and lineage-owned current Run but never owns or changes either. Every live worker, Turn, response fan-out, completion, terminal and successor fence binds both the byte-compatible stable lineage tuple and the exact selector/version/selected epoch/head plus its lease tuple. Traversal and rebuild join each Run to exactly one lineage and each lineage to exactly one current selected physical epoch; dedup keys retain stable lineage identity and add the current epoch only as an authority fence.

Current-version canonical mapping is closed:

| Current-version name | Semantic value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `root_id`, tagged `root_subject`, `root_fingerprint` | stable `ExecutionLineage` identity | never denotes a physical epoch |
| `execution_lineage_root_head` | stable lineage head/current-Run state | lineage remains sole `current_run_id` owner |
| Run reciprocal root reference | stable lineage id/subject/fingerprint | remains unchanged across physical rollover |
| physical-root selector/epoch/head/lease | mandatory internal authority companion records | separate tagged current-version domains present from genesis/first publication |
| work-epoch selector/epoch/head/lease | mandatory internal post-terminal authority companions | stable work-subject and original obligation remain unchanged |

Byte-identical replay is guaranteed only for an exact current-version decision and complete commitment: it compares every ordinary member and every mandatory companion record in their registered current domains. Replay never derives or synthesizes missing companions for a historical or version-mismatched decision. Such a store is unsupported before replay; a current-version non-prefix or incomplete-companion state is an integrity hold.

The current-version occurrence decision's internal materialization commitment includes one separately tagged companion manifest for the initial physical epoch, selector and genesis lease alongside the lineage/Run members. First publication, exact-prefix completion and complete replay apply to the whole exact-version decision commitment, so neither the ordinary batch nor a companion record is visible as an authoritative partial. No older decision or envelope is extended.

The tagged subject binding is permanent and is never directly rebound to a replacement Run. Root lookup, lease acquire/renew/takeover, `current_run_id` CAS, successor publication, Run-to-root references, lineage traversal, replay, and deduplication all consume and compare that exact tagged subject and root fingerprint; none may accept a member occurrence, a chosen representative, an aggregate ID without its tag and manifest fingerprint, or a separately reconstructed alias. The root has one explicit CAS-advanced `current_run_id`; every Run in its singly linked predecessor/successor chain immutably references the same root subject and fingerprint, and traversal from that root through exact successor-transition records must yield exactly one current non-superseded Run. Successor allocation advances the root's current Run in the same transaction that publishes predecessor `SUPERSEDED` plus the successor. Lease expiry alone never allocates a successor. Independently authenticated provider, channel, and reconciliation evidence may append against its exact original intent/attempt after lease expiry or Run supersession; reducers are monotone and impossible rival evidence conflicts. Takeover and successor initialization handle only explicitly classified open obligations and never reissue an action without the closed action-lineage proof that its no-retry boundary was not crossed.

Every physical epoch owns one durable closed `ExecutionRootLeaseState`: canonical genesis `UNLEASED { lease_head, generation = 0:uint64 } | HELD { lease_head, holder_id, holder_session_id, lease_id, generation:uint64, trusted_expiry, clock_contract_version } | GENERATION_EXHAUSTED_HOLD { lease_head, generation = UINT64_MAX, exhausted_command_id, trusted_expiry, authority_epoch }`. The root-creating occurrence sub-batch is the sole zero-to-one producer of the stable lineage, initial physical epoch, selector and that epoch's `UNLEASED`; no later initializer exists. Each immutable `ExecutionRootLeaseTransition { command_id, command_fingerprint, kind, execution_lineage_root_id, root_fingerprint, observed_lineage_head, observed_current_run_id, observed_physical_root_selector_head, observed_physical_root_epoch_id, observed_physical_root_epoch_head, observed_lease_head, observed_holder, observed_holder_session_id, observed_lease_id, observed_generation, observed_expiry, proposed_holder, proposed_holder_session_id, proposed_lease_id, proposed_expiry, trusted_clock_proof, schema/canonicalization_version }` has a stable identity derived from all fields. The one EventAppender CAS transaction rereads and byte-matches the exact lineage/current Run, current selector/version, selected epoch/head and lease head, validates the proof against the registered trusted clock at its linearization point, and publishes exactly one new lease head or nothing.

The legal physical-epoch lease transitions are closed. `ACQUIRE` requires the exact current `UNLEASED` head, a fresh lease ID, `proposed_generation = observed_generation + 1` within uint64, a live holder/session and future expiry. `RENEW` requires the same exact holder/session, lease ID and generation on `HELD`, a still-live lease at the CAS cut, and a strictly later bounded expiry; generation does not change. `TAKEOVER` requires exact current `HELD`, a different fresh lease ID and valid holder/session, positive trusted-clock proof that `now >= observed_expiry` at the CAS cut, and exactly `proposed_generation = observed_generation + 1`. When an expired `HELD` is already at `UINT64_MAX`, takeover cannot wrap or reset: the same CAS publishes durable `GENERATION_EXHAUSTED_HOLD` and admits no further old-epoch work. Pre-expiry or unknown-clock takeover writes nothing. Rival commands compete on the same lease head; exact replay returns the committed state.

Recovery from exhaustion uses one versioned journal-decided `PhysicalExecutionRootRolloverDecision` and immutable `PhysicalExecutionRootRolloverEdge`. The authenticated command CAS-matches the exact stable lineage/head/fingerprint/current Run, protected current-physical-root selector/version, exhausted predecessor epoch/head, `GENERATION_EXHAUSTED_HOLD` head, old lease identity/holder/session/generation/expiry, authority epoch and trusted operator proof. Its fingerprint also binds the predecessor physical rollover decision/edge when present and selector version. One transaction terminally closes old-epoch lease authority, creates exactly one fresh non-aliasing physical epoch inside the same lineage with one canonical genesis `UNLEASED(0)`, publishes the edge, and monotonically advances `ExecutionLineageCurrentPhysicalRootSelector`. It creates no Run, changes no Run/Turn state and leaves the lineage subject, fingerprint, initial/current Run and every pre-existing canonical byte unchanged. Causal references to predecessor Runs, accepted actions, evidence and obligations are immutable observations only and are never copied as authority; existing original-stream ownership and no-retry proofs remain in force. Lookup, traversal, rebuild, dedup and every registry row resolve and compare the selector before work; stale old-epoch commands reject. The invariant is exactly one current physical epoch per stable execution lineage. Rival rollover, crash and lost-ack replay select one decision/edge/selector winner; changed reuse conflicts.

`WorkerAuthoritativeCommitRegistry` is a closed independently generated internal manifest over executable EventAppender commit reachability and authoritative record kinds. Each row binds exact command/handler path, owner, input DTO variant, resulting record kinds, one closed applicability variant, and the required transaction-local fence. `PRE_ROOT_SCHEDULER_DECISION` is permitted only for interval publication/replay. Physical-root and post-terminal work-epoch rollovers have distinct exact decision/edge/selector rows. Non-scheduler ordinary commands use `NON_SCHEDULER_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

The registry includes every worker-originated authoritative path, including response fan-out, Turn/tool/read/retry/reducer mutations, completion/terminalization, interval publication/materialization/replay, both epoch-rollover families, delivery/effect work, successor publication and post-terminal work claim/renew/takeover, resolver submission, original-obligation closure, permitted recovered-outcome publication and work closure. The post-terminal variant may publish only those exact closed record kinds for its bound member/stable work subject/current selected work epoch/obligation; stale selector, epoch or generation, physical-root mutation, evidence mutation, unrelated obligation or retry rejects. Build, startup and every runtime generation independently generate executable writer-path/record-kind/applicability reachability and require bidirectional equality; unknown, omitted, duplicate, extra, aliased, differently fenced, wrong-variant or raw paths reject. Independently authenticated evidence remains a separate non-worker lane and never borrows a work or physical-root lease.

### Response delivery

Final response delivery has its own stable identity and lifecycle after atomic semantic acceptance. The acceptance transaction durably publishes the complete validated delivery records/intents before `SUCCEEDED` is visible; asynchronous channel dispatch then advances each delivery lifecycle independently and cannot retroactively revoke accepted semantic success. Automatic retry requires channel idempotency or affirmative no-delivery evidence. An ambiguous Telegram outcome opens `RECONCILE_DELIVERY`; the system never reruns the Run or model to recreate the answer. CLI/status reads the stored accepted result, while channel status reports delivery convergence separately.

## Evaluation architecture

Evals run the exact production loop with injected clock, model, channels, tools, dashboard sources, effect adapters, and database. A separate eval-only orchestration loop is an architectural violation.

The unit of evaluation is a versioned scenario bundle containing:

- initial conversation, tenant, planning, policy, and external-system state;
- prompt artifact, model, tools, policy, budget, clock, and adapter configuration;
- expected intermediate trace invariants;
- allowed outcome set rather than one brittle expected string;
- deterministic, model, sandbox, and external-state graders as applicable;
- complete replayable trace, including `DashboardScreen` sequence and effect evidence.

The harness owns `arrange`, `observe`, and `assert_transition` for each external system. Grading covers internal invariants and real state transitions outside the loop.

Each scenario produces a versioned feature vector, not only a scalar score. Deterministic features are extracted directly from the trace or observed external state and include exact authority, identity, state, effect, and `agent-dashboard` transitions. Nondeterministic features are graded by LLM-as-a-judge over the full trajectory, including tool-call ordering (especially reads), model responses, and compliance with natural-language instructions. The judge prompt, rubric, model, and sampling configuration are bound artifacts.

A versioned verdict policy first fails on any deterministic hard gate, then applies critical-feature minima and an aggregate threshold to the nondeterministic scores. Judge scores and rationales remain in the canonical result; a weighted aggregate is secondary and supports experiment comparison only. A judge cannot forgive a deterministic failure, waive a product regression, or authorize release.

The `dev` profile runs one trajectory and one judge pass. The `promotion` profile runs at least three trajectories and two independently seeded judge passes per trajectory. Critical judge disagreement yields `INCONCLUSIVE`, which blocks promotion without claiming product failure. A rerun has a new eval identity and retains all prior samples; its sampling policy is immutable once execution begins.

## Architectural invariants

1. `VISION.md` is the sole product authority; implementation stages may defer but never redefine its contracts.
2. Tenant and principal identities are never merged, even in a one-owner deployment.
3. Only typed validated application commands append planning revisions.
4. Plan, Fact claims, candidate evidence, proposals, traces, and projections remain distinct stores/roles even when one database contains them.
5. Projections are disposable and rebuildable; provider state is evidence, not authority.
6. Approval binds an exact proposal and state head; stale authority fails closed.
7. The domain revision and effect intent commit atomically; provider execution never participates in that local transaction.
8. Unknown model, provider, or channel outcomes remain explicit and deny unsafe replay.
9. Production and eval execute one orchestration loop.
10. Every channel renders from the same committed query model and never overstates external success.
11. Version one initializes only an empty database/journal at the exact current genesis/schema/journal-schema/canonicalization/registry/companion-manifest tuple. Historical or mismatched stores enter `UNSUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION / HOLD_ADOPTION` before replay or admission; there is no synthesis, adoption or migration. Exact-current decided absent/prefix materialization may recover idempotently, while corrupt/non-prefix/incomplete-companion state holds for integrity.
12. Release remains `HOLD_ADOPTION` without the independent readiness and release records required by `VISION.md`.
13. The model has no secret or network capability; all model-visible state and tool schemas are tenant-scoped and policy-selected.
14. Each capability boundary is a hexagon: cross-module calls use public ports, and architecture tests reject private-package or adapter shortcuts.
15. A suspended Run has one immutable `SuspensionBaseline`; same-Run resume requires equality or a complete admissible monotone frontier-successor proof that causally accounts for every changed head using only baseline-permitted authenticated evidence and typed obligation disposition/closure events, closes obligations monotonically, and preserves all semantic, authority/policy, prompt/tool-schema, requested-work, and no-retry bindings exactly. Only a writer transaction that still observes the exact `SUSPENDED` head, byte-compares the recomputed complete frontier manifest and registry fingerprint, transaction-locally reclassifies `SAME_RUN`, and satisfies every activation-blocking frozen closure predicate may CAS-publish `ACTIVE`; every mismatch writes nothing and restarts classification. Any checkpoint remains a rebuildable, non-authoritative cursor.
16. Eval verdicts preserve their feature vectors; deterministic hard failures cannot be averaged away or forgiven by an LLM judge.
17. A scheduled occurrence is claimed once by stable revision-bound identity; restart does not reinterpret it from current time or schedule state.
