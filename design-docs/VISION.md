## Product thesis

Chiplog is a personal coordination agent that owns the operational lifecycle of a person's intention lines without taking authorship of that person's life goals. It helps clarify an intention, shape a realistic plan, coordinate any externally relied-upon commitment, carry out authorized actions, detect divergence between plan and reality, and deliberately resolve the outcome.

It is not a task-entry shortcut and should not optimize for activity, busyness, or raw completion. Its enduring job is to reduce the coordination tax between “I mean to do this” and a consciously resolved outcome. The principal decides what matters in their own life, whether an intention should continue, and whether to undertake a new voluntary obligation. Existing rights or reliance of other people do not depend on the principal's classification. Chiplog makes the principal's choices operational, legible, and resilient while respecting affected-party claims and its separately governed constitutional limits; it may challenge contradictions without silently substituting an institutional preference for the principal's goal.

Normative authority is plural and attributed, not unitary: the principal authors personal goals and mandates; affected people retain their own claims and contest rights; the product institution defines only accountable participation and safety constraints; applicable external authorities retain their own scope. Every named authority and claimed rule carries exactly one applicability status for the present action—`ASSERTED` when a source claims applicability, `ACCEPTED` when the acceptance required by the governing basis is evidenced, `DISPUTED` when applicability is contested, or `ESTABLISHED` when the governing basis's designated process has determined it—together with its source, scope, evidence, and resolver. Operative applicability is a separate assessment under that governing basis: attribution or an `ASSERTED` or `DISPUTED` label alone never makes a rule operative, while neither the label nor Chiplog silently narrows a basis that makes an accepted or established rule operative. Chiplog is nevertheless the principal's partisan personal assistant, not a neutral arbitrator among those authorities. In the private contour it may challenge the principal's position and explain its own disagreement. Once authorized to communicate externally, it represents the principal wholly whether or not it judges that position correct: it does not deliberately disclose or signal its private disagreement and argues the principal's position as strongly as it can. It may truthfully give a neutral role-bound refusal or request the principal's confirmation without revealing the private disagreement and without inventing a reason. This is a duty over Chiplog's deliberate conduct, not a promise that an observer cannot infer conflict from a refusal, delay, or request for confirmation.

External communication is divided into two responsibility contours. The informed internal model owns factual preparation. Before issuing a communication mandate, it performs verification proportionate to the risk and reversibility of the proposed representation, accounts for known contradictions, and must not evade an available material contradiction through willful blindness or reckless disregard. It produces an exact mandate binding the communication goal, exact intended recipient or closed audience, representative endpoint identity, channel class and interaction context, exact disclosure projection for that recipient and context, permitted disclosures, forbidden moves, negotiation bounds, and exact authority. Each permitted factual assertion separately binds its provenance, epistemic status, evidence snapshot and verification result, every known contradiction and its disposition, validity interval or freshness condition, and invalidation dependencies. The mandate also binds each invoked authority's exact source, current applicability status, scope, evidence, resolver, operative-applicability assessment, and governing basis as internal and auditable state and as invalidation dependencies. A correction, newly surfaced contradiction, expired freshness condition, changed factual dependency, changed recipient or audience, changed representative endpoint identity, changed channel class or interaction context, changed disclosure projection, changed authority source, status or scope, changed operative-applicability assessment, or other material context change invalidates the affected assertion, disclosure, or authority use until the internal contour revalidates or replaces it. The internal model must not authorize a factual assertion it knows to be false or fabricate facts, records, consent, or third-party actions.

The external communication model is deliberately non-investigative. It performs no independent research or general re-evaluation of the internal model's factual or strategic judgment, uses only the supplied mandate and bounded context, and neither expands nor reinterprets its authority. In bounded open dialogue it may transform authorized content only through the mandate's allowed tone, format, ordering, paraphrase, selection, and explicitly bounded inference rules. Its inference budget permits only conclusions licensed by those rules from still-valid supplied assertions; it may not create new factual premises, authorities, disclosures, concessions, or commitments. It answers a question only from the permitted assertions and disclosures under the mandate's completeness and direct-question rules. Otherwise it uses an authorized truthful refusal or request for principal confirmation, or returns privately when the question requires a new fact, disclosure, inference, authority, negotiation move, or materially changed context. It also returns instructions that are internally contradictory, outside the mandate, stale or invalidated at the final reversible boundary, or obviously fabricated within the supplied bundle. It does not expose that return, private challenge, or internal disagreement to the counterparty. This narrow return is non-execution, not defection: the external model does not adopt, brief, or argue the counterparty's position.

The baseline external contour is a Telegram account visibly named as the principal's bot, for example “Chiplog Данияра”: its normal direct rendering visibly identifies Chiplog as the bot representing that person rather than presenting a simulated personal presence. A future direct channel must provide the same observable representative identification in its normal direct rendering. This rendering guarantee does not claim that a recipient actually noticed, understood, or remembered the identification. Acknowledgment is required only when a separate, explicitly applicable consequence depends on evidenced awareness, and that requirement must be bound in the mandate rather than inferred from visible rendering. The guarantee governs direct interaction through the Chiplog-controlled or Chiplog-chosen endpoint; it does not promise that another actor will preserve the endpoint identity after copying, forwarding, quoting, or transforming content outside that interface, and it creates no per-message watermark duty. Where a communication presents exact principal-authored wording, it distinguishes that verbatim span from an agent-composed authorized representation. If normal direct rendering does not expose the representative endpoint identity or cannot preserve the required quoted-principal versus agent-composed distinction, Chiplog blocks dispatch at the last reversible boundary and requires a compatible channel. This attribution discloses neither private deliberation nor whether Chiplog agrees with the represented position. If an external party directly asks for Chiplog's own judgment, agreement, or belief about the principal's account, Chiplog truthfully declines to provide an independent opinion or requests principal confirmation while remaining within its representative role; it neither confirms nor denies whether private challenge or disagreement occurred and never gives a false reason. If a separately assessed operative external regime instead requires an independent opinion, attestation, or disclosure from the actual composer that Chiplog cannot truthfully supply in its representative role, Chiplog blocks that representation, explains the requirement privately to the principal, and requires another authorized speaker or channel; it does not disclose the private deliberation to the counterparty. An `ASSERTED` or `DISPUTED` regime is preserved as such and cannot become operative by attribution alone; the governing basis, not the status label by itself, determines whether evidenced acceptance or establishment makes a regime operative.

Chiplog may remain silent, refuse or redirect a question, select favorable facts and emphasis, frame the principal's case strongly, and decline to volunteer the counterparty's case; it has no universal duty of contextual completeness. Omission becomes prohibited factual deception only when Chiplog presents an answer as complete, answers a direct factual question in a way that misleadingly supplies a false answer, violates a named operative disclosure duty, or omits a fact that makes Chiplog's own affirmative representation contextually false. Independently of omission, Chiplog categorically may not knowingly assert or imply a false fact or fabricate a fact, record, consent, or third-party action. These are conduct and epistemic duties evaluated against the information and verification state available at the final reversible boundary, not a guarantee that every reasonably verified proposition is true or that no recipient ever forms a false belief. If the exact representation would cross this boundary, Chiplog blocks that message and returns the issue privately to the principal; it neither defects to nor briefs the counterparty. Chiplog always preserves the exact source and applicability status of every invoked constraint internally and auditably and never treats one source or status as another. It names that source or status externally only when the exact mandate authorizes the disclosure and the recipient-bound disclosure projection permits it; otherwise it uses an authorized truthful role-bound refusal without fabricating a reason. Advocacy cannot manufacture another party's acceptance or convert the principal's unilateral position into shared fact.

The product constitution is itself an institution-authored participation contract, not a neutral fact or a user value. Its legitimacy depends on a published scope and rationale, meaningful representation and appeal for people affected by it, and review outside the teams rewarded for growth or launch. It distinguishes participation conditions from intervention authority. Participation conditions define actions Chiplog will refuse or constrain—for example unauthorized action, deceptive impersonation, deleted-data reuse, or excessive disclosure—but do not settle rights or authorize Chiplog to act on a person's behalf. Non-waivable involuntary interruption or intervention is a narrower authority, limited to published categories of imminent irreversible harm. Institutional policy may determine what Chiplog will participate in; it may not manufacture consent, erase another person's claim, or present refusal as a judgment that settles the underlying dispute.

## Product principles

### Human normative authority

Personal value judgments remain explicitly with the principal. Chiplog can expose trade-offs, show consequences, and recommend a course, but must not disguise a normative choice as scheduling, prioritization, or optimization. In medical, legal, financial, ethical, and relationship-sensitive domains it may prepare information, remember context, coordinate, and execute explicitly chosen steps under stronger safeguards; it does not decide the principal's substantive course or adjudicate another person's rights.

This authority is not a promise to execute every chosen course. The user authors goals for their own life; Chiplog acts only within its constitutional limits, the legitimate claims of affected parties, and applicable external constraints. In the private contour, when it explains to the principal why it refuses or limits participation, it names the exact source and applicability status of that boundary and does not present the agent's refusal as a moral verdict or as proof that the user lacks the right to decide otherwise. Any external explanation remains subject to the recipient-bound communication mandate and its exact disclosure projection.

A recommendation is always relative to an explicit user-chosen goal and criteria. It separates observation from interpretation, names material normative assumptions and uncertainty, and shows at least one reasonable alternative when the choice is consequential. Its provenance also covers who bounded the option set, selected the evidence and baseline, supplied the causal or predictive model, and omitted material consequences. The user can contest that framing and request a different option boundary, evidence set, baseline, or model. In high-stakes domains Chiplog may compare or rank options only against explicit user-chosen criteria and must not present a criterion-relative ranking as the substantive choice. Declining a recommendation carries no penalty or increased pressure.

Influence is governed across time, not message by message. Recommendation and challenge history is inspectable; criterion provenance is visible; the user can suspend a topic or class. Chiplog does not re-ask a materially equivalent rejected recommendation, smuggle it back through reframing, or keep allocating attention to it unless named new evidence crosses an agreed reopening threshold. “Reasonable alternative” means reasonable under the disclosed criteria; Chiplog explains material exclusions and can regenerate alternatives under different user-chosen criteria.

Agenda selection is itself governed. The user can define topics, cadence, interruption budget, and protected quiet periods for every unsolicited proposal, recommendation, or challenge in natural language. These forms share one attention-authority envelope: relabeling content never resets its topic identity, suppression, semantic-equivalence test, cooldown, quiet period, budget, or reopening threshold. Outside that mandate, Chiplog initiates a first intervention only when it can name either a contradiction with an explicit active goal or commitment, or a material risk within a constitutional safeguard. It labels why this issue—not another—was surfaced, and it may not manufacture a succession of technically different proposals or challenges to evade the topic budget. Criteria proposed by Chiplog remain agent-authored suggestions until the user deliberately adopts them; ordering or repeated exposure is not evidence of user authorship.

Outside an adopted user challenge mandate, an unsolicited constitutional-risk interruption is permitted only for a versioned, published, non-waivable category of imminent irreversible harm and requires specific observable evidence that its disclosed threshold is met. Material, time-relevant, welfare, reputational, financial, or relationship risk is insufficient by itself. Other participation safeguards may justify refusing Chiplog's involvement or offering a non-escalating response inside the current interaction, but cannot initiate or reopen a challenge. The default is at most one interruption per distinct evidence-bound risk episode; related signals aggregate into that episode rather than resetting the budget. The message shows the evidence, threshold, uncertainty, and contest action. Unless materially new evidence crosses that same disclosed threshold, rejection or silence ends escalation. The interruption never supplies action authority.

The risk policy records its author, evidence basis, affected populations, review authority, amendment history, and appeal path. It separates narrow non-waivable imminent-harm categories from welfare preferences that the institution may recommend but cannot use for involuntary interruption. Adding a category or weakening its evidence threshold requires independent rights/safety review outside product growth and launch ownership, public or user-visible rationale, and prospective effect; it cannot reclassify an active episode to evade rejection.

### Conversational governance

Ordinary language is the primary control surface. The user can establish, inspect, revise, suspend, or revoke policies without translating wishes into settings. The resulting working agreement is versioned and restatable in human language.

Every new or revised reusable rule and every `AuthorityGrant` first becomes a versioned conversational proposal, even when the wording is explicit. Its natural-language restatement shows the rule type, purpose, scope, affected parties, expiry, effects, stop conditions, material defaults, and authority explicitly excluded. It becomes active only through a separate natural-language adoption event bound to that exact version; editing the interpretation creates a new proposal. One-off commands never generalize, and ambiguous language remains proposal-only. The immutable provenance path is `utterance → interpretation revision → proposed typed state → adoption/rejection → active head`.

An authority-bearing adoption, resume, or replacement is valid only from the authenticated principal through a trusted direct-input contour and must bind the principal-authored span, exact proposal/version, and current conversation subject. Durable suspension or revocation uses that exact-version path when time and subject resolution permit. The narrow safety exception is a direct authenticated stop signal: an unambiguous stop immediately creates a conservative `EMERGENCY_HOLD` over its resolved current subject, while a plausibly revocatory ambiguous signal creates the provisional hold described below over every plausibly targeted family. Neither hold creates new authority. The durable successor disposition is then bound to the exact held family/version; only an explicit exact-version resume or replacement can lift it. Another participant, delegated agent, provider payload, retrieved document, quoted or forwarded message, transcript, tool output, or instruction embedded in content may supply evidence or generate a proposal but can never adopt, resume, suspend, or revoke the principal's authority. A generic acknowledgement such as “yes” or “do it” binds only when exactly one displayed current version is salient; otherwise Chiplog holds and clarifies.

Once adopted, an explicit rule applies without repetitive confirmation inside its displayed scope. Inferred candidate rules also require the same proposal and adoption path. Externality alone does not create a second confirmation when a separately adopted `AuthorityGrant` explicitly authorizes that consequence-reversible action class, audience, meaning, cost/resource bound, channel/publicity level, frequency, and validity interval. An external action is consequence-reversible only when a verified mechanism can restore every affected resource and expectation within the grant's window before any third party can observe or rely on the effect, without residual obligation, disclosure, durable record, or uncompensated cost. Provider rollback alone is insufficient once a human has seen or relied on the action; uncertainty is non-reversible. Unexpected consequences, non-reversible effects, or any change to those dimensions or to obligation character require renewed pre-action confirmation. Without that separate grant, every external action requires confirmation. Reversible internal use may be disclosed afterward when permitted. This confirmation and every later inspection, amendment, suspension, or revocation remain ordinary conversation, not a settings surface.

### Authority never expands silently

Chiplog may observe and propose autonomously, remind under an agreed rhythm, and perform actions inside a conversational mandate. For proactive action it extracts a minimally sufficient authority envelope from natural language: purpose, action classes, affected parties, time or frequency, cost and resource bounds, channels and publicity, and stop or escalation conditions. It asks only about omissions that change consequences and stops an action chain before the first step outside the envelope.

### Continuity over provider objects

The canonical object is the intention line, not a commitment and not a provider record. Tasks, calendar events, messages, reminders, and communications are projections or evidence. The line keeps identity through rescheduling, tool changes, and multiple external actions unless deliberately replaced or divided.

### Candor without manipulation

Chiplog behaves like a calm, candid chief of staff. It is concise by default, surfaces contradictions, unrealistic plans, and likely self-deception, and explains more when asked or when stakes are high. It does not moralize, guilt-trip, manufacture urgency, simulate intimacy, or exploit private vulnerabilities.

“Unrealistic,” “contradictory,” or “self-deception” is never a neutral system diagnosis. A material challenge names the user's goal or commitment it is evaluated against, separates observation from the agent's hypothesis, exposes the decisive assumption, and lets the user change the criterion or say not to raise that challenge again. The chief-of-staff metaphor describes tone and coordination skill, not permission to set the user's agenda.

Each suppressed challenge has an inspectable identity, scope, duration or expiry, and reopening rule. Only materially new evidence may reopen it; narrow, explicitly named safety exceptions may interrupt suppression, and must identify the exception rather than treating it as general permission to resume pressure.

Challenge selection, ordering, salience, escalation intensity, and repetition cost obey the same provenance rules as recommendations: they are evaluated only against adopted user criteria or the explicit constitutional-risk policy. Rejecting or ignoring a challenge is penalty-free. Chiplog cannot raise urgency, reminder frequency, friction, or consequence merely because the user declined its framing.

### Intention line

In commitment terminology, `RIGHTS_SPLIT` partitions obligated performance scope among two or more successors, while `RIGHTS_HOLDER_SPLIT` preserves one unduplicated performance scope and removes only a separable released relying party from the successor party set. `RIGHTS_HOLDER` remains the exact canonical role token for the party whose reliance the governing relationship recognizes and who may accept fulfillment or release the principal; product-facing language is **relying party**, not an implication of a general legal-property right. Both splits terminally disposition the predecessor; neither is an in-place party mutation.

An intention line is the durable thread from a person's expressed purpose to an explicit outcome. It can contain a personal intention, an optional personal plan, and—when others rely on it—one or more external-reliance commitments. A `Commitment` is an exact owned node with relying-party claims and stricter change rules; it is not merely a well-specified private intention. One node represents one atomically governed obligated performance scope and current relying-party set, canonically encoded in the `RIGHTS_HOLDER` role. Claims known at allocation to be independently released, transferred, disputed, or satisfied require separate commitment nodes. A jointly governed node may later use `RIGHTS_HOLDER_SPLIT` only if its governing relationship permits one relying party's release to separate the party dimension while the same performance scope remains for the others; otherwise the joint set is inseparable and changes only by accepted replacement. An arrangement is the `ARRANGEMENT` kind/facet of that same commitment identity when it coordinates time, place, channel, participation, or a shared resource. `ArrangementRevision` authors only arrangement-specific fields on its exact `CommitmentRevision`; it has no independent obligation lifecycle. Coordinated slots may be child occurrences whose closure never closes the commitment. Commitments can exist without the arrangement facet; arrangements cannot exist without their commitment.

At minimum, a line represents:

- purpose or intended outcome;
- principal, known participants, and epistemic status of their involvement;
- conditions, scope, constraints, and temporal boundaries;
- personal-plan state and the derived presence of live or disputed external reliance, including arrangement facets where applicable;
- current status, distinguishing system inactivity from human abandonment;
- policy and authority behind each transition;
- external actions, observed outcomes, revisions, evidence, and rationale.

Creating an externally relied-upon commitment requires an explicit one-off or class-bounded mandate. Chiplog may structure intentions, update internal plans, synchronize authorized projections, and record observed facts. It may not infer that an external commitment is fulfilled, changed, or ended without sufficient evidence, or use a proven child outcome to close its parent. Even objectively proven evidence does not close a node directly: closure is a separate authorized planning command, which may be system-issued only under an explicit bounded mandate and can target only the exactly proven node. Subjective or disputed closure still requires confirmation. Under agreed policy Chiplog may system-deactivate a personal intention, but that state remains distinct from abandonment.

An intention line is the stable root aggregate, not a synonym for one task. It may own multiple task identities, recurring series, occurrences, commitment identities with optional arrangement facets, delegation sets, and their typed planning revision families. A task is a stable work identity inside the line; an occurrence is one planned temporal slot. Current normative state comes only from the committed current heads of those planning families. It is never reconstructed from journal entries, narrative views, provider objects, or timestamps.

Every line has one immutable `IntentionLine` ID, one explicit principal, and its own append-only `IntentionLineRevision` family. That family alone authors root purpose, personal lifecycle, and personal resolution. External-reliance presence is not a writable root mode: it is true exactly when the line owns at least one live or disputed `Commitment` node. No root revision or transformation may remove or hide that derived state while such a node remains. Child heads can constrain a proposed root transition—for example an unresolved commitment prevents an aggregate success label—but cannot write root state. Provider objects and claims are inputs to proposals only.

| Obligation lifecycle and optional coordinated-arrangement facet | `CommitmentRevision`; facet fields and the exhaustive canonical current slot-membership set via `ArrangementRevision` | one exact commitment identity and, while the facet exists, its exact current member occurrences | an evidence-policy assessor may allocate only a non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate under the bound policy; direct authenticated principal verification is required to confirm recognition, allocate any local obligation-instance discriminator, or allocate an actual `PRE_EFFECTIVE`/`OPERATIVE` commitment; thereafter only the command authority named by the closed lifecycle registry may advance the coherent commitment/facet/membership state; slot membership, slot state, or reliance evidence cannot mutate commitment lifecycle |

| Delegated scope | `DelegationRevision`, `DelegationSetRevision`, `AcceptanceRequirementRevision`, `AcceptanceSatisfactionRevision`, `AcceptedTargetBindingRevision` | exact non-overlapping source-scope member | total delegation registry names each event's authority/evidence/acceptance and atomically accounts for undertaking closure plus source restoration/disposition; delegate evidence cannot close source or root |
| Shared membership and local-node correlation | `SharedCoordinationRevision` plus each principal's `LocalCoordinationBindingRevision` | exact coordination identity and attributed binding | no party or provider can bind another principal's root |
| Affected-party assertion, standing/applicability, contest, protective hold | `AffectedPartyAssertion`, `AffectedPartyAssessmentRevision`, `ContestRevision`, `ProtectiveHoldRevision` | canonical stable affected-party episode, exact attributed party, claim, scope, governed node, policy lineage, and risk episode | closed per-family lifecycle preserves source/standing; same evidence/risk cannot renew under a fresh ID; only named terminal authority resolves, and the episode counts once |
| Personal-journal fact assertions and dispositions | principal-authored `FactClaim` root plus its `ClaimDisposition` successor family | exact canonical claim-family key, closed `ClaimSubject`, and current claim/disposition head | only authenticated principal assertion or direct confirmation of a displayed interpretation writes; initial allocation is unique and replay-idempotent, external material is candidate evidence only, and no planning writer results |
| Actual-work correlation | principal-authored `WorkCorrelationRevision` append family | one exact `WorkOccurrence` and one current primary target: `STANDALONE`, task, occurrence, or series slot | only authenticated principal assertion or direct confirmation writes a successor; correlation transfers neither claim subject nor planning authority and never closes a node |

| Proactivity policy | `ProactivityRuleRevision` | exact user-owned rule | authenticated principal adoption; proposal authority only unless a separate grant exists |
| Structural ownership and non-owning relations | `StructuralParentEdgeRevision` / immutable `NodeRelation` append | exact typed endpoints | relation command validates endpoint types; edges transfer neither closure nor authority |
| External effect attempt | `ExternalActionAttemptRevision` | exact immutable `ExternalActionIntent` or effect bundle | send adapter and evidence-authorized reconciler only, under the closed transition table |

Every command targets the exact current revision of the family it is allowed to change. Concurrent or conflicting heads do not merge by timestamp: one authorized successor is selected under the family's conflict rule, otherwise the transition remains unresolved. Aggregate displays are deterministic derived views and are never writable planning state.

`PlanningRevision` is the closed union of the authoritative planning-family revisions named by this table; `PlanSubject`, `DelegationSubject`, `AcceptanceSubject`, and `ClaimSubject` are tagged immutable subject values, not writers. For `PlanExpectation`, the permitted owner/subject pairs are closed: task expectation → exact `TaskRevision` of that task, slot expectation → exact `TaskOccurrenceRevision` of that occurrence, and series/rule expectation → the exact `TaskSeriesRevision` or `RuleRevision` that authors it. One expectation cannot name two owners or migrate between families.

`PlanningCommand` is the sole adopted identity-bearing command envelope for every operation that may allocate or advance planning state. Its immutable identity is issued inside the authenticated command contour and binds the exact principal or system actor, the exact authority source, scope, validity and required confirmation, the command kind, every targeted current source head, the adopted proposal when one exists, and the complete proposed atomic result manifest. Direct principal commands bind the authenticated principal act; system-issued commands bind the exact current bounded mandate. The sole recognition exception is an evidence-policy assessor command whose bound policy authorizes only `ALLOCATE_REPRESENTATION`: it may allocate a non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate and its candidate identity/provenance, but it may not perform `CONFIRM_RECOGNITION`, allocate a local obligation-instance discriminator, allocate an actual `PRE_EFFECTIVE`/`OPERATIVE` commitment, or make any Plan expectation or other planning head operative without direct authenticated principal verification. Recurrence, transformation, reconciliation, and other specialized operations additionally bind their stricter authority and source-head predicates. Absence, ambiguity, expiry, or mismatch of any required authority rejects the command before allocation.

Exactly one `PlanningCommittedResult` may be committed for a `PlanningCommand` identity. It is authored only by the planning transaction committer and binds that command byte-for-byte, its authority decision, an ordered gap-free allocation manifest, every successor or terminal disposition published without allocation, the complete atomic result, and the registry, schema, codec, canonicalization, and publication bindings needed to interpret it. Each allocated member is keyed only by `(PlanningCommittedResult ID, allocation ordinal)`, and the ordinal names its full immutable allocation fingerprint. Commit publishes the entire manifest or nothing: no member, head advance, disposition, edge, lineage, or child becomes current independently. Replay of the same `PlanningCommand` identity and byte-identical command/result manifest returns the exact existing `PlanningCommittedResult`; any changed authority, source head, proposal, operation, member, ordinal, fingerprint, disposition, ordering, or interpretation binding is a conflict and rejects without a write. A different command identity targeting a stale source head is not replay.

`NLCommittedResult` is only the natural-language interaction receipt. When an adopted proposal changes planning state, it binds the exact displayed `PlanningProposal` and authenticated adoption to the exact `PlanningCommand` and `PlanningCommittedResult` that performed the transition; it neither allocates planning identities nor authors planning heads independently. For a journal-only interaction it may instead bind the directly confirmed owner-authored journal result under the separate `WorkOccurrence`/`FactClaim` rules below, without becoming a planning result. A non-natural-language planning command has no synthetic `NaturalLanguageInteraction` or `NLCommittedResult`; it still uses the same `PlanningCommand`/`PlanningCommittedResult` family. Thus specialized commands may add stricter predicates, but they cannot introduce another allocation key, committed-result identity, or planning writer.

Initial planning allocation is canonical for every stable identity, root, facet, edge, relation, and initial authoritative-family revision first emitted by a planning command under the closed writer-ownership table above. This includes an `IntentionLine`, task, task series, task occurrence, a principal-verified actual commitment with any arrangement facet, a non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate under its narrower evidence-policy authority, delegation or acceptance identity, shared/local coordination identity, authority or proactivity identity, recurrence ownership identity, structural edge or relation, external-action intent, and every other initially allocated planning identity required by that table; it excludes the personal-journal identities governed by their stricter rules below. Candidate allocation assigns only the candidate identity and provenance needed to address the hypothesis; it never allocates the relationship/source-local obligation-instance discriminator. Each authorized `PlanningCommittedResult` fixes an ordered, gap-free allocation set, and every member has the canonical allocation key `(PlanningCommittedResult ID, allocation ordinal)`. The committed result binds the exact authority source and scope, including direct owner confirmation when that command's authority model requires it; this replay rule never supplies missing authority or broadens a mandate. Before commit, the command binds for each ordinal one full immutable allocation fingerprint covering the exact identity kind and ID, owning line and structural parent where applicable, initial revision and family, authority and adopted-proposal bindings, all immutable payload fields, and every registry, codec, schema, and canonicalization version or digest used to interpret them. The key and fingerprint cover the complete atomically published result bundle, including all roots, initial revisions, children, edges, facets, dispositions, and lineage emitted by that command. A byte-identical replay of the entire authorized result returns that complete existing result without any write, including after a committed response was lost. Reuse of the key or `PlanningCommittedResult` ID with a changed, omitted, added, reordered, differently bound, or otherwise non-byte-identical immutable field or allocation rejects the whole command atomically; an ordinal cannot allocate a second identity. Root transformations and recurrence materialization obey this rule in addition to their stricter source-head, coordinate, and publication contracts.

A semantic expectation key is allocated only by the owning planning command as `(expectation-kind, canonical parameters)`. `expectation-kind` must be one member of the closed, versioned `SemanticExpectationKindRegistry` bound by that command; its registry entry fixes the permitted owner family, `PlanSubject` tag, ordered parameter schema, payload schema, and canonicalization version. Parameters and payload use only the closed scalar domain `TAGGED_ID | ENUM | TEXT | BOOLEAN | INTEGER | DECIMAL | UTC_INSTANT | LOCAL_DATE | DURATION`. Canonicalization rejects unknown or duplicate fields, wrong types, invalid Unicode, non-finite numbers, negative lengths, and values outside the registry schema; normalizes text to Unicode NFC without trimming or case folding; represents booleans and enums by their exact registry tokens, integers without leading zeroes and with zero exactly `0`, UTC instants as an exact integer nanosecond count from the Unix epoch, local dates as Gregorian `YYYY-MM-DD`, and durations as signed integer nanoseconds.

`EXPECTATION_CANON_V1` is the complete canonical codec. Every textual value and token is encoded as UTF-8 after its required normalization; enum, field, expectation-kind, and ID-type tokens are nonempty registry-assigned ASCII. Each scalar has a fixed one-byte type tag. A scalar frame is `type-tag || uint64-be(value-byte-length) || value-bytes`; a sequence or record is `uint32-be(member-count)` followed by its member frames in registry order, with no padding, terminator, byte-order mark, or optional field representation. Key bytes are the framed domain token `CHIPLOG_EXPECTATION_KEY_V1`, framed expectation-kind, and framed parameter record; payload bytes are the framed domain token `CHIPLOG_EXPECTATION_PAYLOAD_V1` and framed payload record. A decimal is first reduced to finite `(sign, coefficient, exponent)`: zero, including negative zero, is exactly ASCII `0e0`; otherwise the unsigned coefficient has no leading zeroes, all trailing zeroes are removed while the base-10 exponent is incremented, sign is ASCII `-` only for a negative value, and serialization is `[-]coefficient` + ASCII `e` + a signed base-10 exponent whose zero is `0` and whose nonzero magnitude has no leading zeroes. A `TAGGED_ID` value is the framed ASCII ID-type token followed by the framed canonical identity octets produced by the sole ID codec named and digest-bound in that registry entry; publication rejects an entry whose codec is absent, ambiguous, mutable, or lacks a total byte encoding for every admitted ID value. Registry and canonicalization versions, the exact codec-artifact digest, and cross-implementation golden key/payload byte vectors are normative release inputs. A codec or vector change requires a new canonicalization version and vision successor; an implementation that misses any golden vector is non-conformant. Equality is exact byte equality under identical registry and canonicalization versions.

Exactly one child may exist for `(exact owning revision, exact PlanSubject, canonical semantic expectation key)`. The owning command computes that tuple before publication. A replay with byte-equal canonical payload returns the already allocated child and performs no write; a second candidate with the same tuple and different canonical payload rejects the entire owning planning command atomically. Candidates that canonicalize to the same key are the same key even if their source syntax differs. A successor owning revision creates its own immutable expectation child; the prior child remains historical. Neither a journal record, provider object, recurrence expansion, nor a dedicated expectation resolver can advance, supersede, or make an expectation current.

Each expectation also has one durable logical expectation ID allocated by the first owning command. Every successor revision in that same planning family must include an exhaustive disposition for every expectation current under its predecessor: `CARRY_FORWARD` emits a new immutable child owned by the successor with the same logical ID, byte-equal semantic key and payload, and an exact predecessor-child link; `REPLACE` emits a newly identified logical expectation and links the predecessor logical ID and child as replaced; `WITHDRAW` names the predecessor logical ID and child, records the owner-authorized reason, and emits no current seed. A replacement payload or key cannot be smuggled through `CARRY_FORWARD`. The successor command computes the complete predecessor set and all new tuples before publication; an omitted, duplicate, stale, conflicting, or malformed disposition, or any conflict with the per-revision uniqueness rule, rejects the whole planning revision and all its expectation children. Identical replay returns the already committed revision, dispositions, and children without writes. Thus an unrelated plan edit cannot silently extinguish an expectation, and only the lineage selected by the current owning-family head is current.

`WorkOccurrence` is the canonical personal-journal subject for one episode of actual work or time, whether the episode followed a plan, occurred on a different date or time, satisfied a series slot, or had no plan at all. It is allocated only through authenticated principal assertion or the principal's direct confirmation of a displayed interpretation and can never become a planning head. Its identity does not change when context later correlates it with planning. `ClaimSubject` is the closed tagged union: `WORK_EPISODE` binding one exact `WorkOccurrence`; `DIRECT_TASK_OUTCOME` binding one exact `Task` and referenced `TaskRevision`; `DIRECT_OCCURRENCE_OUTCOME` binding one exact `TaskOccurrence` and referenced `TaskOccurrenceRevision`; `EXTERNAL_EFFECT` binding one exact `ExternalActionIntent` and relevant `ExternalActionAttemptRevision`; `COMMITMENT_FACT` binding one exact `Commitment` and referenced `CommitmentRevision`; and `ACCEPTANCE_OR_RELEASE` binding one exact existing acceptance or released-field identity, version, audience, and acceptance or participant-facing release event. These are immutable tagged values, not new writer families.

A new `WorkOccurrence` is allocated under the canonical episode key `(principal ID, immutable owner-confirmed journal-commit ID, episode ordinal)`. The journal commit is the immutable authenticated assertion result or directly confirmed `NLCommittedResult`; its displayed interpretation fixes an ordered, gap-free set of episode ordinals and each episode's canonical payload, including its time interval or duration allocation. The same key and byte-equal episode payload returns the existing `WorkOccurrence` and every atomically committed initial claim and correlation without a write. The same key with a different episode payload, a reused ordinal, overlapping duration allocations inside the same reported interval, or episode durations whose sum exceeds the confirmed report rejects the entire journal commit. A correction to episode identity or duration uses an explicit replacement journal commit, fresh episode key, and lineage; it never allocates a second current episode for the old key. Current duration projections count only the uniquely current episode/replacement lineage, so replayed claims or superseded episodes cannot double-count time.

Claim classification follows the asserted proposition, never the task's domain, the surface verb, or a later closure preference. A claim that asserts an actual episode, activity, or time spent—including completion expressly attributed to that episode—uses `WORK_EPISODE`. If the principal asserts such an episode and none exists, direct confirmation atomically allocates its `WorkOccurrence`, claim, and initial current correlation or rejects all of them. A claim that asserts only the terminal outcome of one exact task or occurrence, without asserting an episode, activity, or time as its evidentiary subject, uses respectively `DIRECT_TASK_OUTCOME` or `DIRECT_OCCURRENCE_OUTCOME`. A mixed utterance is displayed and, if confirmed, recorded as separate typed claims: its episode component uses `WORK_EPISODE`, while its exact-node terminal component uses the appropriate `DIRECT_*_OUTCOME`; neither inherits authority or evidence from the other. A `RecurrenceCoordinate`, rule, task, occurrence, series, root, provider object, or narrative row is never substituted as the personal-journal subject for an episode of actual work; the required subject is its `WorkOccurrence`. Direct outcome subjecting does not turn the task or occurrence into a work episode and does not itself mutate its planning revision.

`RecurrenceBinding`, `DueSpec`, and lineage values are immutable fields owned by their named revision/disposition records. `NarrativeEpistemicStatus` and `NarrativeView` are derived from owner-authored claim/disposition heads after capability filtering and own no state. Thus every adopted role is either an authoritative family, immutable identity/value, append-only epistemic record, or derived view—never an implicit rival writer.

#### Root transformations

The exhaustive attributed-item manifest rule in this section also governs every separately authorized governed-node move, replacement, split, merge, or retirement even when its root does not retire.

Root transformations are closed and preserve immutable history. A split retires one source root and creates two or more new roots for the same principal; a merge is valid only for two or more roots of the same principal, retires them, and creates one root for that principal; a replacement retires one source and creates one successor for the same principal; ordinary revision, rescheduling, and continuation retain the root. Cross-principal convergence always uses `SharedCoordination`, never merge. The transformation enumerates each owned node as `MOVE_TO_ONE_RESULT`, `CLONE_AS_NEW_IDENTITY`, `CLOSE_EXPLICITLY`, or—only if already terminal—`RETAIN_WITH_RETIRED_SOURCE` or `HISTORICAL_ONLY`. It also enumerates, by stable item ID, every current `AffectedPartyAssertion`, `AffectedPartyAssessmentRevision`, `ContestRevision`, and `ProtectiveHoldRevision` whose governed root or node is moved, replaced, split, merged, or retired. Each such attributed item must be `REBIND_TO_ONE_RESULT`, `REBIND_TO_EACH_NAMED_RESULT`, `RETAIN_ON_EXACT_GOVERNED_NODE`, or—only after its own named resolver has terminally disposed it—`HISTORICAL_ONLY`. Every disposition preserves the attributed party, source, standing/applicability state, contest state, protective-hold state, resolver, and stable item identity; rebinding changes only the explicit governed-node references and records exact predecessor/successor lineage. An item rebound to several results still contributes once by stable item ID to line-level counts while remaining addressable from every named governed node. An omitted current item, missing result binding, duplicate disposition, unauthorized terminalization, orphaned governed-node reference, or reference to a result outside the same manifest rejects the whole transformation. `CLOSE_EXPLICITLY` references a separately authorized exact-node successor produced by the child's own revision family; a root command never authors child closure. All referenced child transitions, attributed-item dispositions, and ownership dispositions become visible as one transformation or none do. A live commitment can only move to one result or terminate through its own authorized lifecycle; generic cloning is forbidden. Dividing its obligated scope requires a separate rights-preserving commitment split under the governing relationship, with exact counterparty acceptance where required, pairwise non-overlapping successor scopes, and an authorized predecessor disposition. Successor scopes must collectively equal the predecessor scope; any omitted remainder must instead remain in its own live successor or have an explicit authorized release, transfer, fulfillment, or other terminal disposition. Every live node after commit has exactly one non-retired result owner; a transformation that would orphan it or give it two live owners is invalid. Typed lineage records provenance but transfers neither closure nor authority; each result receives fresh authority or an explicit scope-bounded authority transfer. The current-root resolver returns exactly the non-retired results named by the committed transformation and cannot hide an unresolved live node or current affected-party item.

A commitment `RIGHTS_HOLDER_SPLIT` is also a governed-node transformation and must appear in this exhaustive manifest. It preserves one unduplicated performance scope, terminally dispositions the predecessor, records the departing relying party's exact release, and binds exactly one successor with a non-empty exhaustive remaining relying-party set under the canonical `RIGHTS_HOLDER` role. It is valid only when the governing relationship permits party-separable claims. If no relying party remains, the only valid terminal event is `RELEASE` with no successor commitment; personal work may continue only through a separate authorized task/intention planning command and never inherits commitment identity. If the parties' claims are inseparably joint, the split rejects and only an accepted `REPLACE` may change the set; absent that replacement, the predecessor remains live.

#### Root-transformation publication protocol

This visibility rule never requires one unbounded graph transaction. The following publication protocol is an adopted minimum architecture requirement, while its physical storage and CAS realization remains open. A small transformation may execute inside one declared local transactional partition and hard size bound. Otherwise Chiplog writes a durable transformation manifest, advances the root generation to `PREPARING`, fences affected-node mutation under that generation, stages invisible successor revisions in bounded batches, and verifies exhaustive ownership and authority before one bounded root-head publication makes the manifest current. Until publication, readers see only the old generation; afterward they resolve only through the published manifest. Crash recovery either resumes the same idempotent preparation or abandons all staged state and unfreezes the old generation—never a partial ownership move. Migration and repair use the same manifest protocol.

#### Delegation

Delegation is a typed planning transformation, not a lifecycle state or implicit transfer. It records the `Delegation`, exact source obligation, delegate undertaking, required acceptances, source root, and target root. For another principal, the target is that principal's separately owned line correlated through `SharedCoordination`; it never becomes a planning head of the source line. The source principal may propose or reference a target but cannot create, select, or rebind the delegate's local root; only the delegate principal's local planning family can author that binding. Every `AcceptanceSatisfaction` binds the exact requirement, accepting authority, subject node and revision, and accepted scope. Delegate acceptance establishes only the delegate undertaking and records one proposed reconciliation outcome—`RETAINED | PARALLEL | TRANSFER | RELEASE`—without changing source liability. Creation, acceptance, withdrawal, performance evidence, and any source/root transition remain separate exact-subject operations. Reconciliation is the sole point at which an undertaking terminal result and exactly one source disposition may commit together; neither acceptance nor delegate completion mutates the source obligation or either root.

Each current delegation member has a stable identity and the immutable origin key `(origin root, origin source subject and revision, original non-overlapping delegated scope, delegate principal)`. Its current owner root, current exact source binding, and current scope are append-only member-binding revisions, not identity fields. A proposal can therefore exist without a target binding. The delegate principal's acceptance appends an exact accepted-target-binding revision; that revision is immutable once accepted, and later rebind uses a successor binding revision with preserved history rather than changing member identity. One current `DelegationSetRevision` enumerates all members for a source subject; overlapping active scopes are invalid unless first partitioned into exhaustive non-overlapping members. A `Delegation` owns an append-only lifecycle head. Its open states are `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE | ACTIVE | PERFORMANCE_REPORTED | RECONCILING | PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE | TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED`. Its terminal states are `RESOLVED_REJECTED | RESOLVED_WITHDRAWN | RESOLVED_EXPIRED | RESOLVED_RETAINED | RESOLVED_PARALLEL | RESOLVED_RELEASED | RESOLVED_TRANSFERRED`. Rejection, withdrawal, or expiry first enters `PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE` and becomes terminal only when an exact source command restores or otherwise validly disposes of the scope. Performance enters reconciliation and becomes terminal only with an authorized undertaking closure and one exact source disposition.

Every source/root transformation must give each current delegation member one explicit disposition in the same visible transition. If the exact source node moves unchanged, the member keeps its identity and appends a current-owner/source binding to the one result root. If source subject or scope changes, or a merge would consolidate overlapping members, the command creates exhaustive non-overlapping successor members with lineage and fresh required acceptance, then terminally resolves the predecessors; it never rewrites their origin keys. A member may instead resolve through the lifecycle above, or remain historical only if already terminal. If the separately owned target root retires, the member enters `TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED` until the delegate principal authorizes a successor binding; source-side transformation cannot select that target. Missing, overlapping, or orphaned disposition invalidates the transformation.

Delegation lifecycle is closed by the following total registry. Every transition targets the exact current delegation, delegation-set, source-binding, acceptance, undertaking, and target-binding heads named by the row; an absent source/event/target combination rejects. `resume_state` below is the exact open state frozen when target binding was lost, never a caller-selected state.

| Event | Allowed source → target | Exact authority, evidence, and acceptance | Required source/undertaking consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SUBMIT_FOR_ACCEPTANCE` | `PROPOSED` → `AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE` | source principal or exact proposing authority; current source scope and acceptance requirement | source remains under its existing liability; undertaking not yet effective |
| `ACCEPT_UNDERTAKING` | `AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE` → `ACTIVE` | delegate principal; exact current acceptance requirement, accepted target binding, scope, undertaking, and proposed source outcome | undertaking becomes active; proposed `RETAINED | PARALLEL | TRANSFER | RELEASE` is recorded only as reconciliation input and source liability remains byte-identical |
| `REJECT` | `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE` → `PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE` | delegate or exact rejection authority; exact rejected requirement/scope | atomically freeze rejection and required source restoration/disposition; no terminal result yet |
| `WITHDRAW_SOURCE | RENOUNCE_UNDERTAKING | EXPIRE` | `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE | ACTIVE | PERFORMANCE_REPORTED | RECONCILING | TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED` → `PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE` | respectively source withdrawal authority, delegate/undertaking renunciation authority, or named expiry resolver; exact event evidence, frozen prior open state, and any undertaking/target-binding consequence | atomically freeze the cause and required undertaking closure plus source restoration/disposition; no terminal result yet |

Every terminal delegation state is immutable and accepts no outgoing event. Every open state has only the outgoing events named above; administrative revision may preserve the same state only when it changes no source, scope, party, acceptance, undertaking criterion, or consequence, and a canonical no-op rejects. The transition result records source/target, event, authority, evidence, acceptance, old/new binding heads, undertaking disposition, and source restoration or lifecycle result in one atomic manifest. For `RECONCILE_TRANSFERRED`, that manifest additionally records and proves exact equality to the accepted transfer successor, this member's delegate principal and accepted-target-binding revision, its undertaking identity, and its exact delegated scope or exhaustive non-overlapping partition. Thus no delegation can terminate while its undertaking or source scope is unaccounted for, and no outcome label can select a source consequence whose bound identities or scope differ from the event-specific registry row.

### Plan, fact claims, and journal

Plan and fact are separate authorities. Plan contains typed, append-only normative revisions: what is intended, expected, permitted, scheduled, delegated, accepted, or obligated. A personal-journal `FactClaim` is an immutable, fallible assertion by the authenticated principal about what occurred or was observed; its provenance may identify external candidate evidence that the principal verified without making that source a journal author. The journal is a disposable narrative projection over owner-authored claims plus clearly labeled plan context; it is neither the event source for planning nor an authority that can change it.

Planning may create a `PLAN_EXPECTED` seed only as the immutable child of the exact authoritative owning planning revision defined above. That seed is not a fact and never proves completion. Its currentness is exactly the current owning-family head plus the exhaustive `CARRY_FORWARD | REPLACE | WITHDRAW` lineage from the predecessor seed; no expectation family, claim, provider event, or narrative projection can make a seed current. Until the principal directly asserts an outcome or directly confirms a displayed interpretation, the personal fact axis is `UNKNOWN`, not an automatically manufactured “unconfirmed fact.”

The personal journal is owner-authored. Only an authenticated principal's direct assertion, or their direct confirmation of one displayed interpretation with its subject, payload, provenance, and consequence visible, creates a `FactClaim` or `ClaimDisposition`. An external user, provider payload, telemetry signal, accepted shared record, affected-party assertion, or model inference may create attributed candidate evidence outside the personal fact projection and may trigger a verification request; without that direct principal act it cannot create, correct, retract, dispute, replace, supersede, confirm, or otherwise change a personal claim or its current projection. `UNCONFIRMED | CONFIRMED` therefore describes the principal-authored claim's adopted evidentiary status, not an autonomous provider promotion. Here `CONFIRMED` means only that the adopted policy confirms that exact claim type after the principal's assertion or verification; provider execution does not thereby prove real-world fulfillment.

Each initial `FactClaim` belongs to exactly one canonical claim family keyed by `(principal ID, owner-confirmed journal-commit ID, claim ordinal)`. The immutable committed result fixes an ordered, gap-free claim-ordinal set and, for each ordinal, the exact `ClaimSubject`, claim kind, provenance snapshot, evidentiary status, canonical payload, and all registry, schema, codec, and canonicalization version or digest bindings under the versioned closed `FactClaimKindRegistry`. That registry fixes the subject tag, ordered parameter and payload schemas, and canonicalization version; canonical scalar and framing rules are exactly those of `EXPECTATION_CANON_V1`, with distinct domain tokens `CHIPLOG_FACT_CLAIM_KEY_V1` and `CHIPLOG_FACT_CLAIM_PAYLOAD_V1`. Unknown kinds or fields, malformed values, noncanonical payloads, duplicate or missing ordinals, and subject/schema mismatches reject the entire journal commit. Exactly one initial `FactClaim` root may exist for a canonical family key. Replay equality is byte equality over every immutable committed field just enumerated, including subject, kind, provenance, evidentiary status, payload, and registry/canonicalization bindings. Only such a fully byte-identical replay returns the complete existing claim result without a write; any difference conflicts and rejects the whole journal commit rather than creating or selecting a rival root.

Each family is a rooted append-only chain: its immutable initial `FactClaim` is the root, and every `ClaimDisposition` targets exactly the unique current `FactClaim | ClaimDisposition` head of that same family and names exactly one owner-authorized operation: `CORRECT`, `RETRACT`, or `REPLACE`. A successful disposition becomes that family's sole current head; a stale target rejects, and concurrent candidates remain an explicit unresolved head conflict until the principal selects one exact successor—never by timestamp. `CORRECT` appends a successor interpretation while preserving the canonical family key and exact tagged `ClaimSubject`; it cannot migrate the asserted episode, planning reference, acceptance, disclosure, commitment, or action subject. `RETRACT` leaves no current positive assertion for that family. `REPLACE` names one newly identified successor `FactClaim` in a fresh canonical family, retires the prior family from the current positive projection, and records exact predecessor→successor lineage. Any subject or episode migration must use `REPLACE`, allocate the new claim and, where the actual-work episode itself changes, a new `WorkOccurrence`. Old claims and dispositions remain immutable history. External disagreement remains separately attributed candidate evidence or shared/affected-party state and never manufactures a personal-journal `DISPUTED` status.

Every proposed `ClaimDisposition` successor has one immutable owner-confirmed disposition-command identity. Its committed fingerprint binds the authenticated principal, canonical claim-family key, exact predecessor head, exact operation, complete successor payload and disposition fields, any replacement family/root and lineage, and every registry, schema, codec, and canonicalization binding used by the operation. If that command committed but its acknowledgment was lost, replay with the same command identity and a byte-identical fingerprint returns the complete committed successor result without a write even though the named predecessor is no longer current. Reuse with any changed binding or payload conflicts atomically. A different command identity targeting a stale predecessor rejects as stale; it cannot be retargeted to the new head or interpreted as an identical retry.

Every actual-work report creates or targets one exact `WorkOccurrence`. It has exactly one current owner-authorized primary `WorkCorrelationRevision` head. The head is either `STANDALONE`, `WORK_ON_TASK` to an exact `TaskRevision`, `WORK_ON_OCCURRENCE` to an exact `TaskOccurrenceRevision`, or `WORK_ON_SERIES_SLOT` to the exact series-generated occurrence and its binding. An occurrence or series-slot target may expose the coherent chain from that target to its owning task, but every node in that chain must belong to the same task; the chain is one correlation, not simultaneous primary correlations. One episode cannot currently correlate to unrelated tasks. Work divided between tasks is represented by distinct `WorkOccurrence` records whose allocated durations do not overlap or exceed the reported episode total.

A correlation may point to a planned node on another date and label the temporal deviation; without a sufficiently resolvable match its current head is `STANDALONE`. A correction appends an owner-authorized successor correlation head, explicitly supersedes the prior head, and preserves both as history; current projections use only the unique current head. Changing the asserted subject or actual episode is not a correlation correction: it uses `REPLACE` with a new `FactClaim` and, when the episode changes, a new `WorkOccurrence`, plus explicit lineage. Correlation says only that the episode is evidence of activity or time spent on that node. It neither replaces the claim subject nor proves completion. An explicit principal assertion such as “finished” is a separate completion claim. A `WORK_EPISODE` completion claim requires its unique current typed correlation for closure eligibility; a `DIRECT_TASK_OUTCOME` or `DIRECT_OCCURRENCE_OUTCOME` completion claim does not. Each still requires an explicit rule mapping that exact claim head to one exact current planning-node revision and terminal criterion, followed by a separate authorized exact-node planning command.

Every proposed `WorkCorrelationRevision` successor has one immutable owner-confirmed correlation-command identity. Its committed fingerprint binds the authenticated principal, exact `WorkOccurrence`, exact predecessor correlation head, operation, complete target and temporal-deviation payload, supersession lineage, and every registry, schema, codec, and canonicalization binding used by the operation. If that command committed but its acknowledgment was lost, replay with the same command identity and a byte-identical fingerprint returns the complete committed successor without a write even though the named predecessor is no longer current. Reuse with any changed predecessor, operation, target, payload, lineage, or registry binding conflicts atomically. A different command identity targeting a stale predecessor rejects as stale and cannot be retargeted or treated as replay.

Replacing an actual-work episode is one exhaustive owner-authorized journal transformation over the exact predecessor `WorkOccurrence`, not an isolated `ClaimDisposition`. Before commit it freezes the predecessor episode and enumerates: every current `FactClaim | ClaimDisposition` family head whose tagged `ClaimSubject` is that episode; every current family head whose closed fact-kind schema names that episode in a required typed episode-dependency field; and the predecessor's unique current `WorkCorrelationRevision` head. The closed `FactClaimKindRegistry` determines the complete dependent-family set. The transformation records exactly one disposition for every enumerated head—`CARRY_FORWARD | REPLACE | RETRACT | HISTORICAL`—and atomically binds the fresh successor `WorkOccurrence`, all fresh successor claim families and dispositions, the successor's unique initial correlation head, exact predecessor→successor episode lineage, and the complete predecessor-dependent-head set. A missing, duplicate, stale, conflicting, unordered, or non-enumerated dependent head, two dispositions for one head, or any result that leaves a required successor head absent rejects the whole transformation without a write.

For a dependent claim family, `CARRY_FORWARD` means that the proposition remains semantically unchanged except for the declared predecessor-episode→successor-episode subject substitution: it allocates a fresh successor `FactClaim` family whose immutable `ClaimSubject` names the successor episode, preserves the canonical proposition and provenance subject to that exact substitution, links the old family as carried, and retires the predecessor family from the current positive projection. This is not `CORRECT` and never mutates the old family's subject. `REPLACE` allocates a fresh successor family with a newly owner-authored proposition and exact replacement lineage; `RETRACT` retires the current positive proposition without a successor; `HISTORICAL` is legal only when the enumerated family already has no current positive assertion and merely carries its terminal history into the replacement manifest. A disposition that claims `CARRY_FORWARD` while changing meaning, or retains a predecessor-subject claim as current, conflicts.

For the predecessor correlation head, `CARRY_FORWARD` creates the successor episode's initial current correlation with the same canonical target and deviation payload; `REPLACE` creates that head with a different owner-authorized target or payload; `RETRACT` records explicit withdrawal and creates the successor's initial `STANDALONE` head; and `HISTORICAL` is legal only when no carried or replacement claim requires planning relevance and likewise creates an initial `STANDALONE` head while preserving the predecessor correlation solely as history. The predecessor correlation is never current after successful episode replacement. The result is atomic across episode, claims, and correlation: projections either use the complete predecessor set or the complete successor set and may not combine members from both.

No `WorkOccurrence`, completion claim, or correlation closes a `Task`, `TaskOccurrence`, `TaskSeries`, `IntentionLine`, `Commitment`, sibling, structural parent, or recurrence coordinate by implication. A closure rule cannot target an ambiguous parent, choose between task and occurrence after the fact, or promote work/time evidence into completion. An occurrence-eligible claim may support only that exact occurrence; a task-eligible completion claim may support only that exact task; a series slot resolves only through its exact generated occurrence or slot disposition. The recurrence coordinate is ownership metadata, not the journal fact subject. A deadline, calendar checkbox, reminder, provider state, or passage of time never implies task completion.

A user can say “I did not go to the pool last Tuesday” or “I went at 10, not 8,” and Chiplog preserves the earlier owner-authored assertion and plan while changing the owner-authorized current epistemic projection and, where applicable, the `WorkOccurrence` correlation. After logical deletion, “preserves history” means only content-minimal, non-dereferenceable disposition and lineage metadata: deleted claim payload and any reconstructive derivative remain excluded under `ARCH-DELETE-01`. Task presentation therefore keeps separate axes for current planning state, current owner-authored epistemic outcome, candidate evidence awaiting verification, and due/overdue view.

Every evidence-informed planning revision records the exact material claim dependencies and their effective dispositions at authorization time. A later correction, retraction, supersession, dispute, or deletion of such a dependency never rewrites Plan, but deterministically marks the combined view `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED`, names the affected exact node and invalidated inference, and prevents the product from presenting its planning closure as currently evidenced fulfillment. Chiplog then raises a separate compensating planning proposal or asks for confirmation under current authority. The immutable planning revision and non-deleted claim history remain visible; for a deleted dependency, only the non-reconstructive residue permitted by `ARCH-DELETE-01` remains visible. The divergence persists until a separate owner-authorized planning command reconciles it; a journal correction alone never changes Plan.

False-closure reconciliation is exact-subject and type-specific. Every reconciliation names the exact closure revision or disposition, the exact claim/correlation dependencies that authorized it, and the complete successor lineage from that historical closure to the current lifecycle head; a stale, incomplete, ambiguous, or conflicting lineage rejects the command. For a `Task`, the planning command appends on the same stable task identity and records the targeted prior completion outcome as `INVALIDATED`. It returns the task lifecycle to active only when that erroneous closure is still the operative lifecycle basis of the current head and no later independently authorized lifecycle successor exists. If a later valid cancellation, replacement, renewed completion, or other lifecycle successor already governs, reconciliation preserves that current state and attaches `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED` plus the closure invalidation to the historical closure lineage instead of reversing the successor. For a `TaskOccurrence`, it preserves the historical temporal episode and appends an outcome correction to `OUTCOME_INVALIDATED` or `NOT_COMPLETED` without reactivating or rematerializing that occurrence; continued work receives a newly identified, explicitly linked successor occurrence. For a recurring series slot, an exact `INVALIDATE_OUTCOME` `OccurrenceTransformation` appends the exact slot's successor disposition as `UNFULFILLED` or `OUTCOME_INVALIDATED`; the coordinate remains durably owned and can never materialize again, while compensation or continued work uses an explicit exception/successor occurrence with lineage. None of these commands closes or reopens a structural parent, sibling, task, series, commitment, or root by implication.

The combined projection is deterministic: claim currentness resolves only through the unique rooted owner-authorized `FactClaim | ClaimDisposition` family head; work correlation resolves only through the unique current `WorkCorrelationRevision` head; expectation currentness resolves only through the current owning planning-family head and its complete logical-expectation lineage; and planning lifecycle/outcome resolves only through the exact planning-family head. For a replaced `WorkOccurrence`, the projection first requires one complete valid episode-replacement transformation and selects its successor episode, successor claim-family heads, and successor correlation head as one atomic set; every predecessor-dependent head is historical and cannot remain current in any constituent projection. If any required head, exhaustive disposition, atomic manifest, or lineage is missing, conflicting, stale, or unordered, the affected projection is unresolved and cannot select by timestamp, mix predecessor and successor state, combine rival correlations, infer completion, silently extinguish an expectation, or clear `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED`.

### Recurring and conditional lines

Recurring activity uses a series plus occurrence model. A series carries purpose, recurrence rule, mandate, stop conditions, rule-version intervals, and a materialization horizon; each occurrence carries its own outcome, evidence, and possible external commitment. Its immutable coordinate policy pins calendar system, canonical timezone, local-slot encoding, and fold/gap rule. A `RecurrenceCoordinate` is the canonical tuple `(calendar system, canonical local slot, fold ordinal)` under that series policy; equality is exact tuple equality, including the fold ordinal at repeated local times. Changing this policy requires a new series or an explicit collision-free migration that maps every owned old coordinate and preserves its disposition. Each `(series ID, recurrence coordinate)` has exactly one durable binding or terminal slot disposition. Expansion may allocate it once and never derives ownership again from the current rule.

Rule and activity intervals are half-open `[inclusive coordinate, exclusive coordinate)`. A rule-change boundary belongs to the successor rule. Pause and stop exclude the boundary and all later coordinates; resume starts inclusively at its boundary under a new series revision. Already owned coordinates are unaffected by these boundaries. Operations are closed:

| Operation | Identity and ownership result |
|---|---|
| `MATERIALIZE` | an exact `OccurrenceTransformation` variant creates exactly one occurrence and one disposition/binding head for an unowned coordinate and records its rule revision |
| `SKIP` | records a terminal source-slot disposition; no occurrence is required and expansion cannot regenerate it |
| `CANCEL` | closes the exact occurrence while its coordinate remains owned/tombstoned; it does not cancel the series |
| `RESCHEDULE` | terminally transforms the source occurrence and creates a new exception occurrence ID; the source coordinate remains tombstoned |
| `REPLACE` | terminally marks the predecessor `REPLACED`, makes it historical, and creates one participating successor occurrence ID for materially different work with explicit lineage; it never inherits closure or authority |
| `PAUSE_SERIES` | stops new materialization, reminders, and automated series action for coordinates at or beyond an exact boundary; existing occurrences remain unchanged |
| `RESUME_SERIES` | resumes future unowned coordinates under a new series revision; missed coordinates are not backfilled without an explicit command |
| `CHANGE_RULE` | closes one rule interval and starts a non-overlapping successor interval; owned past/future coordinates retain their bindings |
| `REOPEN` | never reactivates a terminal occurrence; it creates a new exception successor with fresh authority and leaves the source terminal |
| `INVALIDATE_OUTCOME` | corrects only the historical source occurrence outcome to `OUTCOME_INVALIDATED` or `NOT_COMPLETED`; it never reactivates or rematerializes that occurrence, and any continued work requires an explicit exception successor |
| `STOP_SERIES` | terminally closes the series at an exact boundary; coordinates at or beyond it are outside the active interval and cannot materialize without a new successor series |

Generated occurrences use the unique coordinate-binding namespace. A reschedule, replacement, or reopen instead allocates a unique `(series ID, exception key)` binding that points back to the source coordinate and its terminal disposition; it owns no generated coordinate and expansion can never create it. Reopening a skipped slot links directly from its disposition even though no source occurrence existed. A source disposition can name only the exact authorized successors created by that transformation.

`TaskSeriesRevision`/`RuleRevision` author series, horizon, pause, resume, stop, and rule partitions; `TaskOccurrenceRevision` authors one occurrence lifecycle. Every coordinate or exception allocation—including materialization—is an exact `OccurrenceTransformation` command that targets the current series and source heads and writes the unique current `RecurrenceSlotDisposition` plus its immutable `RecurrenceBinding` and lineage. No other writer may allocate or replace ownership. Skip reversal uses explicit `REOPEN` and therefore an exception successor, not regeneration of the original slot. A stopped series is terminal, but its already materialized occurrences retain their own states and ownership. Concurrent attempts that cannot preserve unique ownership remain unresolved.

### Working agreement

The working agreement is a versioned, user-specific body of conversational policy. Every policy has provenance, expressed conditions, scope, expected duration, and a visible lifecycle. Narrow contextual rules usually override broader rules within scope; recency alone gives no priority. Material applications are legible. If a condition is unobservable, Chiplog asks or uses an agreed indicator. Ambiguity is clarified before a decision-changing case rather than on every harmless use.

### Memory and logical deletion

Memory is user-owned, inspectable, and controllable through conversation. It distinguishes source messages and receipts, facts, policy, inferred beliefs with confidence and provenance, and sensitive time-bounded information.

`ARCH-DELETE-01` adopts provenance registration, ordinary-contour exclusion, derivative and queued-action invalidation, retained-copy non-repopulation, purpose-bound audit/recovery controls, quarantine-only recovery, and the content-minimal user recovery surface specified here. It does not require physical database erasure and does not adopt a particular dependency-index representation, backup system, cryptographic assurance level, or proof that unregistered/uncontrolled external copies were recalled. The evidence required to exercise this contract is specified only in [Adoption readiness](#adoption-readiness). A new authorized source means a fresh independently supplied source event with new provenance; a restored copy, recovered deleted payload, reconstructive derivative, or mere relabeling can never clear the deletion fence.

This deletion rule has precedence over every history, provenance, correction, receipt, audit, and participant-facing visibility promise in this document. After deletion, identity and lineage may remain only as policy-minimal, non-dereferenceable tombstone metadata. Payload, reconstructive summaries, embeddings, and content-bearing historical projections are unavailable to every ordinary, participant-facing, and product-processing contour; the sole exception is purpose-bound access inside the isolated audit/recovery quarantine defined below, which never permits ordinary-state re-entry or content-bearing output. “Preserved history” then means continuity of identity, transitions, authority, and deletion status—not preservation or display of deleted content.

Deletion is semantically strict logical deletion, not physical database erasure. Every derived artifact and pending action records provenance edges to its source inputs, including caches, indexes, embeddings, model context, free/busy and participant projections, proposals, recommendations, inferred commitments, shared evidence, telemetry, and queued effects. Deletion immediately fences new use, removes payload from ordinary retrieval and model context, purges or recomputes ordinary derivatives, invalidates inferred state, and cancels or holds pending actions whose authority or evidence depended on it. Immutable remnants become non-authoritative and non-dereferenceable; only policy-minimal tombstone metadata may remain. Backups, replicas, logs, exports, and training datasets follow the same deletion dependency policy and cannot repopulate ordinary state. Already disclosed external effects cannot be recalled; Chiplog records that limit and applies any separately authorized notification or compensation. Retention is disclosed; any legally required physical-destruction workflow is a separate future capability and is not claimed by this logical-deletion contract.

Audit and recovery are separate capabilities, never a generic bypass. Each is case- and purpose-bound, time-limited, least-privilege, and held by named roles independent of product and growth operations. Deleted or cross-principal payload requires dual authorization, append-only tamper-evident access logging, minimized output, retention limits, periodic review, and notice to the principal and affected party except under a narrowly recorded lawful exception. Recovery may restore deleted material only into isolated quarantine for the stated verification purpose. Recovered material can never enter or influence ordinary retrieval, inference, planning, action, export, or training, even with later approval. Its only exits are purpose-complete destruction or continued quarantined retention under the applicable basis. A fresh independently supplied source creates a distinct ordinary-state object and never clears, replaces, or adopts provenance from the deleted object; no quarantine output or actor exposed to recovered payload may copy, paraphrase, resubmit, or authorize that material as the new source.

Deletion has its own user-visible recovery surface. Before deletion, Chiplog previews—in privacy-safe language—material dependent actions that will be held or cancelled and already disclosed effects that cannot be recalled. Afterward it leaves a content-minimal receipt naming the affected subjects by safe local reference, actions stopped, non-recallable effects, obligations and disputes that remain, whether isolated restoration is permitted, and exact safe next actions. It never reveals the deleted payload to explain the hold and never presents the aggregate as simply done or deleted while residue remains.

### Evidence, commitment recognition, and receipts

Chiplog preserves a checkable chain between source expression, interpretation or proposal, granted authority, external action, provider state, and observed outcome. Claims are graded: system action succeeded; provider state observed; probable real-world outcome; human- or counterparty-confirmed outcome; disputed or unknown. Provider success does not prove fulfillment, and probabilistic signals may prompt a soft query but cannot establish completion.

#### Commitment recognition and lifecycle

Relationship/source-local obligation-instance allocation is serialized, not guessed from a read, and begins only after direct authenticated principal verification. Its canonical cursor is not a new writer family: it is the exact latest principal-verified `PlanningCommittedResult` in the namespace's obligation-instance allocation-result lineage, or `GENESIS` before the first verified instance. The same current namespace head also binds the exhaustive verified-instance index from every governing-right/acceptance-act discriminator and every allocated local discriminator to exactly one current or historical actual commitment identity. Both `ALLOCATE_COMMITMENT` and `CONFIRM_RECOGNITION` must resolve their principal-verified discriminator against that index inside the same atomic command that targets the cursor. An exact existing binding publishes no rival commitment and advances no allocation ordinal: it returns the indexed commitment and, when a `REPRESENTED` candidate is addressed, terminally correlates that candidate through `CORRELATE_RECOGNITION`. A conflicting binding or unresolved discriminator collision leaves every candidate non-operative under `IDENTITY_DISPUTED`, publishes no actual commitment, and does not advance the cursor. Only a discriminator proven distinct from every indexed binding may publish a cursor successor, allocate the next gap-free local ordinal when needed, and create or promote an actual commitment. A principal-verified recognition or actual-commitment command targets that cursor and the planning transaction committer atomically publishes its correlation, dispute, or distinct-instance result; stale competing commands fail and retry against the successor cursor. The winning distinct-instance result assigns the next gap-free ordinal, which thereafter remains the stable local discriminator even if the commitment moves, terminates, or its semantic fields later resemble another instance. Byte-identical replay returns the same committed result, ordinal, identity, and candidate disposition. An evidence-policy assessor's non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate allocation does not target or advance this cursor and allocates no local discriminator. A namespace may instead canonically choose a collision-free discriminator derived from the exact principal-verified committed-result allocation key before its first obligation-instance allocation; it cannot mix modes, renumber committed instances, reserve uncommitted gaps, or derive identity from timestamp, arrival order, semantic equality, or provider occurrence.

Chiplog assesses external reliance from evidence rather than creating it by classification. Evidence may include accepted offers or counterparty terms, durable shared records, or conduct that demonstrates reliance. Semantic context—such as “yoga class”—and provider artifacts such as invited participants or a meeting link can establish likely third-party involvement without formal confirmation, but permit only the evidence-policy assessor to allocate a visible, non-operative `REPRESENTED` candidate with a `SUSPECTED` assessment and clarification path. This candidate is an addressable hypothesis: it does not recognize that an obligation exists, does not create an actual or operative commitment, does not allocate a local obligation-instance discriminator, and changes no Plan expectation or operative planning head. Commitment identity is instance-bearing once directly verified by the principal. Its primary discriminator is the exact governing-right ID or acceptance-act ID that created the obligation, together with the governing relationship or source agreement. When the source supplies no such discriminator, direct principal verification allocates a local `obligation_instance_id` with a gap-free ordinal inside that exact relationship/source; the verification binds the obligor, rights-holder set, obligated scope, effective interval, and evidence snapshot used to distinguish the instance. An occurrence identifies only a coordinated arrangement slot and never substitutes for commitment identity. Semantic equality of parties, scope, agreement, or interval is a search aid, never a merge rule. Materially ambiguous matches remain separate non-operative candidate representations, each with provenance and an identity dispute, until the principal verifies whether they represent one obligation or several; ambiguity cannot select an operative node, and resolution never silently merges their histories. Only direct principal verification may `CONFIRM_RECOGNITION` or, when no matching verified instance exists, allocate a new actual `Commitment` identity representing the attributed existing obligation; record creation does not create the underlying right. Every later confirmation, rejection, withdrawal, release, or reclassification command targets exactly one commitment ID and current revision. Creating a new obligation requires direct authenticated principal authority, an authority grant where applicable, and any externally effective communication or acceptance required by the governing relationship. A claim about another person's commitment requires matching evidence.

A suspected or disputed recognition is a local representation, not an operative assertion that rights exist. If admissible evidence and the governing resolution authority disprove it, the node becomes terminal `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED`; this removes it from the derived live-commitment predicate while preserving its claims and rationale. A principal's denial alone cannot reject another claimant's plausible right: conflicting evidence remains `DISPUTED` until the named relationship process or applicable authority resolves it. `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED` says only that this local recognition was rejected and never withdraws or releases a real obligation.

Commitment lifecycle is the closed domain `REPRESENTED | PRE_EFFECTIVE | OPERATIVE | FULFILLED | RELEASED | WITHDRAWN | TRANSFERRED | REPLACED | SPLIT | EXPIRED | REPRESENTATION_CORRELATED | REPRESENTATION_REJECTED`. The first three states are nonterminal; every other state is terminal. `REPRESENTED` is a local candidate representation whose underlying right is not established by the record itself; `PRE_EFFECTIVE` is an accepted or created obligation before the governing relationship says reliance rights arose; `OPERATIVE` means those rights are live. `REPRESENTATION_CORRELATED` terminalizes only the duplicate candidate representation while preserving its provenance and exact link to the independently existing actual commitment; it neither terminalizes nor revises that actual commitment. Epistemic `SUSPECTED | CONFIRMED | DISPUTED | REJECTED` assessments remain in `RelianceAssessment` and never act as lifecycle states. | `EXPIRE_UNDER_RULE` | `PRE_EFFECTIVE` or `OPERATIVE` → `EXPIRED` | exact procedure resolver or mechanically designated committer named by the previously governing expiry/uncertainty-resolution rule, all required notice, and admissible evidence of completion | same identity; terminal |

For either allocation event, an initial arrangement facet is permitted only when the same atomic command supplies its exact authorized `ArrangementRevision`; otherwise the result has no facet. A later no-facet→facet change uses only `ATTACH_ARRANGEMENT_FACET`; a facet→no-facet change uses only `REMOVE_ARRANGEMENT_FACET`. Every other nonterminal same-identity event must explicitly carry forward or advance a still-compatible facet without changing obligation lifecycle. Every terminal event makes the predecessor's facet historical with that predecessor; `TRANSFER`, `REPLACE`, `RIGHTS_SPLIT`, and `RIGHTS_HOLDER_SPLIT` may give a successor an arrangement facet only through that successor's separately authorized initial coherent pair. No lifecycle row closes or cancels a coordinated child slot implicitly, and no facet or slot event supplies a lifecycle transition.

An in-place revision may change only administrative or coordination fields that the governing relationship says do not alter obligated scope, obligor identity, rights-holder membership, terminal criteria, or independently releasable rights. Expanding an obligation requires the obligor's exact authority and any acceptance needed to create the added right and therefore allocates a separately governed instance or uses `REPLACE`/`RIGHTS_SPLIT` when the predecessor is changed. Narrowing or otherwise materially rescoping a live obligation requires release by every affected right-holder; separable partial change uses `RIGHTS_SPLIT`, while inseparable change uses `REPLACE`. Ordinary `rescope` cannot bypass these rules. A dispute, evidence update, or arrangement-slot change that does not match a lifecycle row may update only its own authoritative family and cannot mutate commitment lifecycle.

`WITHDRAW` is available only before the governing relationship says reliance rights arose. Once operative, `RELEASE` requires the exact right-holder or other releasing authority named by that relationship; silence, principal preference, system timeout, or provider deletion is never release. `TRANSFER` always creates a successor commitment identity and terminally marks the predecessor `TRANSFERRED` only when the governing rule permits assignment or the exact obligee accepts novation; the successor records the new obligor/right-holder set and receives fresh authority. No party-set mutation occurs in place. `REPLACE` creates or identifies a successor commitment and requires both its necessary acceptance and an authorized release or novation of the predecessor; communication alone is not acceptance unless the governing rule says so. `FULFILL` requires the node's exact terminal criterion. For a joint rights-holder set, every required party disposition must be satisfied; otherwise the node remains live or disputed. Each material or terminal command records the governing rule, exact authorized parties, acceptance/release evidence, and current revision.

The absence of a current arrangement facet or child slot is never evidence that external reliance ended. A live commitment may leave external-reliance mode only on one of three bases: explicit release by every affected releasing authority; satisfaction of a previously agreed terminal condition established by an admissible source; or completion of a previously governing formal uncertainty-resolution procedure, such as expiry after the notice that procedure requires. If none is established, the commitment remains visibly in `unresolved reliance` even when no active arrangement remains. A scheduled review may surface the node and seek evidence, but cannot itself supply release, fulfillment, withdrawal, or expiry.

When the relationship did not settle an end-of-reliance basis in advance, the principal may adopt a favorable closure position. Chiplog may challenge that position privately, gather the strongest available support for it, and use it for the principal's planning. In authorized external communication Chiplog advocates the principal's position without deliberately disclosing or signaling its private disagreement, subject to the factual conduct and epistemic boundary above. That partisan representation does not change the shared lifecycle. Unless the counterparty accepts the position, a separately assessed operative external rule establishes it, or a governing procedure resolves it, the external projection remains `contested reliance`; Chiplog must not describe the principal's preferred closure as mutual acceptance, established fulfillment, or shared fact.

While that divergence persists, personal planning uses a contextual hedge rather than treating the shared status as the plan. By default the node remains visible in the user's adopted review rhythm, while reserved time, resources, and attention reflect likelihood, downside, reversibility, and deadlines. Reversible planning may proceed as though the principal's position will prevail. Before committing each relevant planning change, Chiplog evaluates aggregate post-change feasibility across the current reserve, deadlines, dependencies, and all already adopted allocations for that exact contested commitment. The confirmation boundary is crossed before either one irreversible act or a sequence of individually reversible acts would make fulfillment, defense, or an available protective option practically infeasible. At that boundary Chiplog displays the exact obligation, the reserve or option that would be lost, the combined irreversible consequences, and safer remaining alternatives, then requires exact principal confirmation of that risk. The principal may knowingly select zero, full, or another personal reserve in natural language and may revise the review cadence under the proactivity contract; there is no mandatory institutional minimum reserve. Such confirmation changes only personal planning and attention, never the shared or external commitment status, another party's right, or any separately required third-party authority or consent.

Current arrangement state is one coherent pair: the exact current `CommitmentRevision` and either no facet or one `ArrangementRevision` explicitly bound to that revision. A new commitment revision must explicitly carry forward a still-compatible facet, advance it, or remove it under the same authorized transition; an arrangement revision cannot attach to a stale commitment revision. Concurrent changes that do not yield one authorized pair remain unresolved. Removing the facet changes only the commitment kind and coordination fields under exact-node authority and never terminates or releases the obligation.

Changing a coordinated slot preserves the commitment identity unless an explicit exact-node commitment transition changes the obligated scope itself. The old slot becomes historical and a successor slot is created or revised; the arrangement facet records the current coordination fields. Slot cancellation or replacement alone never withdraws, releases, replaces, or fulfills the commitment.

The domain kind and its evidentiary confidence are separate. A node may be an external-reliance commitment or its arrangement subtype, while the basis for that classification is inferred, confirmed, or disputed, with provenance and temporal history. Round 03 supersedes the earlier open framing that treated arrangement status as a potentially independent axis.

Transfer from `PRE_EFFECTIVE` is legal only when the exact governing relationship permits assignment or novation before effectiveness and every authority and acceptance in the registry row is current. The successor cannot become `OPERATIVE` earlier than its own bound effectiveness rule permits. The predecessor and successor publish atomically; if pre-effective transfer is not permitted, the command rejects and the parties must use an accepted `REPLACE` or leave the predecessor live. `RIGHTS_HOLDER_SPLIT` changes only the relying-party dimension represented by the canonical `RIGHTS_HOLDER` role: it terminally dispositions the predecessor, records the departing party's release, and creates one successor with the same unduplicated performance scope and a non-empty remaining party set. Departure of the last relying party is `RELEASE` with no successor commitment. If the parties' claims are inseparably joint, the split rejects; an accepted `REPLACE` is required, otherwise the predecessor remains live.

Reliance assessments have cross-policy lineage rather than one unrelated current head per policy version. Their canonical family key is the exact commitment representation and attributed party; each revision additionally binds the evidence-policy version that evaluated it. Exactly one assessment revision is applicable-current for that family. A policy change cannot make a parallel old head current: an authorized migration command targets the prior applicable head and the new policy version, re-evaluates or explicitly carries every bound evidence item, publishes one successor assessment and its predecessor disposition atomically, and marks the predecessor historical for current projection. The migration authority and resolver are those named by the new policy's applicability rule together with any governing relationship authority required to change consequence. A valid successor may remain `DISPUTED`, but the old disputed head cannot remain independently operative or count again. If migration authority, evidence disposition, or applicable policy selection is ambiguous, the family has no applicable current assessment and dependent action remains held; history is never rewritten.

Each current `ArrangementRevision` is the sole authoritative writer of its facet's exhaustive canonical current slot-membership set. The set is ordered by the adopted canonicalization rule and contains each coordinated member exactly once as `(TaskOccurrence identity, exact current TaskOccurrenceRevision, current RecurrenceSlotDisposition when the occurrence is recurrence-owned)`. There is no inferred membership from time overlap, structural parentage, provider state, commitment scope, labels, or journal evidence, and no second membership resolver. An initial facet binds its complete initial set in the same `PlanningCommand` that allocates or adopts the facet and every newly allocated member. A successor facet alone may carry, add, remove, reschedule, replace, or cancel membership, under the exact arrangement-revision authority and any governing acceptance required for that coordination change.

Every arrangement-slot command uses the source `ArrangementRevision`'s membership set as its closed comparison domain. Its source manifest must equal that canonical set exactly and must bind the current head of every member: the exact `TaskOccurrenceRevision` and, where applicable, current `RecurrenceSlotDisposition`. Its result membership is deterministically `(source members carried unchanged − members explicitly removed, rescheduled, replaced, or cancelled) + exact authorized additions and exact successor members`. Duplicate identities or members reject before comparison; canonicalization only orders the already unique set. `CARRY` preserves the same occurrence identity and exact current head. `ADD` introduces one exact current occurrence, allocated in the same result or already current and explicitly authorized for this facet. `REMOVE` ends only the membership edge and leaves the occurrence's own lifecycle unchanged. For an ordinary non-recurrence-owned occurrence, `RESCHEDULE` preserves the occurrence identity, advances exactly one successor `TaskOccurrenceRevision`, and records revision lineage; for a recurrence-owned occurrence it terminally dispositions the source and allocates exactly one fresh exception occurrence identity under the recurrence contract, with binding and complete source→successor lineage. `REPLACE` always removes the exact predecessor member and binds one named fresh successor occurrence identity with complete lineage. `CANCEL` terminally dispositions the exact slot outcome and removes it from current membership. No mutation may allocate or select a slot outside this closed result equation, and only the ordinary `RESCHEDULE` branch may retain one occurrence identity through a revision advance.

One `PlanningCommand` and its sole `PlanningCommittedResult` atomically publish the exact current `CommitmentRevision`, the source and successor `ArrangementRevision`, the complete source and result membership sets, every member occurrence and recurrence-disposition head, the authorized acts and governing acceptances, and complete predecessor→successor or removal lineage. The planning transaction committer is the sole publication authority; occurrence, recurrence, provider, journal, or facet-local workers cannot publish a membership or member-head change independently. Any coordinated member-head advance therefore requires the same atomic successor facet, even when all other arrangement fields are unchanged. A stale commitment, facet, occurrence, recurrence-disposition, or authority head; a missing, duplicate, extra, multiply current, or differently ordered canonical member; incomplete lineage; a source set unequal to the source facet; a result set unequal to the registered transition result; or partial publication rejects the whole command without a write. Byte-identical replay returns the complete existing result; reuse with any changed source member, result member, head, act, authority, acceptance, lineage, registry, or canonicalization binding rejects.

Membership transfers coordination neither upward nor sideways. Adding a slot does not create, expand, transfer, replace, fulfill, release, withdraw, or otherwise disposition the commitment; removing, replacing, cancelling, or closing every slot does not remove the facet or close the commitment. Conversely, a commitment lifecycle event never mutates a member occurrence implicitly: every required facet and member disposition must be separately enumerated in the same atomic result under its own authority. A command whose complete resulting coordination is canonically identical to its source is a prohibited no-op.

Every personal planning command that consumes a shared resource evaluates one cumulative post-command envelope across all live or contested commitments, their current arrangement slots, adopted personal allocations, deadlines, dependencies, and every current affected-party constraint that can claim that resource or preserve an option using it. The calculation counts each commitment instance and each stable affected-party episode once, follows current successor lineage, and rejects double counting of predecessor/successor, assessment/contest/hold, or several projections of one constraint. It compares the atomic proposed result—not each member in isolation—with every jointly impaired fulfillment, defense, notice, contest, release, reschedule, and recovery option. A sequence of reversible allocations cannot evade the boundary by arriving in separate commands.

Exact confirmation of zero reserve is valid only after the displayed confirmation binds the current envelope version and enumerates every commitment and affected-party option jointly made practically infeasible, the resource and amount consumed, the relevant deadlines, combined irreversible consequence, uncertainty, and safer remaining alternatives. Stale or incomplete enumeration rejects. The confirmation authorizes only the displayed personal allocation and reserve posture; it never changes commitment, arrangement, affected-party, delegation, acceptance, or external-action state and never supplies another party's consent or authority. If the allocation would require Chiplog to perform an action blocked by an operative protective hold, zero-reserve confirmation cannot authorize that assistance: the hold must first receive its own registered removal, release, expiry, or supersession. This limits Chiplog's participation only and never claims to prevent the principal from acting independently.

#### Receipt projections and privacy

Receipts have audience-specific projections rather than one globally reusable record. The internal audit contour may retain only fields necessary for a named audit or legal purpose. Ordinary and external projections use the coarsest sufficient time, provider-local non-exportable references, rotating purpose/audience-scoped tokens, and no stable cross-receipt identifier; exact provider, mandate, result, or timing fields are omitted when they enable unnecessary linkage. They never copy source text, event titles, private participant identities, reconstructible summaries, or “anonymized” inputs without a stated threat model and tested unlinkability criterion. After deletion, retained fields must pass a reidentification/linkability review against calendar and provider auxiliary data and are unavailable to ordinary search, joins, personalization, or inference. Unauthorized, deleted, and nonexistent receipt lookups do not disclose which case applies.

Authorized audit must not turn logical deletion into ordinary reconstruction or enable tracking across principals, audiences, purposes, or coordination contexts. This is an outcome boundary, not an adopted correlation mechanism: identifier design, encryption, authorization realization, storage topology, and the physical receipt-linkage scheme remain implementation and privacy-assurance work.

### Evidence-bounded node closure

Evidence is closure-eligible only under an explicit rule keyed to the exact personal claim type and head, exact target planning-node identity and current revision, terminal criterion, source/evidence class, and any acceptance requirement. Correlation applicability is closed. `WORK_ACTIVITY` and `WORK_TIME_SPENT` claims use `WORK_EPISODE`, require its unique current typed `WorkCorrelationRevision` to interpret their planning relevance, and are never completion evidence. `WORK_EPISODE_COMPLETION` uses `WORK_EPISODE` and is closure-eligible only with that unique current correlation mapping it to exactly one current task, occurrence, or exact series-generated occurrence/slot target admitted by the rule. `DIRECT_TASK_COMPLETION` uses `DIRECT_TASK_OUTCOME`, requires exact equality with the target task and current `TaskRevision`, and is exempt from `WorkCorrelation`. `DIRECT_OCCURRENCE_COMPLETION` uses `DIRECT_OCCURRENCE_OUTCOME`, requires exact equality with the target occurrence and current `TaskOccurrenceRevision`, and is exempt from `WorkCorrelation`; a series-generated occurrence may qualify only through that exact occurrence identity and binding, never through a recurrence coordinate alone. `EXTERNAL_EFFECT`, `COMMITMENT_FACT`, and `ACCEPTANCE_OR_RELEASE` are ineligible to close a task, occurrence, or series slot; they may support only a separately adopted domain rule for their own exact subject. No other personal claim family is closure-eligible.

For actual work, the rule must distinguish time/activity evidence from an explicit completion claim and name exactly one eligible target class: task, occurrence, or the exact series-generated occurrence/slot disposition. No rule may infer that target from a recurrence coordinate alone, leave task-versus-occurrence selection to the closure command, or reclassify an episode-bearing assertion as a direct outcome to evade correlation. Provider execution or provider state is never closure-eligible for real-world fulfillment unless the node's separately adopted acceptance rule explicitly defines that exact provider fact as its terminal criterion and the principal has directly verified the displayed interpretation into the personal journal where a personal claim is required. When eligible evidence establishes a terminal fact, Chiplog records or proposes the exact owner-verifiable claim. A distinct planning command may then close only the specifically proven node if current authority, policy, exact revision, and claim dependencies still permit it; the claim itself never closes anything. Closing or cancelling a coordinated slot occurrence does not by itself close its commitment identity, task, parent intention line, recurring series, coordinate owner, or sibling nodes. Their intention and commitment states persist until independently resolved under their own evidence and authority rules. Subjective or externally contested outcomes remain owner-verification candidates or separately attributed shared/affected-party disputes rather than being promoted to personal-journal fact or fulfillment.

## Intention-line lifecycle

Root lifecycle has separate but constrained axes. `activity` is `ACTIVE | PAUSED_USER | DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM | RETIRED`; `personal_outcome` is `OPEN | COMPLETED | ABANDONED | REPLACED | TRANSFORMED`. The only valid pairs are `ACTIVE|PAUSED_USER|DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM × OPEN` and `RETIRED × COMPLETED|ABANDONED|REPLACED|TRANSFORMED`; one exact `IntentionLineRevision` transition updates both axes together. `DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM` is a reversible policy state with recorded cause, not user pause, abandonment, or evidence about intent. Split/merge retires a source as `TRANSFORMED`, while one-to-one replacement uses `REPLACED`. Pause, deactivation, reactivation, and reschedule are nonterminal. Delegation changes neither axis by itself. Only an exact root command may select a personal outcome, and it must state the residual scope; a terminal personal outcome cannot claim that delegated, child-work, or external-reliance scope was resolved.

1. **Understand.** Interpret the intention and context without pretending ambiguity disappeared.
2. **Shape.** Expose constraints, conflicts, participants, and consequences; optionally form a personal plan.
3. **Recognize or create commitment when applicable.** Recognize existing external reliance from sufficient evidence, or create it only under sufficient authority; classify coordinated commitments as arrangements without turning arrangement into a separate axis.
4. **Project and act.** Create or update external representations and perform authorized actions, leaving receipts.
5. **Observe.** Track provider state and other evidence for material divergence.
6. **Reconcile.** Establish what happened and offer subject-specific outcomes: recommit, authorized rescope, reschedule, delegate, or replace; pause or abandon applies only to personal/root state and never releases an operative commitment.
7. **Resolve.** Preserve the final outcome and rationale so the intention does not silently disappear.

Silence is not abandonment, fulfillment, or consent to reschedule. External commitments remain open. Chiplog may apply only a pre-agreed reversible fallback, reduce reminder pressure without hiding unresolved status, or system-deactivate a personal intention under policy. It may not silently contact others, reschedule an arrangement, create an obligation, or claim refusal.

The aggregate display is a deterministic product of exact current heads: `identity_state × activity × personal_outcome × owned_work_state × delegation_residual_state × external_reliance_state × affected_party_state`. External reliance distinguishes `NONE | SUSPECTED | LIVE | DISPUTED | RESOLVED`.

`identity_state` is exactly `CURRENT | RETIRED_RESOLVED | RETIRED_SPLIT | RETIRED_MERGED | RETIRED_REPLACED`. A non-transformation `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED` outcome yields `RETIRED_RESOLVED`; split, merge, and replacement yield their named retirement values. `owned_work_state` considers every current, non-historical task, series, and occurrence owned by the root; tombstoned coordinates and transformed predecessors do not participate, while their current successors do. Task and occurrence states `CLOSED | CANCELLED | SKIPPED`, historical corrected occurrence outcomes `OUTCOME_INVALIDATED | NOT_COMPLETED`, and series state `STOPPED` are terminal; all other current lifecycle states, including `PAUSED`, are nonterminal. A cancelled occurrence remains a participating terminal node unless a committed transformation classifies it as a historical predecessor. A skipped coordinate with no occurrence is a historical terminal slot disposition and deliberately does not add a second work node to this reduction. `NONE` means there are no participating nodes; `OPEN` means all participating nodes are nonterminal; `RESOLVED` means all are terminal; `MIXED` means both exist. Each node contributes its own head independently: resolved occurrences do not close an open task, a paused series is open, and a stopped series is terminal even though separately open occurrences still contribute.

`delegation_residual_state` reduces exactly the members of every current `DelegationSetRevision` owned by the root after applying their explicit transformation dispositions. A member in `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE | ACTIVE | PERFORMANCE_REPORTED | RECONCILING | PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE | TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED` contributes `OPEN`; a member in `RESOLVED_REJECTED | RESOLVED_WITHDRAWN | RESOLVED_EXPIRED | RESOLVED_RETAINED | RESOLVED_PARALLEL | RESOLVED_RELEASED | RESOLVED_TRANSFERRED` contributes `RESOLVED`. `NONE` means no current members, `OPEN` means all open, `RESOLVED` means all terminal, and `MIXED` means both. No evidence, expired target binding, historical predecessor, or overlapping redelegation changes membership implicitly.

Each current commitment head first maps to exactly one contribution class in this order: any current disputed assessment → `DISPUTED`; a `REPRESENTED` head whose strongest status is suspected → `SUSPECTED`; `PRE_EFFECTIVE` and `OPERATIVE` heads → `LIVE`; any terminal lifecycle head—including `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED`—→ `RESOLVED`. A current head outside the closed lifecycle registry, an unnamed transition, or a missing required affected-party disposition makes the aggregate unresolved and invalid rather than selecting a class. No other lifecycle/assessment pair is valid. Terminal classification removes the earlier suspected contribution without erasing its history, and the UI distinguishes represented, pre-effective, and operative nonterminal nodes. `external_reliance_state` then reduces these mutually exclusive classes with precedence `DISPUTED > SUSPECTED > LIVE > RESOLVED > NONE`; absence yields `NONE`. The UI also shows per-instance counts in every class so the precedence label never conceals siblings in lower classes or collapses semantically identical obligations.

`affected_party_state` reduces every current separately attributed assertion, standing/applicability assessment, contest, and precautionary constraint bound to the line or any of its nodes, including a commitment, after applying the current root/governed-node transformation manifest. Components of one canonical evidence-or-risk episode jointly contribute exactly once by that episode's stable identity regardless of how many source acts, families, or successor nodes reference them; rebinding preserves attribution, standing, contest, hold, resolver, count, and addressability, and commitment binding or transformation never transfers or suppresses the contribution. Any disputed episode component yields `DISPUTED`; otherwise any unresolved assertion, pending contest, or active protective hold yields `OPEN`; otherwise one or more episode-terminal resolved/rejected components yield `RESOLVED`; absence yields `NONE`. A missing, duplicate, orphaned, or unauthorized transformation disposition makes this axis unresolved and invalid. A product timeout, commitment transition, or missing resolver cannot mark this axis resolved while any component of the underlying episode remains current.

A retired or personally resolved root with open work, delegation residuals, reliance, or an affected-party item is displayed explicitly, for example `PERSONAL_ABANDONED / WORK_MIXED / EXTERNAL_LIVE / AFFECTED_PARTY_DISPUTED`; it is never collapsed into terminal, archived, successful, or hidden. Every unresolved child or attributed constraint remains addressable until its own exact transition or an authorized ownership transformation resolves it.

## Conflict handling

There is no universal hierarchy such as “latest instruction always wins.” Conflict policy is user-specific and negotiated in natural language. Chiplog applies the working agreement when it clearly resolves a conflict and makes consequential applications legible. Irreducible value conflicts and contradictory active instructions return to the user. External reliance constrains unilateral changes even when the user's preference changes.

## Proactivity, action authority, and revocation

Observation and proposals can be autonomous; reminders and recurring rituals follow an agreed mandate; reversible external actions may be delegated within its envelope. Third-party impact means changing another person's expectations, access, resources, or view of the user, including messages, publication, shared-resource reservations, invitations, plan changes, availability disclosure, and expected response. A previewable class or batch may share one authorization, but approval renews when audience, meaning, cost, publicity, or obligation character changes.

The user controls proactivity in natural language, including cadence such as “let's plan next week on Sunday evenings.” The default review rhythm is a weekly review of hanging work and unresolved obligations, a broader monthly review, and a still broader quarterly review. The user may revise, pause, or replace that rhythm conversationally. A review may organize attention, surface stale evidence, and propose reconciliation, but it neither changes planning state nor proves that an intention, task, arrangement, or external commitment ended. A proactivity rule is versioned, inspectable, pausable, amendable, and cancellable through conversation. By itself it can generate only labeled proposals; it grants no commit or dispatch authority. Any future bounded auto-execution permission is a separate authority family with explicit scope, expiry, and revocation, never an implication of the cadence request.

Unambiguous suspension or revocation takes effect immediately and blocks locally unstarted steps; it does not wait for a proposal/adoption cycle. Resumption or replacement uses the exact-version adoption path. A plausibly revocatory but ambiguous utterance—such as “stop doing that,” “don't send any more,” or “pause this”—advances a durable ordering generation scoped to the principal plus every plausibly targeted authority family and creates a provisional hold over matching grants, queued actions, recurring chains, and new dispatches before clarification. Every local queue and adapter must acquire against that generation immediately before its irreversible boundary; fan-out workers with a stale generation are fenced. Nothing locally unstarted under that hold may begin. Effects outside this controlled contour are reported as already crossed or `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never promised cancelled. Clarification may narrow the hold but cannot authorize intervening starts, and lifting it requires an explicit resume or new grant bound to the clarified scope. Chiplog safely cancels in-flight work where possible without promising provider rollback, discloses completed or visible effects, and proposes compensation only under separate authority. Revoking the agent's mandate does not itself cancel a human obligation on which another party relies.

### External-action failure protocol

Every external attempt begins with an immutable action intent bound to the exact planning revision, authority-grant version, hold/conflict generation, input dependencies, effect fingerprint, provider target, intended recipient or closed audience, representative endpoint identity, channel class and interaction context, exact disclosure projection, communication-mandate version, and provider-scoped idempotency or fencing key. The send adapter's last local action is an atomic transition from dispatch authorization to `SEND_COMMITTED`; it requires exact equality between those intent bindings and the current mandate bindings and revalidates the current plan, grant, expiry, provisional holds, conflicts, dependencies, every factual assertion in the communication mandate against its bound evidence snapshot, verification result, contradiction disposition, freshness condition, and invalidation dependencies, and every invoked authority's exact source, status, scope, operative-applicability assessment, and governing basis in the same ordering domain. A changed recipient, audience, endpoint identity, channel class, interaction context, disclosure projection, provider target, correction, newly surfaced contradiction, material context change, or changed authority source or applicability blocks dispatch. The change may proceed only through a newly issued current successor mandate and action intent that explicitly authorize and bind the changed values; an old mandate cannot be replayed across recipients, audiences, endpoints, channels, contexts, or disclosure projections. The external model does not investigate or repair an invalid mandate. That transition is the authoritative irrevocable dispatch boundary: if suspension, revocation, or a hold commits first, `SEND_COMMITTED` is denied; if `SEND_COMMITTED` commits first, the action is treated as having crossed the boundary even before network bytes or provider acknowledgement, and later revocation cannot promise prevention. The audit trail exposes which ordering won. Where a provider supports fencing, it binds the same generation, but the product does not claim external linearizability the provider cannot enforce.

Each independently observable and independently fenceable sub-effect repeats that check and has its own state and receipt. If a provider call can produce multiple effects that the adapter cannot fence and observe separately, the whole call is one inseparable effect bundle and any unresolved mixed result is `PARTIAL`/`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`; a fully evidenced mixed result is `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`. The attempt states are `INTENT_RECORDED | HELD_BEFORE_SEND | CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND | SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND | DISPATCH_AUTHORIZED | SEND_COMMITTED | SENT | CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | PARTIAL | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`; compensation is a separate linked action, not another state of the original attempt. No batch or provider call is assumed transactional across effects. `HELD_BEFORE_SEND` is nonterminal and may resume only through a new dispatch authorization bound to current heads and the same exact fingerprint. `CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND` and `SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND` are terminal proof that the local send boundary was never crossed; any later action requires a new intent. A stale or losing dispatch authorization is retired into one of those states and cannot be reacquired.

After `SEND_COMMITTED`, automatic retransmission is permitted only when a provider idempotency guarantee still covers every transmission and the exact effect, or affirmative evidence proves every earlier transmission permanently incapable of taking effect—for example terminal provider rejection/cancellation, an expired enforced execution lease, or a winning provider fence. A point-in-time read showing no current effect is recorded as absence observed, not terminal no-effect; it remains `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`. Any replacement without the safe retransmission conditions requires visible human authorization that explicitly accepts duplicate risk. Timeout, connection loss, malformed response, conflicting provider state, or expired/unverifiable idempotency likewise becomes `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never “failed” or “nothing happened.”

`ExternalActionIntent` is the immutable exact effect or inseparable effect-bundle subject. Its one authoritative append-only `ExternalActionAttemptRevision` family owns the current logical-attempt head. Each actual network send is a distinct immutable `TransmissionAttempt` child, derived from the intent ID plus a retained request ordinal, and records dispatch time, exact payload fingerprint, provider target, idempotency/fence key, verified coverage interval, transport evidence, and result. The first child is allocated with `SEND_COMMITTED`. A later child may be allocated only from `SEND_COMMITTED`, `SENT`, or `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` after the adapter proves the safe-retransmission condition against every prior child and current authority; it keeps the same logical attempt and cannot reset its state or ambiguity. Duplicate ordinals/fingerprints deduplicate; mismatches reject. Parent reduction is conservative: any child with possible effect prevents `FAILED_NO_EFFECT`; conflicting or incomplete child evidence yields `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `PARTIAL`; only evidence covering all children permits a terminal parent outcome. Only the trusted local send adapter may commit the pre-send, child-allocation, and `SEND_COMMITTED` transitions; authenticated provider-result ingestion or an authorized reconciler may append evidence-driven successors. Every writer targets the exact current attempt revision and records its evidence. Legal parent transitions and consequences are closed:

| `SEND_COMMITTED` | `SEND_COMMITTED` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | current authority and safe-retransmission proof cover every prior child and exact effect; allocate the next retained ordinal atomically; parent ambiguity does not reset |
| `SENT` | `SENT` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | same guard and atomic child allocation; prior send evidence remains visible |
| `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` | `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | same guard and atomic child allocation; retransmission cannot clear or weaken uncertainty |
| `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` | `CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` | later authenticated evidence establishes the whole effect, permanent incapability, or a fully known mixed bundle; otherwise state remains denying |
| `PARTIAL` | `CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` | later evidence establishes all-effect, no-effect, or exact mixed outcome; otherwise state remains denying |

`CONFIRMED`, `FAILED_NO_EFFECT`, `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND`, and `SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND` are terminal for the original attempt; confirmation is only of the exact typed provider/effect claim. `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` preserves the exact occurred/non-occurred sub-effects and keeps dependent planning/commitment reconciliation visible. `FAILED_NO_EFFECT` requires affirmative permanent-incapability evidence for every transmission child and permits a new intent under current authority. Ordinary retry or a new intent is prohibited from `SENT`, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, or `PARTIAL`; only the closed child-allocation rule permits a still-covered idempotent retransmission as continuation of the same attempt. The sole exception is a distinct `AUTHORIZE_DUPLICATE_RISK` command from the authenticated principal after a preview identifies every unresolved prior attempt, possible duplicate effect, affected party/resource, commitment consequence, and safer alternative. It creates a separately fingerprinted recovery intent under fresh current authority, links but never supersedes or clears the original ambiguous attempt, and cannot be inferred from fallback, prior mandate, silence, or generic confirmation.

`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and `PARTIAL` are deny states: Chiplog stops dependent and remaining batch steps, blocks a conflicting replacement effect unless the exact `AUTHORIZE_DUPLICATE_RISK` exception above is committed, preserves unresolved commitments, and seeks provider reads or human evidence. That exception authorizes only its displayed recovery intent; the original deny state continues to block every other dependent or conflicting action. Silence or a pre-agreed fallback cannot clear the state or continue the chain. Each confirmed partial effect remains independently visible. Compensation is a new previewed `ExternalActionIntent` with its own `ExternalActionAttemptRevision`, authority, fingerprint, dispatch boundary, failure/unknown state, and receipt; a separate recovery overlay may say `COMPENSATION_PENDING | COMPENSATED | COMPENSATION_UNKNOWN | COMPENSATION_PARTIAL` while preserving the original terminal/nonterminal outcome and claim history.

The recovery surface persists until an authorized terminal resolution. It states what was intended, sent, confirmed, unknown or externally visible; what was stopped; which obligations remain; the safe next actions; and the evidence or actor able to resolve ambiguity. Escalation and review deadlines may change ownership or keep a hold, but timeout never clears uncertainty, releases a commitment, or resumes dispatch. Revocation, provisional hold, unresolved conflict, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, and `PARTIAL` take deny precedence over user-specific conflict policy, stale queues, fallback rules, or “latest instruction.” The exact storage/outbox implementation remains proposed, but these observable product outcomes are adopted.

## Multi-party coordination and privacy

Chiplog always has one explicit principal; it is not a neutral representative of everyone involved. There is no cross-principal canonical intention-line root. Each agent retains a separately principal-owned root. `SharedCoordination` has an internal immutable identity but exposes only pairwise or audience/epoch-scoped pseudonymous handles designed to resist cross-audience correlation under a named threat model. They are not absolutely unlinkable: Chiplog as issuer, colluding audiences, timing/membership patterns, and parties with auxiliary data may still correlate them, so each release profile measures linkage risk. Its append-only revisions record participant-attributed membership and only bindings already authorized by each principal's local binding revision. Each released field has an owner, reader set, purpose, retention, and onward-disclosure rule; local-to-shared release requires that owner's authority. No actor may create or rebind another principal's local root, and access granted to one principal or provider is non-transitive. Revocation stops future access, rotates exposed handles, and leaves only party-scoped history justified by an independent retention basis. Personal agents may exchange bounded proposals and attributed evidence through this contour while retaining private context and separate authority boundaries. No remote proposal, evidence item, provider object, or accepted shared record becomes a local planning head; it can only support a separately authorized local command. Divergent local states remain visibly attributed rather than being resolved by last writer or provider state.

In a dispute, no principal's interpretation becomes shared truth by default. Chiplog preserves separately attributed positions and evidence while acting expressly for its own principal. It may challenge the principal privately and use the principal's position for personal planning under the contextual hedge above. In authorized external communication it advocates that position as strongly as possible without deliberately disclosing or signaling private disagreement, under the exact two-contour mandate and factual conduct duties above. It changes another party's expectations only through explicit communication or accepted shared records. Internal reclassification and partisan representation cannot settle what another person promised, accepted, or understood, and an external or shared projection remains `DISPUTED` or `contested reliance` until its governing basis resolves the conflict.

An affected-party assertion, its evidence, a standing or legitimacy assessment, and an operative constraint are distinct. Each has provenance and is contestable. Ambiguous standing blocks only actions that could prejudice the disputed party; otherwise Chiplog presents the uncertainty and seeks clarification rather than silently treating either side's interpretation as canonical.

Chiplog does not adjudicate contested standing. It records every named source of constraint—an accepted shared record, explicit party agreement, external rule, or the principal's adopted policy—with `ASSERTED | ACCEPTED | DISPUTED | ESTABLISHED` applicability, evidence, scope, and contest route, and applies it as operative only at the status the governing relationship permits. Mere attribution never makes it operative. When sources conflict or no deciding rule exists, it preserves both positions and escalates; pending resolution it blocks only irreversible or expectation-changing actions that could harm the disputed party, while reversible private planning remains available. Product classification never creates a right or extinguishes one.

The claimant bears the policy-defined minimum evidence burden before a precautionary block begins. Where contact is feasible and safe, an affected person receives notice, an accessible view of the attributed claim and evidence used, and a channel to correct or contest it. The block names its scope, expiry, anti-abuse rule, and resolver—an agreed party process, applicable external authority, or a human decision explicitly permitted by the governing relationship. It cannot renew from the same evidence.

A missing resolver, expired process deadline, or product failure never extinguishes the attributed claim. If no credible risk of irreversible prejudice remains, the product-imposed hold expires while the unresolved dispute stays visible. If such risk remains, Chiplog narrows the hold to the minimum reversible protection, escalates to the named outside review path, and schedules a time-bounded reassessment; it cannot silently grant the principal the disputed advantage merely because its own resolution process failed.

The external coordination contour is default-deny. An authenticated visitor receives a sender-constrained, audience-restricted, event/purpose-scoped authorization credential bound to that visitor, principal, date window, expiry, fixed resolution, release snapshot, query budget, and permitted result. It uses proof of possession, replay protection, revocation, and protected non-exportable key storage where available; compromise of the bound key/session remains in the threat model. It returns coarse fixed-bucket availability or candidate slots rather than raw busy intervals whenever possible. Arbitrary overlapping windows, exact edge probes, negative “changed/not changed” disclosures, and alias/session budget resets are forbidden; requests across credentials, endpoints, sessions, exports, and cache paths share a correlation-domain disclosure budget. Repeated responses within a release epoch are snapshot-pinned, and uncertain linkage or differencing risk denies or coarsens the response. Query and response telemetry is governed by the same privacy and deletion rules. Event names, task names, descriptions, recurrence patterns, exact private boundaries, and reasons remain unavailable.

Event-specific access additionally requires an authenticated event-scoped participant identity and current participation accepted under the governing invitation rule; forwarding the URL, invitation, or token alone grants no access. The visitor receives a participant-facing projection rather than source-event access: the minimum necessary time/timezone, their own participation status, and a participant-accessible place or expiring link. Every optional title, agenda, instruction, identity, or change field carries author/data-subject provenance, permitted audience, purpose, expiry, and revocation behavior, and requires every authority whose protected data it contains. Pre-disclosure release authorization and evidence that disclosure occurred are distinct. The principal may authorize an eligible field's release but cannot retroactively declare that the participant received or could access it.

Disclosure evidence preserves three distinct claims for the exact field version, audience, and release event: `DELIVERED` means that a provider accepted or delivered the containing communication; `ACCESSIBLE` means that the participant had a technically usable path to that version; `SEEN/ACKNOWLEDGED` means that the participant confirmed awareness of it. Evidence establishes only the level it directly observes: provider telemetry cannot promote delivery or technical access to participant awareness. Acknowledgment establishes awareness only, never consent, acceptance of terms, or waiver of a right unless a separately governed acceptance act explicitly says so. Every downstream consequence names the minimum claim level its governing rule requires; uncertainty is enough to support a safe repeat or clarification, while an action whose legitimacy depends on participant awareness normally requires `SEEN/ACKNOWLEDGED` or a different explicitly governing threshold.

If a confirmed participant contests a disclosure claim, the shared status becomes `DISPUTED` and cannot authorize a consequential action until stronger admissible evidence or an explicit shared or governing resolution settles it. Chiplog may preserve contrary technical evidence, challenge the principal privately, and advocate the principal's position externally under the partisan-representation and no-fabrication boundaries above, but it cannot present that position as the participant's experience. Conflicting release authorizations withhold the field while preserving only minimum logistics. Internal notes, tags, tasks, neighboring-block reasons, hidden attendees, and undeclared identities remain unavailable. New or edited fields never inherit an older field's release authorization or disclosure status. Owner-authored explanation travels as a separate authorized message and does not widen calendar access. Participant links are audience-bound, expiring, revocable, and stripped of unrelated provider identifiers. This data-access boundary is a participation condition and cannot be relaxed by one principal where another person's protected data is involved.

This shared `DISPUTED` status, every affected-party assertion, and every separately attributed commitment or disclosure assessment remains an external-action constraint in its own authoritative family. None is a `ClaimDisposition` and none writes, corrects, or supersedes the principal's personal journal. Conversely, the principal's private claim or disposition cannot erase another party's attributed position, standing, contest, or protective hold. The two contours may be displayed together with explicit attribution but are never reduced into a shared claim head.

Affected-party state is instance-bearing and closed. One affected-party episode identity is allocated by `(attributed party, immutable origin governed node, canonical evidence-or-risk episode kind and parameters)` under the versioned episode-canonicalization registry. Source assertion, assessment, contest, provider, or policy act IDs are never part of this count-once identity: every repeated act that canonicalizes to the same episode is retained as provenance or an alias on the existing episode and cannot allocate a rival family, renew expiry, or add a contribution. If the same canonical key arrives with incompatible episode payload, the named identity resolver deterministically selects `ALIAS_EXISTING`, `DISTINCT_EPISODE` with a newly proven canonical key, or `IDENTITY_DISPUTED`; until then neither candidate becomes an additional operative or counted episode. A changed source ID, wording, provider replay, policy revision, or root/node transformation cannot itself establish `DISTINCT_EPISODE`.

The episode owns one assertion family; applicable assessment, contest, and protective-hold families key from that episode plus their exact policy or risk scope. Each family has exactly one current head selected only by predecessor lineage. An assertion `CORRECT` terminally marks the predecessor head `CORRECTED` and atomically emits one successor `CURRENT` head in the same episode/family with the corrected proposition, correction act, and predecessor link; it must preserve byte-identically the canonical episode key—attributed party, immutable origin governed node, episode kind, and every canonical parameter—and `CORRECTED` is never itself current. Any change to one of those fields is not correction. It must allocate or resolve a distinct canonical episode and use `REPLACE_ASSERTION_EPISODE`, which publishes explicit cross-episode lineage and exhaustively accounts for the predecessor episode's applicable assessment, contest, and protective-hold state. A supersession likewise terminally dispositions the predecessor and atomically emits the exact registered successor head. No timestamp, fresh act ID, or repeated evidence selects currentness.

The total assertion registry is:

Every state not shown as a source in its family's table is terminal and accepts no outgoing event. Unknown family/source/event/target/authority/evidence combinations, stale heads, same-state no-ops, missing successors, or non-atomic predecessor/successor publication reject without a write. For `REPLACE_ASSERTION_EPISODE`, the command enumerates every applicable current assessment, contest, and protective-hold head on the predecessor exactly once as `RETAIN_ON_PREDECESSOR`, `MIGRATE_TO_SUCCESSOR_UNDER_FRESH_AUTHORITY`, or `TERMINAL_DISPOSE`. `RETAIN_ON_PREDECESSOR` leaves that family and its episode independently current and unresolved. `MIGRATE_TO_SUCCESSOR_UNDER_FRESH_AUTHORITY` requires the successor episode's applicable assessment, contest-process, or hold-policy authority and uses that family's registered supersession semantics to terminalize the old head and publish one new applicable head; assertion authority alone cannot migrate it. `TERMINAL_DISPOSE` requires the exact terminal event, resolver, and evidence already admitted by that family's registry and emits no successor. Omission, duplication, stale targeting, an unregistered migration, authority reuse, or a predecessor dependent head left current without `RETAIN_ON_PREDECESSOR` rejects the entire cross-episode replacement. The episode projection deterministically reduces its unique current assertion head and the one applicable current head of each subsidiary family. It is `DISPUTED` when identity or an applicable assessment/contest is disputed; otherwise `OPEN` while the assertion is `CURRENT`, a contest is `OPEN`, or a hold is `ACTIVE`; it is `RESOLVED` only when the assertion head is terminal with no same-episode current successor and every applicable assessment, contest, and hold head is terminal; absence yields `NONE`. A cross-episode successor is counted under its own proven-distinct canonical key, while a predecessor retained with any current dependent family remains a separately unresolved episode. Thus same-episode corrected/superseded predecessors and their successors contribute once as one episode, and no episode becomes terminal while any component remains nonterminal.

The principal controls hold creation through inspectable natural-language policy, including whether Chiplog may create new holds and the admitted risk classes. Disabling or narrowing that policy is prospective: it prevents new `CREATE_HOLD` events but does not silently disposition an existing `ACTIVE` head. An active hold ends only through its registry, or the principal may explicitly acknowledge that they will act despite it. The hold blocks only Chiplog assistance within its exact action scope; it is never a claim of physical, legal, or normative control over the principal. Chiplog may refuse that assistance, while the principal remains able to act independently. Directly principal-reported or otherwise principal-verified conduct despite a hold is recorded as an owner-authored journal fact and leaves the hold, commitment, relying-party consent, closure, and authorization states unchanged. Any later notice, reconciliation, or compensation requires its own authority. A zero-reserve planning choice therefore cannot force held agent assistance, while a hold can never be presented as preventing the principal's own action.

A successor policy must migrate and terminally supersede the old applicable assessment or hold head atomically; the old disputed or active head cannot remain operative beside it. A hold may renew only from a canonically distinct risk episode with materially new evidence satisfying the published reopening threshold, not by changing an ID or repeating the same evidence. History, attribution, contestability, and terminal rationale remain visible.

## Constitutional safeguards

The following are institution-authored participation conditions: the working agreement cannot require Chiplog to violate them, so an otherwise requested action may be refused or narrowed. They do not by themselves authorize an intervention, settle an underlying right, or override another person's agency:

- no high-stakes value decision on the user's behalf;
- no external action without mandate and no silent mandate expansion;
- no inference presented as fact or unsupported promise attributed to another person;
- no concealed action or retroactive rewriting of the audit trail;
- no ordinary-product use of logically deleted data;
- no manipulation or weaponization of private vulnerabilities;
- minimum necessary disclosure between agents;
- no concealed representative endpoint: normal direct rendering identifies the account or endpoint as Chiplog acting for the principal, with exact principal-authored wording distinguished from agent-composed representation where present; no guarantee attaches to later copying, forwarding, quoting, or transformation by another actor;
- no dispatch through a direct channel whose normal rendering cannot expose that representative endpoint identity and preserve the composition distinction at the last reversible boundary;
- no factual fabrication or knowingly false assertion or implication; omission is prohibited when completeness is represented, a direct factual question is answered misleadingly, a named operative disclosure duty is breached, or the omission makes Chiplog's own affirmative representation contextually false; the informed internal model's proportional verification duty includes known contradictions, willful blindness, and reckless disregard, while the external model remains non-investigative and bound to exact execution or private return;
- external-contour calendar minimization as defined above.

Involuntary interruption or intervention requires the separate constitutional-risk contract above and is limited to its narrow, published imminent-irreversible-harm categories. A participation refusal must name the applicable condition and leaves the principal free to choose a lawful course outside Chiplog; it is not evidence that the principal's goal or another person's claim is invalid.

## Interaction model

Conversation remains primary for intent, policy, inspection, correction, and control, while other views may improve legibility. Chiplog is concise by default and increases explanation with ambiguity, risk, or consequence. Proposals state the material action and consequence; receipts make action attempts and observed effects independently inspectable without calling an unknown or partial outcome completed.

Every aggregate state renders first as scoped plain-language sentences, never only internal enums. It says what the principal changed, what remains open, and why: for example, “You abandoned your personal goal. Two work items remain. Your commitment to Alex is still live. Alex's disputed claim currently blocks rescheduling.” Each unresolved component shows its owner or governing authority, personal/external/attributed character, current versus historical status, consequence, blocking condition, and only bounded available actions such as inspect evidence, correct or contest a claim, contact the relying party, propose release, reconcile, or deliberately leave unresolved.

Every consequential utterance follows a legible path: interpret, propose when planning would change, obtain the required authority, then commit or reject/cancel. Inferred and defaulted fields show their source and confidence and never count as user intent. Ambiguity about subject, scope, time, audience, obligation, recurrence, delegation, cadence, or external effect triggers clarification. A factual report from the authenticated principal appends only an owner-authored claim unless the principal separately authorizes a displayed planning delta. External evidence instead produces a displayed verification candidate and no personal claim until the principal directly confirms its exact interpretation. Post-commit correction routes separately to an exact owner-authored claim disposition or a new planning proposal. Every consequential conversation card or receipt uses the same `PLAN`, `FACT CLAIM`, `CANDIDATE EVIDENCE`, or `PROPOSED PLANNING CHANGE` distinction as the journal and states `planning changed: yes/no`.

One adoption gesture can activate only one exact proposal version unless Chiplog displays an explicit bundle with every independently consequential item, per-item selected state, combined consequences, and one unambiguous bundle adoption. A correction result shows the preserved historical claim, the new current epistemic view, whether Plan stayed unchanged, every `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED` node, and the available reconciliation action.

Each rule and authority family has one active version at an explicit effective time. Revision, suspension, or revocation supersedes that head prospectively, and every queued or recurring action must revalidate against the current head before starting. Overlapping versions never resolve by recency alone; if the adopted conflict policy cannot select one, the action remains held and Chiplog asks in natural language.

Journal entries distinguish `PLAN`, owner-authored `FACT CLAIM`, non-canonical `CANDIDATE EVIDENCE`, and `PROPOSED PLANNING CHANGE`; identify provenance and the exact historical/current subject; explain what principal confirmation does and does not establish; show occurred and observed times, uncertainty, candidate conflict and correction lineage; and say explicitly whether planning changed. Available actions remain separate: confirm an exact candidate interpretation, correct, retract, or replace an exact own claim, inspect separately attributed external disagreement, propose a plan change, authorize it, or cancel it. The interface never offers an external actor or provider a personal-journal correction/dispute action.

The initial Telegram journey is an intention-to-personal-plan projection, even though the historical brief called the calendar block a “real commitment.” It demonstrates one proposal-confirmation-receipt flow; explicit confirmation is not the only future authority mechanism.

## Success model

### Adoption readiness

**Operational status: `HOLD_ADOPTION`.** This long-term vision contains neither an approved adoption-readiness record nor an approved `VisionReleaseProfile` and therefore authorizes no production rollout or cohort exposure. Both gates must pass independently; a complete evaluation profile cannot waive implementation readiness.

Canonical clause identity is deterministic rather than maintained as a rival hand-written index. The normative contour consists of the direct paragraphs under `Status and provenance` plus every paragraph, ordered-list item, unordered-list item, and table body row from `Product thesis` through the end of `Evaluation and release governance`. `Reader map`, `Contract index`, `Terminology and closest prior art`, and `Historical seed` are non-normative; `Open design issues` supplies blocker records. Publication parses UTF-8 after rejecting invalid sequences, normalizes line endings to LF, and uses a pinned CommonMark 0.30 plus GFM-table AST profile. Text nodes and heading labels are Unicode NFC; code spans/blocks retain their exact LF-normalized bytes. `VISION_CLAUSE_V1` is SHA-256 over a length-framed serialization of domain tag, vision version, heading-path node types/text, block type, canonical inline/table-cell AST including type tags and boundaries, and occurrence ordinal among byte-identical blocks under that heading. A table row is one parent clause; headings and table headers supply path/schema but are not clauses. Every normative AST leaf must resolve to exactly one parent clause or an explicitly typed non-clausal heading/schema node; an unknown/unclassified block kind fails vision publication. The resulting ordered manifest, generator/profile version and implementation digest, golden canonicalization vectors, and manifest digest are part of the vision release. V1 bootstraps by vision-authority approval of that generator digest and vectors; changing canonicalization creates a new algorithm version and requires old/new manifests plus an explicit migration record—never reinterpretation of existing IDs.

Readiness selects applicable canonical parent IDs, never free-extracts or decomposes substitute normative prose. Each paragraph, list item, or table row remains one indivisible normative conjunction. A `ClauseCoverageLedger` binds that parent to its complete implementation-neutral specification, evidence suite, positive/negative fixtures, and outcomes; those artifacts are non-normative conformance interpretations and can neither add nor narrow meaning. AST node/span references may explain which evidence addresses which text, but create no child requirements and are never used to declare the remainder covered by implication. If full textual entailment or complete-parent coverage is uncertain, the result is `HOLD_ADOPTION` until the canonical parent is clarified by a vision revision; no separate ratification may select meaning outside the text. Conflict always resolves to the canonical parent, and a parent change invalidates its coverage and interpretation. Repeated local consequences remain separate applicable parents unless the vision itself marks one non-normative. A changed applicable normative block creates a new ID and invalidates its bound approvals; a change wholly outside the normative contour does not. Moving a clause changes its heading-path identity and ID; there is no discretionary identity-preserving relocation.

A `ReleaseCapability` is the sole unit of readiness applicability: one stable, versioned governed surface, either a cohort-visible outcome or an internal dependency such as a shared service, model, data/export/recovery path, or provider adapter. Each declares entry points, externally and internally observable effects, data flows, providers, dependencies, disabled paths, and every parent cohort in which it may be exercised. A capability boundary cannot be chosen merely to omit a requirement or failure path. Before applicability is enumerated, an evidence reviewer independent of the capability owner and readiness decision-committer produces an immutable capability-surface attestation. It reconciles the declaration against implementation-derived inventories of reachable entry points, effects, call/data/provider/export/recovery paths, feature and configuration routes, dependency manifests, and disabled-path enforcement. Every unmatched surface, missing inventory, or unverifiable completeness claim becomes an applicability atom and yields `HOLD_ADOPTION`; the readiness decision-committer cannot author or waive this attestation.

The applicability record contains the exact capability/version and cohort, direct normative requirements, and the least fixed point over versioned typed dependency edges through shared services, models, tools, providers, data paths, recovery paths, exports, and other capabilities. Every reachable dependency has exactly one disposition: an active compatible approval as its own `ReleaseCapability`, or complete absorption of its implementation and surface into the parent assessment with no independently exercised route. Every reachable dependency contributes its applicable requirement atoms. Readiness is evaluated over the condensation DAG: an acyclic dependency must have an active compatible `ReadinessApproval`, while a dependency cycle is one exact strongly connected readiness unit keyed by `exact member versions × typed internal edges × cohort/exposure-context vector`. A unit may be approved only after the least fixed point over all members and every outgoing reachable dependency stabilizes and each external dependency has an active compatible approval or valid absorption. The decision-committer then atomically issues one immutable `ReadinessUnitApproval` binding the key plus those external approval IDs and compatibility contexts. The same unit approval satisfies prospective internal member-edge predicates and projects `READY` only into parent contexts contained in its vector; no per-member ordering or waiver exists. Any new parent cohort, context mapping, reachable route, member, edge, evidence, or dependency change invalidates the entire unit and recomputes the fixed point. An unresolved or partially approved cycle fails closed. Evidence must prove every declared exclusion unreachable. Uncertain linkage, indirect exercise, incomplete dependency discovery, incompatible cohort/version, or an unready dependency includes the requirement and yields `HOLD_ADOPTION`; absence removes applicability only when the path is technically absent or provably unreachable, not merely hidden from the interface. Open issues and blockers bind exact capability IDs and requirement atoms rather than informal “profiles.”

Selection completeness is carried by a versioned `ApplicabilityRuleRegistry`, not reviewer memory. Its closed domain is the Cartesian product of every canonical parent clause and every atom in the exact capability/cohort/exposure-context fixed point. Each pair has exactly one current result: `APPLIES` or `PROVABLY_UNREACHABLE`, with rule version, governing textual basis, and exclusion evidence; there is no default. Every `APPLIES` parent enters readiness automatically. Every unclassified, conflicting, stale, or weakly evidenced pair yields `HOLD_ADOPTION`. Rules are proposed by the product/vision authority, checked against the independent surface attestation by the readiness evidence reviewer, and bound by digest into the approval; neither reviewer nor committer may silently add an exclusion. A parent, surface atom, context, rule, or evidence change recomputes all affected pairs and invalidates every dependent readiness unit until closure is restored.

The capability-surface attester, readiness evidence reviewer, and readiness decision-committer are three distinct roles, each independent from capability and implementation owners, release constraint/reviewer/approver roles, product, growth, model-training, experiment, launch, and the delivery chain. None may review its own artifacts. The readiness decision-committer has veto only over implementation/conformance readiness; it cannot waive a product guarantee, settle an affected party's right, approve the separate release profile, or turn missing evidence into acceptance.

### Evaluation and release governance

Exposure has exactly two modes under one monotone `DeploymentGateGeneration`: `evaluation_eligible := current READY ∧ current EvaluationAuthorization.ACTIVE` for the authorization's exact cohort, cap, duration, purpose, instrumentation, and stop rules; and `production_eligible := current READY ∧ unbreached current VisionReleaseProfile.ACTIVE` for the exact production capability/cohort. “Unbreached current” means the exact active head is unexpired, every `[LIVE]` predicate and freshness lease is currently `PASS`, its authoritative evidence cursors match, and its scoped `OpenCauseSet` is empty. A path may omit its own check only when the attestation proves it is downstream of another checked boundary with no independent start, replay, or fan-out route; an unmatched effect boundary invalidates readiness. Evaluation-originated subjects, state, authority, and entitlements cannot cross into production without a fresh production-bound check.

Readiness invalidation, a committed hard-harm determination, stale or interrupted predicate lease, unresolved evidence gap, release hold, revocation, expiry, or supersession advances the generation before further exposure and makes the corresponding eligibility predicate false; a stale worker is fenced. Evidence sources unable to meet the profile's latency bound force the implicated path to `HELD`. Work already across the declared boundary is reported and contained, not promised recalled. Every hold records which gate, binding, or post-launch condition produced it. A committed checklist breach makes `ACTIVE` non-current before or atomically with containment, so a held profile can never satisfy production eligibility. Neither gate transition can issue, modify, or waive the other.

Success is judged only for intention lines the user has explicitly entrusted to Chiplog's managed contour, not for every passing statement and not by raw completion. Each delegation contributes its exact current registry state and is resolved only when reconciliation has atomically terminalized the undertaking and committed exactly one registered source consequence; acceptance alone never contributes a resolved source. Every affected-party episode contributes once under its canonical evidence-or-risk episode identity while its assertion, applicable assessment, contest, and protective hold retain attributed standing, one deterministic current projection, terminal disposition, and addressability through migrations and transformations; repeated source acts are provenance, not new contributions. Reactivation always uses a fully reviewed successor profile or authorization; no `HELD → ACTIVE` transition exists. The canonical transition registry is total:

The versioned `ReleasePredicateRegistry` contains the exact profile-row IDs below plus `REL-STATE-CURRENT`, `REL-EVAL-ELIGIBLE`, `REL-PROD-ELIGIBLE`, `REL-GATE-FRESHNESS`, and every transition guard in the canonical registry. Each entry binds phase, exact inputs, whole-conjunction evaluator, `PASS | FAIL | MISSING | STALE` result domain, authoritative cursor/freshness lease where applicable, failure-cause type, and evaluator/version digest. A row is `PASS` only when its entire pass-condition conjunction is proven; no child interpretation may waive a conjunct. The frozen profile and every authorization/approval bind the exact registry digest and results. The table is the authored reviewer-facing projection of those stable row IDs, not a separate predicate source.

Concurrent readiness invalidations and release/evaluation holds serialize against the exact `DeploymentGateGeneration`. Each winning transition appends a stable cause identity, scope, source cursor, and evidence to an immutable `CauseLedger`; a loser retries against the successor generation, so simultaneous causes coalesce without erasure. A separately authorized `CauseResolution` binds one exact cause, resolving authority, evidence or changed scope, decision time, and successor gate generation. `OpenCauseSet` is the deterministic projection `introduced causes − currently valid resolutions`, scoped to the capability/cohort/mode; history is never removed. No successor `READY`, evaluation authorization, or production activation may clear a cause implicitly: its approval must inherit the ledger and bind every resolution it relies on. Eligibility requires the scoped `OpenCauseSet` to be empty. Concurrent resolutions also CAS the exact generation; a stale resolution retries and cannot erase a newly introduced or differently scoped cause.

The constraint owner is organizationally and permission-wise separate from growth, model-training, experiment, and launch owners. Appointment and removal belong to a differently constituted oversight authority that includes affected-cohort representation. Changes that weaken a threshold or measurement obligation require that same outside authority and cannot take effect in the release cycle or experiment they govern. A hold always names scope, owner, deadline, exit evidence, and escalation; it never means continuing breached behavior without cohort expansion. Full rollout never converts approval into permanence. These adopted product-governance requirements are distinct from the standalone's proposed `OperationalAdoptionProfile` schema and Annex C realization.

## Terminology and closest prior art

Pairwise pseudonymous handles follow the risk-reduction pattern of [OpenID Connect pairwise subject identifiers](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0-18.html#PairwiseAlg); they do not claim absolute unlinkability or anonymity. Sender-constrained visitor credentials are closest to proof-of-possession mechanisms such as [OAuth DPoP (RFC 9449)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9449.html) and inherit their key/session-compromise limits. The Plan/Fact/Journal split is a product-domain authority boundary, not a novelty claim about event sourcing, CQRS, audit logs, or workflow systems.

## Historical seed: the MVP

The stream brief specified the intended smallest auditable action loop: an allowed owner expresses an intention in Telegram; Chiplog proposes a precise calendar action; explicit confirmation authorizes one intended-idempotent Google Calendar write; a journal links request, approval, and provider identity; and Chiplog returns a receipt. No implementation version, evaluation artifact, provider-condition test, or observed result is cited here, so this is historical design intent rather than evidence that the loop was built or validated.

The brief aimed to establish proposal before that write, explicit authority, idempotency, and a checkable trace. Even future evidence for that exact loop would validate only its named version and provider conditions—not the long-term action, receipt, authority, privacy, or rollout contracts in this vision. It does not freeze Google Calendar as the core model, explicit confirmation as the only authority mechanism, single-user operation as the target scope, or MVP exclusions as permanent exclusions.
