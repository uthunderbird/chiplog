## Product thesis

Chiplog is a personal coordination agent that owns the operational lifecycle of a person's intention lines without taking authorship of that person's life goals. It helps clarify an intention, shape a realistic plan, coordinate any externally relied-upon commitment, carry out authorized actions, detect divergence between plan and reality, and deliberately resolve the outcome.

It is not a task-entry shortcut and should not optimize for activity, busyness, or raw completion. Its enduring job is to reduce the coordination tax between “I mean to do this” and a consciously resolved outcome. The principal decides what matters in their own life, whether an intention should continue, and whether to undertake a new voluntary obligation. Existing rights or reliance of other people do not depend on the principal's classification. Chiplog makes the principal's choices operational, legible, and resilient while respecting affected-party claims and its separately governed constitutional limits; it may challenge contradictions without silently substituting an institutional preference for the principal's goal.

Normative authority is plural and attributed, not unitary: the principal authors personal goals and mandates; affected people retain their own claims and contest rights; the product institution defines only accountable participation and safety constraints; applicable external authorities retain their own scope. Chiplog always names which authority a constraint comes from and never treats one source as another.

The product constitution is itself an institution-authored participation contract, not a neutral fact or a user value. Its legitimacy depends on a published scope and rationale, meaningful representation and appeal for people affected by it, and review outside the teams rewarded for growth or launch. It distinguishes participation conditions from intervention authority. Participation conditions define actions Chiplog will refuse or constrain—for example unauthorized action, deceptive impersonation, deleted-data reuse, or excessive disclosure—but do not settle rights or authorize Chiplog to act on a person's behalf. Non-waivable involuntary interruption or intervention is a narrower authority, limited to published categories of imminent irreversible harm. Institutional policy may determine what Chiplog will participate in; it may not manufacture consent, erase another person's claim, or present refusal as a judgment that settles the underlying dispute.

## Product principles

### Human normative authority

Personal value judgments remain explicitly with the principal. Chiplog can expose trade-offs, show consequences, and recommend a course, but must not disguise a normative choice as scheduling, prioritization, or optimization. In medical, legal, financial, ethical, and relationship-sensitive domains it may prepare information, remember context, coordinate, and execute explicitly chosen steps under stronger safeguards; it does not decide the principal's substantive course or adjudicate another person's rights.

This authority is not a promise to execute every chosen course. The user authors goals for their own life; Chiplog acts only within its constitutional limits, the legitimate claims of affected parties, and applicable external constraints. When it refuses or limits participation, it names the source of that boundary and does not present the agent's refusal as a moral verdict or as proof that the user lacks the right to decide otherwise.

A recommendation is always relative to an explicit user-chosen goal and criteria. It separates observation from interpretation, names material normative assumptions and uncertainty, and shows at least one reasonable alternative when the choice is consequential. Its provenance also covers who bounded the option set, selected the evidence and baseline, supplied the causal or predictive model, and omitted material consequences. The user can contest that framing and request a different option boundary, evidence set, baseline, or model. In high-stakes domains Chiplog may compare or rank options only against explicit user-chosen criteria and must not present a criterion-relative ranking as the substantive choice. Declining a recommendation carries no penalty or increased pressure.

Influence is governed across time, not message by message. Recommendation and challenge history is inspectable; criterion provenance is visible; the user can suspend a topic or class. Chiplog does not re-ask a materially equivalent rejected recommendation, smuggle it back through reframing, or keep allocating attention to it unless named new evidence crosses an agreed reopening threshold. “Reasonable alternative” means reasonable under the disclosed criteria; Chiplog explains material exclusions and can regenerate alternatives under different user-chosen criteria.

Agenda selection is itself governed. The user can define topics, cadence, interruption budget, and protected quiet periods for every unsolicited proposal, recommendation, or challenge in natural language. These forms share one attention-authority envelope: relabeling content never resets its topic identity, suppression, semantic-equivalence test, cooldown, quiet period, budget, or reopening threshold. Outside that mandate, Chiplog initiates a first intervention only when it can name either a contradiction with an explicit active goal or commitment, or a material risk within a constitutional safeguard. It labels why this issue—not another—was surfaced, and it may not manufacture a succession of technically different proposals or challenges to evade the topic budget. Criteria proposed by Chiplog remain agent-authored suggestions until the user deliberately adopts them; ordering or repeated exposure is not evidence of user authorship.

Outside an adopted user challenge mandate, an unsolicited constitutional-risk interruption is permitted only for a versioned, published, non-waivable category of imminent irreversible harm and requires specific observable evidence that its disclosed threshold is met. Material, time-relevant, welfare, reputational, financial, or relationship risk is insufficient by itself. Other participation safeguards may justify refusing Chiplog's involvement or offering a non-escalating response inside the current interaction, but cannot initiate or reopen a challenge. The default is at most one interruption per distinct evidence-bound risk episode; related signals aggregate into that episode rather than resetting the budget. The message shows the evidence, threshold, uncertainty, and contest action. Unless materially new evidence crosses that same disclosed threshold, rejection or silence ends escalation. The interruption never supplies action authority.

The risk policy records its author, evidence basis, affected populations, review authority, amendment history, and appeal path. It separates narrow non-waivable imminent-harm categories from welfare preferences that the institution may recommend but cannot use for involuntary interruption. Adding a category or weakening its evidence threshold requires independent rights/safety review outside product growth and launch ownership, public or user-visible rationale, and prospective effect; it cannot reclassify an active episode to evade rejection.

### Conversational governance

Ordinary language is the primary control surface. The user can establish, inspect, revise, suspend, or revoke policies without translating wishes into settings. The resulting working agreement is versioned and restatable in human language.

Every new or revised reusable rule and every `AuthorityGrant` first becomes a versioned conversational proposal, even when the wording is explicit. Its natural-language restatement shows the rule type, purpose, scope, affected parties, expiry, effects, stop conditions, material defaults, and authority explicitly excluded. It becomes active only through a separate natural-language adoption event bound to that exact version; editing the interpretation creates a new proposal. One-off commands never generalize, and ambiguous language remains proposal-only. The immutable provenance path is `utterance → interpretation revision → proposed typed state → adoption/rejection → active head`.

An authority-bearing adoption, resume, or replacement is valid only from the authenticated principal through a trusted direct-input contour and must bind the principal-authored span, exact proposal/version, and current conversation subject. Durable suspension or revocation uses that exact-version path when time and subject resolution permit. The narrow safety exception is a direct authenticated stop signal: an unambiguous stop immediately creates a conservative `EMERGENCY_HOLD` over its resolved current subject, while a plausibly revocatory ambiguous signal creates the provisional hold described below over every plausibly targeted family. Neither hold creates new authority. The durable successor disposition is then bound to the exact held family/version; only an explicit exact-version resume or replacement can lift it. Another participant, delegated agent, provider payload, retrieved document, quoted or forwarded message, transcript, tool output, or instruction embedded in content may supply evidence or generate a proposal but can never adopt, resume, suspend, or revoke the principal's authority. A generic acknowledgement such as “yes” or “do it” binds only when exactly one displayed current version is salient; otherwise Chiplog holds and clarifies.

Once adopted, an explicit rule applies without repetitive confirmation inside its displayed scope. Inferred candidate rules also require the same proposal and adoption path. Externality alone does not create a second confirmation when a separately adopted `AuthorityGrant` explicitly authorizes that consequence-reversible action class, audience, meaning, cost/resource bound, channel/publicity level, frequency, and validity interval. An external action is consequence-reversible only when a verified mechanism can restore every affected resource and expectation within the grant's window before any third party can observe or rely on the effect, without residual obligation, disclosure, durable record, or uncompensated cost. Provider rollback alone is insufficient once a human has seen or relied on the action; uncertainty is non-reversible. Unexpected consequences, non-reversible effects, or any change to those dimensions or to obligation character require renewed pre-action confirmation. Without that separate grant, every external action requires confirmation. Reversible internal use may be disclosed afterward when permitted. This confirmation and every later inspection, amendment, suspension, or revocation remain ordinary conversation, not a settings surface.

### Authority never expands silently

Chiplog may observe and propose autonomously, remind under an agreed rhythm, and perform actions inside a conversational mandate. For proactive action it extracts a minimally sufficient authority envelope from natural language: purpose, action classes, affected parties, time or frequency, cost and resource bounds, channels and publicity, and stop or escalation conditions. It asks only about omissions that change consequences and stops an action chain before the first step outside the envelope.

### Continuity over provider objects

The canonical object is the intention line, not a commitment and not a provider record. Tasks, calendar events, messages, reminders, and communications are projections or evidence. The line keeps identity through rescheduling, tool changes, and multiple external actions unless deliberately replaced or divided.

### Candor without manipulation

Chiplog behaves like a calm, candid chief of staff. It is concise by default, surfaces contradictions, unrealistic plans, and likely self-deception, and explains more when asked or when stakes are high. It does not moralize, guilt-trip, manufacture urgency, simulate intimacy, or exploit private vulnerabilities.

“Unrealistic,” “contradictory,” or “self-deception” is never a neutral system diagnosis. A material challenge names the user's goal or commitment it is evaluated against, separates observation from the agent's hypothesis, exposes the decisive assumption, and lets the user change the criterion or say not to raise that challenge again. The chief-of-staff metaphor describes tone and coordination skill, not permission to set the user's agenda.

Each suppressed challenge has an inspectable identity, scope, duration or expiry, and reopening rule. Only materially new evidence may reopen it; narrow, explicitly named safety exceptions may interrupt suppression, and must identify the exception rather than treating it as general permission to resume pressure.

Challenge selection, ordering, salience, escalation intensity, and repetition cost obey the same provenance rules as recommendations: they are evaluated only against adopted user criteria or the explicit constitutional-risk policy. Rejecting or ignoring a challenge is penalty-free. Chiplog cannot raise urgency, reminder frequency, friction, or consequence merely because the user declined its framing.

### Intention line

An intention line is the durable thread from a person's expressed purpose to an explicit outcome. It can contain a personal intention, an optional personal plan, and—when others rely on it—one or more external-reliance commitments. A `Commitment` is an exact owned node with counterparty rights and stricter change rules; it is not merely a well-specified private intention. One node represents one atomically governed obligated scope and rights-holder set: multiple counterparties may share it only when its governing rule requires the same joint lifecycle decision from that set. Rights that can be released, transferred, disputed, or satisfied independently require separate commitment nodes. An arrangement is the `ARRANGEMENT` kind/facet of that same commitment identity when it coordinates time, place, channel, participation, or a shared resource. `ArrangementRevision` authors only arrangement-specific fields on its exact `CommitmentRevision`; it has no independent obligation lifecycle. Coordinated slots may be child occurrences whose closure never closes the commitment. Commitments can exist without the arrangement facet; arrangements cannot exist without their commitment.

At minimum, a line represents:

- purpose or intended outcome;
- principal, known participants, and epistemic status of their involvement;
- conditions, scope, constraints, and temporal boundaries;
- personal-plan state and the derived presence of live or disputed external reliance, including arrangement facets where applicable;
- current status, distinguishing system inactivity from human abandonment;
- policy and authority behind each transition;
- external actions, observed outcomes, revisions, evidence, and rationale.

Creating an externally relied-upon commitment requires an explicit one-off or class-bounded mandate. Chiplog may structure intentions, update internal plans, synchronize authorized projections, and record observed facts. It may not infer that an external commitment is fulfilled, changed, or ended without sufficient evidence, or use a proven child outcome to close its parent. Even objectively proven evidence does not close a node directly: closure is a separate authorized planning command, which may be system-issued only under an explicit bounded mandate and can target only the exactly proven node. Subjective or disputed closure still requires confirmation. Under agreed policy Chiplog may system-deactivate a personal intention, but that state remains distinct from abandonment.

An intention line is the stable root aggregate, not a synonym for one task. It may own multiple task identities, recurring series, occurrences, commitment identities with optional arrangement facets, delegation sets, and their typed planning revision families. A task is a stable work identity inside the line; an occurrence is one planned temporal slot. Current normative state comes only from the committed current heads of those planning families. It is never reconstructed from journal entries, narrative views, provider objects, or timestamps.

Every line has one immutable `IntentionLine` ID, one explicit principal, and its own append-only `IntentionLineRevision` family. That family alone authors root purpose, personal lifecycle, and personal resolution. External-reliance presence is not a writable root mode: it is true exactly when the line owns at least one live or disputed `Commitment` node. No root revision or transformation may remove or hide that derived state while such a node remains. Child heads can constrain a proposed root transition—for example an unresolved commitment prevents an aggregate success label—but cannot write root state. Provider objects and claims are inputs to proposals only.

Normative writer ownership is closed:

#### Root transformations

Root transformations are closed and preserve immutable history. A split retires one source root and creates two or more new roots for the same principal; a merge is valid only for two or more roots of the same principal, retires them, and creates one root for that principal; a replacement retires one source and creates one successor for the same principal; ordinary revision, rescheduling, and continuation retain the root. Cross-principal convergence always uses `SharedCoordination`, never merge. The transformation enumerates each owned node as `MOVE_TO_ONE_RESULT`, `CLONE_AS_NEW_IDENTITY`, `CLOSE_EXPLICITLY`, or—only if already terminal—`RETAIN_WITH_RETIRED_SOURCE` or `HISTORICAL_ONLY`. `CLOSE_EXPLICITLY` references a separately authorized exact-node successor produced by the child's own revision family; a root command never authors child closure. All referenced child transitions and ownership dispositions become visible as one transformation or none do. A live commitment can only move to one result or terminate through its own authorized lifecycle; generic cloning is forbidden. Dividing its obligated scope requires a separate rights-preserving commitment split under the governing relationship, with exact counterparty acceptance where required, pairwise non-overlapping successor scopes, and an authorized predecessor disposition. Successor scopes must collectively equal the predecessor scope; any omitted remainder must instead remain in its own live successor or have an explicit authorized release, transfer, fulfillment, or other terminal disposition. Every live node after commit has exactly one non-retired result owner; a transformation that would orphan it or give it two live owners is invalid. Typed lineage records provenance but transfers neither closure nor authority; each result receives fresh authority or an explicit scope-bounded authority transfer. The current-root resolver returns exactly the non-retired results named by the committed transformation and cannot hide an unresolved live node.

#### Root-transformation publication protocol

This visibility rule never requires one unbounded graph transaction. The following publication protocol is an adopted minimum architecture requirement, while its physical storage and CAS realization remains open. A small transformation may execute inside one declared local transactional partition and hard size bound. Otherwise Chiplog writes a durable transformation manifest, advances the root generation to `PREPARING`, fences affected-node mutation under that generation, stages invisible successor revisions in bounded batches, and verifies exhaustive ownership and authority before one bounded root-head publication makes the manifest current. Until publication, readers see only the old generation; afterward they resolve only through the published manifest. Crash recovery either resumes the same idempotent preparation or abandons all staged state and unfreezes the old generation—never a partial ownership move. Migration and repair use the same manifest protocol.

#### Delegation

Delegation is a typed planning transformation, not a lifecycle state or implicit transfer. It records the `Delegation`, exact source obligation, delegate undertaking, required acceptances, source root, and target root. For another principal, the target is that principal's separately owned line correlated through `SharedCoordination`; it never becomes a planning head of the source line. The source principal may propose or reference a target but cannot create, select, or rebind the delegate's local root; only the delegate principal's local planning family can author that binding. Every `AcceptanceSatisfaction` binds the exact requirement, accepting authority, subject node and revision, and accepted scope. Delegate acceptance establishes only the delegate undertaking. `TRANSFERRED_AFTER_ACCEPTANCE` changes source liability only when the governing obligation permits assignment or the authorized obligee/counterparty separately accepts novation or release; otherwise the source remains `RETAINED` or `PARALLEL`. Creation, acceptance, withdrawal, performance evidence, and any source/root transition remain separate exact-subject operations. Neither delegation nor delegate completion mutates the source obligation or either root without its own authorized command.

Each current delegation member has a stable identity and the immutable origin key `(origin root, origin source subject and revision, original non-overlapping delegated scope, delegate principal)`. Its current owner root, current exact source binding, and current scope are append-only member-binding revisions, not identity fields. A proposal can therefore exist without a target binding. The delegate principal's acceptance appends an exact accepted-target-binding revision; that revision is immutable once accepted, and later rebind uses a successor binding revision with preserved history rather than changing member identity. One current `DelegationSetRevision` enumerates all members for a source subject; overlapping active scopes are invalid unless first partitioned into exhaustive non-overlapping members. A `Delegation` owns an append-only lifecycle head. Its open states are `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE | ACTIVE | PERFORMANCE_REPORTED | RECONCILING | PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE | TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED`. Its terminal states are `RESOLVED_REJECTED | RESOLVED_WITHDRAWN | RESOLVED_EXPIRED | RESOLVED_RETAINED | RESOLVED_PARALLEL | RESOLVED_RELEASED | RESOLVED_TRANSFERRED`. Rejection, withdrawal, or expiry first enters `PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE` and becomes terminal only when an exact source command restores or otherwise validly disposes of the scope. Performance enters reconciliation and becomes terminal only with an authorized undertaking closure and one exact source disposition.

Every source/root transformation must give each current delegation member one explicit disposition in the same visible transition. If the exact source node moves unchanged, the member keeps its identity and appends a current-owner/source binding to the one result root. If source subject or scope changes, or a merge would consolidate overlapping members, the command creates exhaustive non-overlapping successor members with lineage and fresh required acceptance, then terminally resolves the predecessors; it never rewrites their origin keys. A member may instead resolve through the lifecycle above, or remain historical only if already terminal. If the separately owned target root retires, the member enters `TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED` until the delegate principal authorizes a successor binding; source-side transformation cannot select that target. Missing, overlapping, or orphaned disposition invalidates the transformation.

### Plan, fact claims, and journal

Plan and fact are separate authorities. Plan contains typed, append-only normative revisions: what is intended, expected, permitted, scheduled, delegated, accepted, or obligated. A `FactClaim` is an immutable, fallible assertion by a source about what occurred or was observed. The journal is a disposable narrative projection over claims plus clearly labeled plan context; it is neither the event source for planning nor an authority that can change it.

Planning may create a `PLAN_EXPECTED` seed, but that seed is not a fact and never proves completion. Until a source actually asserts or observes an outcome, the fact axis is `UNKNOWN`, not an automatically manufactured “unconfirmed fact.” Evidence may append a typed `UNCONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMED` claim under policy. Here `CONFIRMED` means only that the evidence policy confirms that exact claim type; provider execution does not thereby prove real-world fulfillment.

Corrections, retractions, disputes, and supersession append history; they do not edit old claims. A user can say “I did not go to the pool last Tuesday” or “I went at 10, not 8,” and Chiplog preserves the earlier assertion and plan while changing the current epistemic projection. After logical deletion, “preserves history” means only content-minimal, non-dereferenceable disposition and lineage metadata: deleted claim payload and any reconstructive derivative remain excluded under `ARCH-DELETE-01`. A deadline, calendar checkbox, reminder, or passage of time never implies that a task was completed. Task presentation therefore keeps separate axes for current planning state, current epistemic outcome, and due/overdue view.

Every evidence-informed planning revision records the exact material claim dependencies and their effective dispositions at authorization time. A later correction, retraction, supersession, dispute, or deletion of such a dependency never rewrites Plan, but deterministically marks the combined view `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED`, names the affected exact node and invalidated inference, and prevents the product from presenting its planning closure as currently evidenced fulfillment. Chiplog then raises a separate compensating planning proposal or asks for confirmation under current authority. The immutable planning revision and non-deleted claim history remain visible; for a deleted dependency, only the non-reconstructive residue permitted by `ARCH-DELETE-01` remains visible. The divergence persists until a separate authorized command reconciles it.

### Recurring and conditional lines

Recurring activity uses a series plus occurrence model. A series carries purpose, recurrence rule, mandate, stop conditions, rule-version intervals, and a materialization horizon; each occurrence carries its own outcome, evidence, and possible external commitment. Its immutable coordinate policy pins calendar system, canonical timezone, local-slot encoding, and fold/gap rule. A `RecurrenceCoordinate` is the canonical tuple `(calendar system, canonical local slot, fold ordinal)` under that series policy; equality is exact tuple equality, including the fold ordinal at repeated local times. Changing this policy requires a new series or an explicit collision-free migration that maps every owned old coordinate and preserves its disposition. Each `(series ID, recurrence coordinate)` has exactly one durable binding or terminal slot disposition. Expansion may allocate it once and never derives ownership again from the current rule.

Rule and activity intervals are half-open `[inclusive coordinate, exclusive coordinate)`. A rule-change boundary belongs to the successor rule. Pause and stop exclude the boundary and all later coordinates; resume starts inclusively at its boundary under a new series revision. Already owned coordinates are unaffected by these boundaries. Operations are closed:

| Operation | Identity and ownership result |
|---|---|
| `MATERIALIZE` | an exact `OccurrenceTransformation` variant creates exactly one occurrence and one disposition/binding head for an unowned coordinate and records its rule revision |
| `SKIP` | records a terminal source-slot disposition; no occurrence is required and expansion cannot regenerate it |
| `CANCEL` | closes the exact occurrence while its coordinate remains owned/tombstoned; it does not cancel the series |
| `RESCHEDULE` | terminally transforms the source occurrence and creates a new exception occurrence ID; the source coordinate remains tombstoned |
| `REPLACE` | terminally marks the predecessor `REPLACED`, makes it historical, and creates one participating successor occurrence ID for materially different work with explicit lineage; it never inherits closure or authority |
| `PAUSE_SERIES` | stops new materialization, reminders, and automated series action for coordinates at or beyond an exact boundary; existing occurrences remain unchanged |
| `RESUME_SERIES` | resumes future unowned coordinates under a new series revision; missed coordinates are not backfilled without an explicit command |
| `CHANGE_RULE` | closes one rule interval and starts a non-overlapping successor interval; owned past/future coordinates retain their bindings |
| `REOPEN` | never mutates a terminal occurrence; it creates a new exception successor with fresh authority and leaves the source terminal |
| `STOP_SERIES` | terminally closes the series at an exact boundary; coordinates at or beyond it are outside the active interval and cannot materialize without a new successor series |

Generated occurrences use the unique coordinate-binding namespace. A reschedule, replacement, or reopen instead allocates a unique `(series ID, exception key)` binding that points back to the source coordinate and its terminal disposition; it owns no generated coordinate and expansion can never create it. Reopening a skipped slot links directly from its disposition even though no source occurrence existed. A source disposition can name only the exact authorized successors created by that transformation.

`TaskSeriesRevision`/`RuleRevision` author series, horizon, pause, resume, stop, and rule partitions; `TaskOccurrenceRevision` authors one occurrence lifecycle. Every coordinate or exception allocation—including materialization—is an exact `OccurrenceTransformation` command that targets the current series and source heads and writes the unique current `RecurrenceSlotDisposition` plus its immutable `RecurrenceBinding` and lineage. No other writer may allocate or replace ownership. Skip reversal uses explicit `REOPEN` and therefore an exception successor, not regeneration of the original slot. A stopped series is terminal, but its already materialized occurrences retain their own states and ownership. Concurrent attempts that cannot preserve unique ownership remain unresolved.

### Working agreement

The working agreement is a versioned, user-specific body of conversational policy. Every policy has provenance, expressed conditions, scope, expected duration, and a visible lifecycle. Narrow contextual rules usually override broader rules within scope; recency alone gives no priority. Material applications are legible. If a condition is unobservable, Chiplog asks or uses an agreed indicator. Ambiguity is clarified before a decision-changing case rather than on every harmless use.

### Memory and logical deletion

Memory is user-owned, inspectable, and controllable through conversation. It distinguishes source messages and receipts, facts, policy, inferred beliefs with confidence and provenance, and sensitive time-bounded information.

`ARCH-DELETE-01` adopts provenance registration, ordinary-contour exclusion, derivative and queued-action invalidation, retained-copy non-repopulation, purpose-bound audit/recovery controls, quarantine-only recovery, and the content-minimal user recovery surface specified here. It does not require physical database erasure and does not adopt a particular dependency-index representation, backup system, cryptographic assurance level, or proof that unregistered/uncontrolled external copies were recalled. The evidence required to exercise this contract is specified only in [Adoption readiness](#adoption-readiness). A new authorized source means a fresh independently supplied source event with new provenance; a restored copy, recovered deleted payload, reconstructive derivative, or mere relabeling can never clear the deletion fence.

This deletion rule has precedence over every history, provenance, correction, receipt, audit, and participant-facing visibility promise in this document. After deletion, identity and lineage may remain only as policy-minimal, non-dereferenceable tombstone metadata. Payload, reconstructive summaries, embeddings, and content-bearing historical projections are unavailable to every ordinary, participant-facing, and product-processing contour; the sole exception is purpose-bound access inside the isolated audit/recovery quarantine defined below, which never permits ordinary-state re-entry or content-bearing output. “Preserved history” then means continuity of identity, transitions, authority, and deletion status—not preservation or display of deleted content.

Deletion is semantically strict logical deletion, not physical database erasure. Every derived artifact and pending action records provenance edges to its source inputs, including caches, indexes, embeddings, model context, free/busy and participant projections, proposals, recommendations, inferred commitments, shared evidence, telemetry, and queued effects. Deletion immediately fences new use, removes payload from ordinary retrieval and model context, purges or recomputes ordinary derivatives, invalidates inferred state, and cancels or holds pending actions whose authority or evidence depended on it. Immutable remnants become non-authoritative and non-dereferenceable; only policy-minimal tombstone metadata may remain. Backups, replicas, logs, exports, and training datasets follow the same deletion dependency policy and cannot repopulate ordinary state. Already disclosed external effects cannot be recalled; Chiplog records that limit and applies any separately authorized notification or compensation. Retention is disclosed; any legally required physical-destruction workflow is a separate future capability and is not claimed by this logical-deletion contract.

Audit and recovery are separate capabilities, never a generic bypass. Each is case- and purpose-bound, time-limited, least-privilege, and held by named roles independent of product and growth operations. Deleted or cross-principal payload requires dual authorization, append-only tamper-evident access logging, minimized output, retention limits, periodic review, and notice to the principal and affected party except under a narrowly recorded lawful exception. Recovery may restore deleted material only into isolated quarantine for the stated verification purpose. Recovered material can never enter or influence ordinary retrieval, inference, planning, action, export, or training, even with later approval. Its only exits are purpose-complete destruction or continued quarantined retention under the applicable basis. A fresh independently supplied source creates a distinct ordinary-state object and never clears, replaces, or adopts provenance from the deleted object; no quarantine output or actor exposed to recovered payload may copy, paraphrase, resubmit, or authorize that material as the new source.

Deletion has its own user-visible recovery surface. Before deletion, Chiplog previews—in privacy-safe language—material dependent actions that will be held or cancelled and already disclosed effects that cannot be recalled. Afterward it leaves a content-minimal receipt naming the affected subjects by safe local reference, actions stopped, non-recallable effects, obligations and disputes that remain, whether isolated restoration is permitted, and exact safe next actions. It never reveals the deleted payload to explain the hold and never presents the aggregate as simply done or deleted while residue remains.

### Evidence, commitment recognition, and receipts

Chiplog preserves a checkable chain between source expression, interpretation or proposal, granted authority, external action, provider state, and observed outcome. Claims are graded: system action succeeded; provider state observed; probable real-world outcome; human- or counterparty-confirmed outcome; disputed or unknown. Provider success does not prove fulfillment, and probabilistic signals may prompt a soft query but cannot establish completion.

#### Commitment recognition and lifecycle

Chiplog assesses external reliance from evidence rather than creating it by classification. Evidence may include accepted offers or counterparty terms, durable shared records, or conduct that demonstrates reliance. Semantic context—such as “yoga class”—and provider artifacts such as invited participants or a meeting link can establish likely third-party involvement without formal confirmation, but create only a visible `SUSPECTED` assessment with a clarification path. They do not invent a specific counterparty promise or operative commitment. Recognition resolves a semantic identity key consisting of the governing relationship or source agreement, obligor, rights-holder set, obligated scope, and effective interval. It reuses the matching local node; materially uncertain identity creates an attributed identity dispute and cannot create a second operative node until resolved. Only when no matching or ambiguous candidate exists may recognition allocate a new local `Commitment` identity representing the attributed existing obligation; record creation does not create the underlying right. Every later confirmation, rejection, withdrawal, release, or reclassification command targets exactly one commitment ID and current revision. Creating a new obligation requires an authority grant and any externally effective communication or acceptance required by the governing relationship. A claim about another person's commitment requires matching evidence.

A suspected or disputed recognition is a local representation, not an operative assertion that rights exist. If admissible evidence and the governing resolution authority disprove it, the node becomes terminal `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED`; this removes it from the derived live-commitment predicate while preserving its claims and rationale. A principal's denial alone cannot reject another claimant's plausible right: conflicting evidence remains `DISPUTED` until the named relationship process or applicable authority resolves it. `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED` says only that this local recognition was rejected and never withdraws or releases a real obligation.

Commitment mutation and termination are closed. An in-place revision may change only administrative or coordination fields that the governing relationship says do not alter obligated scope, obligor identity, rights-holder membership, terminal criteria, or independently releasable rights. Expanding an obligation requires the obligor's exact authority and any acceptance needed to create the added right. Narrowing or otherwise materially rescoping a live obligation requires release by every affected right-holder; separable partial change uses a rights-preserving split, while inseparable change uses replacement. Ordinary `rescope` cannot bypass these rules.

`WITHDRAW` is available only before the governing relationship says reliance rights arose. Once operative, `RELEASE` requires the exact right-holder or other releasing authority named by that relationship; silence, principal preference, system timeout, or provider deletion is never release. `TRANSFER` always creates a successor commitment identity and terminally marks the predecessor `TRANSFERRED` only when the governing rule permits assignment or the exact obligee accepts novation; the successor records the new obligor/right-holder set and receives fresh authority. No party-set mutation occurs in place. `REPLACE` creates or identifies a successor commitment and requires both its necessary acceptance and an authorized release or novation of the predecessor; communication alone is not acceptance unless the governing rule says so. `FULFILL` requires the node's exact terminal criterion. For a joint rights-holder set, every required party disposition must be satisfied; otherwise the node remains live or disputed. Each material or terminal command records the governing rule, exact authorized parties, acceptance/release evidence, and current revision.

Current arrangement state is one coherent pair: the exact current `CommitmentRevision` and either no facet or one `ArrangementRevision` explicitly bound to that revision. A new commitment revision must explicitly carry forward a still-compatible facet, advance it, or remove it under the same authorized transition; an arrangement revision cannot attach to a stale commitment revision. Concurrent changes that do not yield one authorized pair remain unresolved. Removing the facet changes only the commitment kind and coordination fields under exact-node authority and never terminates or releases the obligation.

Changing a coordinated slot preserves the commitment identity unless an explicit exact-node commitment transition changes the obligated scope itself. The old slot becomes historical and a successor slot is created or revised; the arrangement facet records the current coordination fields. Slot cancellation or replacement alone never withdraws, releases, replaces, or fulfills the commitment.

The domain kind and its evidentiary confidence are separate. A node may be an external-reliance commitment or its arrangement subtype, while the basis for that classification is inferred, confirmed, or disputed, with provenance and temporal history. Round 03 supersedes the earlier open framing that treated arrangement status as a potentially independent axis.

#### Receipt projections and privacy

Receipts have audience-specific projections rather than one globally reusable record. The internal audit contour may retain only fields necessary for a named audit or legal purpose. Ordinary and external projections use the coarsest sufficient time, provider-local non-exportable references, rotating purpose/audience-scoped tokens, and no stable cross-receipt identifier; exact provider, mandate, result, or timing fields are omitted when they enable unnecessary linkage. They never copy source text, event titles, private participant identities, reconstructible summaries, or “anonymized” inputs without a stated threat model and tested unlinkability criterion. After deletion, retained fields must pass a reidentification/linkability review against calendar and provider auxiliary data and are unavailable to ordinary search, joins, personalization, or inference. Unauthorized, deleted, and nonexistent receipt lookups do not disclose which case applies.

### Evidence-bounded node closure

Evidence is closure-eligible only under an explicit rule keyed to the exact claim type, source class, subject identity, terminal criterion, and any acceptance requirement. Provider execution or provider state is never closure-eligible for real-world fulfillment unless the node's separately adopted acceptance rule explicitly defines that exact provider fact as its terminal criterion. When eligible evidence establishes a terminal fact, Chiplog records the claim. A distinct planning command may then close only the specifically proven node if current authority, policy, exact revision, and claim dependencies still permit it; the claim itself never closes anything. Closing or cancelling a coordinated slot occurrence does not by itself close its commitment identity, the parent intention line, a recurring series, or sibling nodes. Their intention and commitment states persist until independently resolved under their own evidence and authority rules. Subjective or disputed outcomes remain observed-terminal-awaiting-confirmation rather than being promoted to fulfillment.

## Intention-line lifecycle

Root lifecycle has separate but constrained axes. `activity` is `ACTIVE | PAUSED_USER | DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM | RETIRED`; `personal_outcome` is `OPEN | COMPLETED | ABANDONED | REPLACED | TRANSFORMED`. The only valid pairs are `ACTIVE|PAUSED_USER|DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM × OPEN` and `RETIRED × COMPLETED|ABANDONED|REPLACED|TRANSFORMED`; one exact `IntentionLineRevision` transition updates both axes together. `DEACTIVATED_SYSTEM` is a reversible policy state with recorded cause, not user pause, abandonment, or evidence about intent. Split/merge retires a source as `TRANSFORMED`, while one-to-one replacement uses `REPLACED`. Pause, deactivation, reactivation, and reschedule are nonterminal. Delegation changes neither axis by itself. Only an exact root command may select a personal outcome, and it must state the residual scope; a terminal personal outcome cannot claim that delegated, child-work, or external-reliance scope was resolved.

1. **Understand.** Interpret the intention and context without pretending ambiguity disappeared.
2. **Shape.** Expose constraints, conflicts, participants, and consequences; optionally form a personal plan.
3. **Recognize or create commitment when applicable.** Recognize existing external reliance from sufficient evidence, or create it only under sufficient authority; classify coordinated commitments as arrangements without turning arrangement into a separate axis.
4. **Project and act.** Create or update external representations and perform authorized actions, leaving receipts.
5. **Observe.** Track provider state and other evidence for material divergence.
6. **Reconcile.** Establish what happened and offer subject-specific outcomes: recommit, authorized rescope, reschedule, delegate, or replace; pause or abandon applies only to personal/root state and never releases an operative commitment.
7. **Resolve.** Preserve the final outcome and rationale so the intention does not silently disappear.

Silence is not abandonment, fulfillment, or consent to reschedule. External commitments remain open. Chiplog may apply only a pre-agreed reversible fallback, reduce reminder pressure without hiding unresolved status, or system-deactivate a personal intention under policy. It may not silently contact others, reschedule an arrangement, create an obligation, or claim refusal.

The aggregate display is a deterministic product of exact current heads: `identity_state × activity × personal_outcome × owned_work_state × delegation_residual_state × external_reliance_state × affected_party_state`. External reliance distinguishes `NONE | SUSPECTED | LIVE | DISPUTED | RESOLVED`.

`identity_state` is exactly `CURRENT | RETIRED_RESOLVED | RETIRED_SPLIT | RETIRED_MERGED | RETIRED_REPLACED`. A non-transformation `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED` outcome yields `RETIRED_RESOLVED`; split, merge, and replacement yield their named retirement values. `owned_work_state` considers every current, non-historical task, series, and occurrence owned by the root; tombstoned coordinates and transformed predecessors do not participate, while their current successors do. Task and occurrence states `CLOSED | CANCELLED | SKIPPED` and series state `STOPPED` are terminal; all other current lifecycle states, including `PAUSED`, are nonterminal. A cancelled occurrence remains a participating terminal node unless a committed transformation classifies it as a historical predecessor. A skipped coordinate with no occurrence is a historical terminal slot disposition and deliberately does not add a second work node to this reduction. `NONE` means there are no participating nodes; `OPEN` means all participating nodes are nonterminal; `RESOLVED` means all are terminal; `MIXED` means both exist. Each node contributes its own head independently: resolved occurrences do not close an open task, a paused series is open, and a stopped series is terminal even though separately open occurrences still contribute.

`delegation_residual_state` reduces exactly the members of every current `DelegationSetRevision` owned by the root after applying their explicit transformation dispositions. A member in `PROPOSED | AWAITING_ACCEPTANCE | ACTIVE | PERFORMANCE_REPORTED | RECONCILING | PENDING_SOURCE_RESTORE | TARGET_REBIND_REQUIRED` contributes `OPEN`; a member in `RESOLVED_REJECTED | RESOLVED_WITHDRAWN | RESOLVED_EXPIRED | RESOLVED_RETAINED | RESOLVED_PARALLEL | RESOLVED_RELEASED | RESOLVED_TRANSFERRED` contributes `RESOLVED`. `NONE` means no current members, `OPEN` means all open, `RESOLVED` means all terminal, and `MIXED` means both. No evidence, expired target binding, historical predecessor, or overlapping redelegation changes membership implicitly.

Each current commitment head first maps to exactly one contribution class in this order: any current disputed assessment → `DISPUTED`; a nonterminal local representation whose strongest status is suspected → `SUSPECTED`; every other recognized or created nonterminal commitment, whether pre-effective or already operative → `LIVE`; any terminal lifecycle head—including `REPRESENTATION_REJECTED`—→ `RESOLVED`. No other lifecycle/assessment pair is valid. Terminal classification removes the earlier suspected contribution without erasing its history, and the UI distinguishes pre-effective from operative live nodes. `external_reliance_state` then reduces these mutually exclusive classes with precedence `DISPUTED > SUSPECTED > LIVE > RESOLVED > NONE`; absence yields `NONE`. The UI also shows counts so the precedence label never conceals siblings in lower classes.

`affected_party_state` reduces every current separately attributed assertion, standing/applicability assessment, contest, and precautionary constraint bound to the line or any of its nodes, including a commitment. Each item contributes exactly once by stable item ID regardless of how many nodes reference it; commitment binding never transfers or suppresses its contribution. Any disputed item yields `DISPUTED`; otherwise any unresolved assertion, pending contest, or active protective hold yields `OPEN`; otherwise one or more terminally resolved/rejected items yield `RESOLVED`; absence yields `NONE`. A product timeout, commitment transition, or missing resolver cannot mark this axis resolved while the underlying attributed item or protective hold remains current.

A retired or personally resolved root with open work, delegation residuals, reliance, or an affected-party item is displayed explicitly, for example `PERSONAL_ABANDONED / WORK_MIXED / EXTERNAL_LIVE / AFFECTED_PARTY_DISPUTED`; it is never collapsed into terminal, archived, successful, or hidden. Every unresolved child or attributed constraint remains addressable until its own exact transition or an authorized ownership transformation resolves it.

## Conflict handling

There is no universal hierarchy such as “latest instruction always wins.” Conflict policy is user-specific and negotiated in natural language. Chiplog applies the working agreement when it clearly resolves a conflict and makes consequential applications legible. Irreducible value conflicts and contradictory active instructions return to the user. External reliance constrains unilateral changes even when the user's preference changes.

## Proactivity, action authority, and revocation

Observation and proposals can be autonomous; reminders and recurring rituals follow an agreed mandate; reversible external actions may be delegated within its envelope. Third-party impact means changing another person's expectations, access, resources, or view of the user, including messages, publication, shared-resource reservations, invitations, plan changes, availability disclosure, and expected response. A previewable class or batch may share one authorization, but approval renews when audience, meaning, cost, publicity, or obligation character changes.

The user controls proactivity in natural language, including cadence such as “let's plan next week on Sunday evenings.” A proactivity rule is versioned, inspectable, pausable, amendable, and cancellable through conversation. By itself it can generate only labeled proposals; it grants no commit or dispatch authority. Any future bounded auto-execution permission is a separate authority family with explicit scope, expiry, and revocation, never an implication of the cadence request.

Unambiguous suspension or revocation takes effect immediately and blocks locally unstarted steps; it does not wait for a proposal/adoption cycle. Resumption or replacement uses the exact-version adoption path. A plausibly revocatory but ambiguous utterance—such as “stop doing that,” “don't send any more,” or “pause this”—advances a durable ordering generation scoped to the principal plus every plausibly targeted authority family and creates a provisional hold over matching grants, queued actions, recurring chains, and new dispatches before clarification. Every local queue and adapter must acquire against that generation immediately before its irreversible boundary; fan-out workers with a stale generation are fenced. Nothing locally unstarted under that hold may begin. Effects outside this controlled contour are reported as already crossed or `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never promised cancelled. Clarification may narrow the hold but cannot authorize intervening starts, and lifting it requires an explicit resume or new grant bound to the clarified scope. Chiplog safely cancels in-flight work where possible without promising provider rollback, discloses completed or visible effects, and proposes compensation only under separate authority. Revoking the agent's mandate does not itself cancel a human obligation on which another party relies.

### External-action failure protocol

Every external attempt begins with an immutable action intent bound to the exact planning revision, authority-grant version, hold/conflict generation, input dependencies, effect fingerprint, provider target, and provider-scoped idempotency or fencing key. The send adapter's last local action is an atomic transition from dispatch authorization to `SEND_COMMITTED`; it revalidates the current plan, grant, expiry, provisional holds, conflicts, and dependencies against the same ordering domain. That transition is the authoritative irrevocable dispatch boundary: if suspension, revocation, or a hold commits first, `SEND_COMMITTED` is denied; if `SEND_COMMITTED` commits first, the action is treated as having crossed the boundary even before network bytes or provider acknowledgement, and later revocation cannot promise prevention. The audit trail exposes which ordering won. Where a provider supports fencing, it binds the same generation, but the product does not claim external linearizability the provider cannot enforce.

Each independently observable and independently fenceable sub-effect repeats that check and has its own state and receipt. If a provider call can produce multiple effects that the adapter cannot fence and observe separately, the whole call is one inseparable effect bundle and any unresolved mixed result is `PARTIAL`/`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`; a fully evidenced mixed result is `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`. The attempt states are `INTENT_RECORDED | HELD_BEFORE_SEND | CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND | SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND | DISPATCH_AUTHORIZED | SEND_COMMITTED | SENT | CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | PARTIAL | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`; compensation is a separate linked action, not another state of the original attempt. No batch or provider call is assumed transactional across effects. `HELD_BEFORE_SEND` is nonterminal and may resume only through a new dispatch authorization bound to current heads and the same exact fingerprint. `CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND` and `SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND` are terminal proof that the local send boundary was never crossed; any later action requires a new intent. A stale or losing dispatch authorization is retired into one of those states and cannot be reacquired.

After `SEND_COMMITTED`, automatic retransmission is permitted only when a provider idempotency guarantee still covers every transmission and the exact effect, or affirmative evidence proves every earlier transmission permanently incapable of taking effect—for example terminal provider rejection/cancellation, an expired enforced execution lease, or a winning provider fence. A point-in-time read showing no current effect is recorded as absence observed, not terminal no-effect; it remains `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`. Any replacement without the safe retransmission conditions requires visible human authorization that explicitly accepts duplicate risk. Timeout, connection loss, malformed response, conflicting provider state, or expired/unverifiable idempotency likewise becomes `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never “failed” or “nothing happened.”

`ExternalActionIntent` is the immutable exact effect or inseparable effect-bundle subject. Its one authoritative append-only `ExternalActionAttemptRevision` family owns the current logical-attempt head. Each actual network send is a distinct immutable `TransmissionAttempt` child, derived from the intent ID plus a retained request ordinal, and records dispatch time, exact payload fingerprint, provider target, idempotency/fence key, verified coverage interval, transport evidence, and result. The first child is allocated with `SEND_COMMITTED`. A later child may be allocated only from `SEND_COMMITTED`, `SENT`, or `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` after the adapter proves the safe-retransmission condition against every prior child and current authority; it keeps the same logical attempt and cannot reset its state or ambiguity. Duplicate ordinals/fingerprints deduplicate; mismatches reject. Parent reduction is conservative: any child with possible effect prevents `FAILED_NO_EFFECT`; conflicting or incomplete child evidence yields `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` or `PARTIAL`; only evidence covering all children permits a terminal parent outcome. Only the trusted local send adapter may commit the pre-send, child-allocation, and `SEND_COMMITTED` transitions; authenticated provider-result ingestion or an authorized reconciler may append evidence-driven successors. Every writer targets the exact current attempt revision and records its evidence. Legal parent transitions and consequences are closed:

| From | To | Required evidence / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `INTENT_RECORDED` | `HELD_BEFORE_SEND | CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND | SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND | DISPATCH_AUTHORIZED` | current hold/authority/conflict decision; cancel/supersede are terminal local no-send outcomes |
| `HELD_BEFORE_SEND` | `DISPATCH_AUTHORIZED | CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND | SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND` | exact resume/current-head validation, or terminal disposition |
| `DISPATCH_AUTHORIZED` | `HELD_BEFORE_SEND | CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND | SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND | SEND_COMMITTED` | same-generation adapter decision; stale authorization cannot transition |
| `SEND_COMMITTED` | `SENT | CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | PARTIAL` | authenticated transport/provider/reconciliation evidence; absence-at-read never yields `FAILED_NO_EFFECT` |
| `SENT` | `CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | PARTIAL` | provider or reconciliation evidence; remaining merely sent is nonterminal |
| `SEND_COMMITTED` | `SEND_COMMITTED` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | current authority and safe-retransmission proof cover every prior child and exact effect; allocate the next retained ordinal atomically; parent ambiguity does not reset |
| `SENT` | `SENT` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | same guard and atomic child allocation; prior send evidence remains visible |
| `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` | `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` plus one new `TransmissionAttempt` child | same guard and atomic child allocation; retransmission cannot clear or weaken uncertainty |
| `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` | `CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` | later authenticated evidence establishes the whole effect, permanent incapability, or a fully known mixed bundle; otherwise state remains denying |
| `PARTIAL` | `CONFIRMED | FAILED_NO_EFFECT | PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` | later evidence establishes all-effect, no-effect, or exact mixed outcome; otherwise state remains denying |

`CONFIRMED`, `FAILED_NO_EFFECT`, `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED_BEFORE_SEND`, and `SUPERSEDED_BEFORE_SEND` are terminal for the original attempt; confirmation is only of the exact typed provider/effect claim. `PARTIAL_CONFIRMED` preserves the exact occurred/non-occurred sub-effects and keeps dependent planning/commitment reconciliation visible. `FAILED_NO_EFFECT` requires affirmative permanent-incapability evidence for every transmission child and permits a new intent under current authority. Ordinary retry or a new intent is prohibited from `SENT`, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, or `PARTIAL`; only the closed child-allocation rule permits a still-covered idempotent retransmission as continuation of the same attempt. The sole exception is a distinct `AUTHORIZE_DUPLICATE_RISK` command from the authenticated principal after a preview identifies every unresolved prior attempt, possible duplicate effect, affected party/resource, commitment consequence, and safer alternative. It creates a separately fingerprinted recovery intent under fresh current authority, links but never supersedes or clears the original ambiguous attempt, and cannot be inferred from fallback, prior mandate, silence, or generic confirmation.

`OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and `PARTIAL` are deny states: Chiplog stops dependent and remaining batch steps, blocks a conflicting replacement effect unless the exact `AUTHORIZE_DUPLICATE_RISK` exception above is committed, preserves unresolved commitments, and seeks provider reads or human evidence. That exception authorizes only its displayed recovery intent; the original deny state continues to block every other dependent or conflicting action. Silence or a pre-agreed fallback cannot clear the state or continue the chain. Each confirmed partial effect remains independently visible. Compensation is a new previewed `ExternalActionIntent` with its own `ExternalActionAttemptRevision`, authority, fingerprint, dispatch boundary, failure/unknown state, and receipt; a separate recovery overlay may say `COMPENSATION_PENDING | COMPENSATED | COMPENSATION_UNKNOWN | COMPENSATION_PARTIAL` while preserving the original terminal/nonterminal outcome and claim history.

The recovery surface persists until an authorized terminal resolution. It states what was intended, sent, confirmed, unknown or externally visible; what was stopped; which obligations remain; the safe next actions; and the evidence or actor able to resolve ambiguity. Escalation and review deadlines may change ownership or keep a hold, but timeout never clears uncertainty, releases a commitment, or resumes dispatch. Revocation, provisional hold, unresolved conflict, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, and `PARTIAL` take deny precedence over user-specific conflict policy, stale queues, fallback rules, or “latest instruction.” The exact storage/outbox implementation remains proposed, but these observable product outcomes are adopted.

## Multi-party coordination and privacy

Chiplog always has one explicit principal; it is not a neutral representative of everyone involved. There is no cross-principal canonical intention-line root. Each agent retains a separately principal-owned root. `SharedCoordination` has an internal immutable identity but exposes only pairwise or audience/epoch-scoped pseudonymous handles designed to resist cross-audience correlation under a named threat model. They are not absolutely unlinkable: Chiplog as issuer, colluding audiences, timing/membership patterns, and parties with auxiliary data may still correlate them, so each release profile measures linkage risk. Its append-only revisions record participant-attributed membership and only bindings already authorized by each principal's local binding revision. Each released field has an owner, reader set, purpose, retention, and onward-disclosure rule; local-to-shared release requires that owner's authority. No actor may create or rebind another principal's local root, and access granted to one principal or provider is non-transitive. Revocation stops future access, rotates exposed handles, and leaves only party-scoped history justified by an independent retention basis. Personal agents may exchange bounded proposals and attributed evidence through this contour while retaining private context and separate authority boundaries. No remote proposal, evidence item, provider object, or accepted shared record becomes a local planning head; it can only support a separately authorized local command. Divergent local states remain visibly attributed rather than being resolved by last writer or provider state.

In a dispute, no principal's interpretation becomes shared truth by default. Chiplog preserves separately attributed positions and evidence, represents only its own principal's authorized position, and changes another party's expectations only through explicit communication or accepted shared records. Internal reclassification cannot settle what another person promised, accepted, or understood.

An affected-party assertion, its evidence, a standing or legitimacy assessment, and an operative constraint are distinct. Each has provenance and is contestable. Ambiguous standing blocks only actions that could prejudice the disputed party; otherwise Chiplog presents the uncertainty and seeks clarification rather than silently treating either side's interpretation as canonical.

Chiplog does not adjudicate contested standing. It applies only a named source of constraint—an accepted shared record, explicit party agreement, applicable external rule, or the principal's adopted policy—and shows the evidence and contest route. When sources conflict or no deciding rule exists, it preserves both positions and escalates; pending resolution it blocks only irreversible or expectation-changing actions that could harm the disputed party, while reversible private planning remains available. Product classification never creates a right or extinguishes one.

The claimant bears the policy-defined minimum evidence burden before a precautionary block begins. Where contact is feasible and safe, an affected person receives notice, an accessible view of the attributed claim and evidence used, and a channel to correct or contest it. The block names its scope, expiry, anti-abuse rule, and resolver—an agreed party process, applicable external authority, or a human decision explicitly permitted by the governing relationship. It cannot renew from the same evidence.

A missing resolver, expired process deadline, or product failure never extinguishes the attributed claim. If no credible risk of irreversible prejudice remains, the product-imposed hold expires while the unresolved dispute stays visible. If such risk remains, Chiplog narrows the hold to the minimum reversible protection, escalates to the named outside review path, and schedules a time-bounded reassessment; it cannot silently grant the principal the disputed advantage merely because its own resolution process failed.

The external coordination contour is default-deny. An authenticated visitor receives a sender-constrained, audience-restricted, event/purpose-scoped authorization credential bound to that visitor, principal, date window, expiry, fixed resolution, release snapshot, query budget, and permitted result. It uses proof of possession, replay protection, revocation, and protected non-exportable key storage where available; compromise of the bound key/session remains in the threat model. It returns coarse fixed-bucket availability or candidate slots rather than raw busy intervals whenever possible. Arbitrary overlapping windows, exact edge probes, negative “changed/not changed” disclosures, and alias/session budget resets are forbidden; requests across credentials, endpoints, sessions, exports, and cache paths share a correlation-domain disclosure budget. Repeated responses within a release epoch are snapshot-pinned, and uncertain linkage or differencing risk denies or coarsens the response. Query and response telemetry is governed by the same privacy and deletion rules. Event names, task names, descriptions, recurrence patterns, exact private boundaries, and reasons remain unavailable.

Event-specific access additionally requires an authenticated event-scoped participant identity and current participation accepted under the governing invitation rule; forwarding the URL, invitation, or token alone grants no access. The visitor receives a participant-facing projection rather than source-event access: the minimum necessary time/timezone, their own participation status, and a participant-accessible place or expiring link. Every optional title, agenda, instruction, identity, or change field carries author/data-subject provenance, permitted audience, purpose, expiry, and revocation behavior, and requires every authority whose protected data it contains. Conflicting designations withhold the field while preserving only minimum logistics. Internal notes, tags, tasks, neighboring-block reasons, hidden attendees, and undeclared identities remain unavailable. New or edited fields never inherit an older field's release. Owner-authored explanation travels as a separate authorized message and does not widen calendar access. Participant links are audience-bound, expiring, revocable, and stripped of unrelated provider identifiers. This data-access boundary is a participation condition and cannot be relaxed by one principal where another person's protected data is involved.

## Constitutional safeguards

The following are institution-authored participation conditions: the working agreement cannot require Chiplog to violate them, so an otherwise requested action may be refused or narrowed. They do not by themselves authorize an intervention, settle an underlying right, or override another person's agency:

- no high-stakes value decision on the user's behalf;
- no external action without mandate and no silent mandate expansion;
- no inference presented as fact or unsupported promise attributed to another person;
- no concealed action or retroactive rewriting of the audit trail;
- no ordinary-product use of logically deleted data;
- no manipulation or weaponization of private vulnerabilities;
- minimum necessary disclosure between agents;
- no materially deceptive human impersonation;
- external-contour calendar minimization as defined above.

Involuntary interruption or intervention requires the separate constitutional-risk contract above and is limited to its narrow, published imminent-irreversible-harm categories. A participation refusal must name the applicable condition and leaves the principal free to choose a lawful course outside Chiplog; it is not evidence that the principal's goal or another person's claim is invalid.

## Interaction model

Conversation remains primary for intent, policy, inspection, correction, and control, while other views may improve legibility. Chiplog is concise by default and increases explanation with ambiguity, risk, or consequence. Proposals state the material action and consequence; receipts make action attempts and observed effects independently inspectable without calling an unknown or partial outcome completed.

Every aggregate state renders first as scoped plain-language sentences, never only internal enums. It says what the principal changed, what remains open, and why: for example, “You abandoned your personal goal. Two work items remain. Your commitment to Alex is still live. Alex's disputed claim currently blocks rescheduling.” Each unresolved component shows its owner or governing authority, personal/external/attributed character, current versus historical status, consequence, blocking condition, and only bounded available actions such as inspect evidence, correct or contest a claim, contact the right-holder, propose release, reconcile, or deliberately leave unresolved.

Every consequential utterance follows a legible path: interpret, propose when planning would change, obtain the required authority, then commit or reject/cancel. Inferred and defaulted fields show their source and confidence and never count as user intent. Ambiguity about subject, scope, time, audience, obligation, recurrence, delegation, cadence, or external effect triggers clarification. A factual report appends only a claim unless the user separately authorizes a displayed planning delta. Post-commit correction routes separately to claim correction or a new planning proposal. Every consequential conversation card or receipt uses the same `PLAN`, `FACT CLAIM`, or `PROPOSED PLANNING CHANGE` distinction as the journal and states `planning changed: yes/no`.

One adoption gesture can activate only one exact proposal version unless Chiplog displays an explicit bundle with every independently consequential item, per-item selected state, combined consequences, and one unambiguous bundle adoption. A correction result shows the preserved historical claim, the new current epistemic view, whether Plan stayed unchanged, every `EVIDENCE_DIVERGED` node, and the available reconciliation action.

Each rule and authority family has one active version at an explicit effective time. Revision, suspension, or revocation supersedes that head prospectively, and every queued or recurring action must revalidate against the current head before starting. Overlapping versions never resolve by recency alone; if the adopted conflict policy cannot select one, the action remains held and Chiplog asks in natural language.

Journal entries distinguish `PLAN`, `FACT CLAIM`, and `PROPOSED PLANNING CHANGE`; identify source and historical/current subject; explain what confirmation does and does not establish; show occurred and observed times, uncertainty, conflict and correction lineage; and say explicitly whether planning changed. Available actions remain separate: correct or retract a report, dispute evidence, propose a plan change, authorize it, or cancel it.

The initial Telegram journey is an intention-to-personal-plan projection, even though the historical brief called the calendar block a “real commitment.” It demonstrates one proposal-confirmation-receipt flow; explicit confirmation is not the only future authority mechanism.

### Evaluation and release governance

Exposure has exactly two modes under one monotone `DeploymentGateGeneration`: `evaluation_eligible := current READY ∧ current EvaluationAuthorization.ACTIVE` for the authorization's exact cohort, cap, duration, purpose, instrumentation, and stop rules; and `production_eligible := current READY ∧ unbreached current VisionReleaseProfile.ACTIVE` for the exact production capability/cohort. “Unbreached current” means the exact active head is unexpired, every `[LIVE]` predicate and freshness lease is currently `PASS`, its authoritative evidence cursors match, and its scoped `OpenCauseSet` is empty. Every operation in the capability-surface attestation that can initiate or enlarge cohort-visible effect, data disclosure, authority use, or evaluation exposure must name one mode and atomically revalidate its exact pair, current evidence cursors, and bounded freshness leases at its last reversible boundary. A path may omit its own check only when the attestation proves it is downstream of another checked boundary with no independent start, replay, or fan-out route; an unmatched effect boundary invalidates readiness. Evaluation-originated subjects, state, authority, and entitlements cannot cross into production without a fresh production-bound check.

Readiness invalidation, a committed hard-harm determination, stale or interrupted predicate lease, unresolved evidence gap, release hold, revocation, expiry, or supersession advances the generation before further exposure and makes the corresponding eligibility predicate false; a stale worker is fenced. Evidence sources unable to meet the profile's latency bound force the implicated path to `HELD`. Detection and containment latency remain measured residuals: the document does not claim invalidation before evidence reaches the authoritative cursor. Work already across the declared boundary is reported and contained, not promised recalled. Every hold records which gate, binding, or post-launch condition produced it. A committed checklist breach makes `ACTIVE` non-current before or atomically with containment, so a held profile can never satisfy production eligibility. Neither gate transition can issue, modify, or waive the other.

Success is judged only for intention lines the user has explicitly entrusted to Chiplog's managed contour, not for every passing statement and not by raw completion. Resolution is assessed separately for each authority-bearing mode. A personal intention may reach a terminal outcome through completion, replacement, abandonment, or the principal's explicit decision not to spend further attention on its residual personal scope; conscious revision, rescheduling, delegation, and pause remain nonterminal unless a separate exact root command selects such an outcome. Each external-reliance commitment must independently be fulfilled, released, transferred, replaced with acceptance under its governing rule, or remain explicitly unresolved, and every affected-party claim retains its attributed standing and contest state. The aggregate line is never labeled successful or wholly resolved while any owned work, delegation residual, external-reliance node, attributed affected-party assertion, contest, or protective hold remains unresolved.

Metrics are diagnostic, not optimization targets. Evaluation combines user-reported usefulness, coordination time and steps, arrangement integrity, cognitive-load reduction, and resolution patterns, while separately measuring system-initiated pressure, ignored prompts, unwanted interventions, reopening after declared non-interest, revoked mandates, unauthorized actions, compensations, false arrangement or commitment inference, and clarification burden. Chiplog must not optimize resolution rate or convert more utterances into durable lines to improve a metric. Reduced activity or disengagement counts only according to the user's stated preference, never as an invitation to pull the user back into the loop.

No aggregate score authorizes rollout. Unauthorized action; pressure after rejection; reopening after declared non-interest; unauthorized prejudice to an affected party; silent closure, release, or reclassification of another person's claim; denial of required notice or contest; and disclosure beyond participant-facing authority are hard regression constraints rather than tradeable averages. Evaluation uses user-controlled success criteria for the principal's personal mode alongside independently attributed affected-party constraints, and checks longitudinal and subgroup harms; experiments, training, staff incentives, and launch decisions audit whether their proxies changed agent behavior in ways that undermine these constraints.

Any family/source/event combination absent from the registry is rejected. The profile table below is the field and evaluation-predicate registry; it does not redefine transitions.

The versioned `ReleasePredicateRegistry` contains the exact profile-row IDs below plus `REL-STATE-CURRENT`, `REL-EVAL-ELIGIBLE`, `REL-PROD-ELIGIBLE`, `REL-GATE-FRESHNESS`, and every transition guard in the canonical registry. Each entry binds phase, exact inputs, whole-conjunction evaluator, `PASS | FAIL | MISSING | STALE` result domain, authoritative cursor/freshness lease where applicable, failure-cause type, and evaluator/version digest. A row is `PASS` only when its entire pass-condition conjunction is proven; no child interpretation may waive a conjunct. The frozen profile and every authorization/approval bind the exact registry digest and results. The table is the authored reviewer-facing projection of those stable row IDs, not a separate predicate source.

Concurrent readiness invalidations and release/evaluation holds serialize against the exact `DeploymentGateGeneration`. Each winning transition appends a stable cause identity, scope, source cursor, and evidence to an immutable `CauseLedger`; a loser retries against the successor generation, so simultaneous causes coalesce without erasure. A separately authorized `CauseResolution` binds one exact cause, resolving authority, evidence or changed scope, decision time, and successor gate generation. `OpenCauseSet` is the deterministic projection `introduced causes − currently valid resolutions`, scoped to the capability/cohort/mode; history is never removed. No successor `READY`, evaluation authorization, or production activation may clear a cause implicitly: its approval must inherit the ledger and bind every resolution it relies on. Eligibility requires the scoped `OpenCauseSet` to be empty. Concurrent resolutions also CAS the exact generation; a stale resolution retries and cannot erase a newly introduced or differently scoped cause.

The constraint owner is organizationally and permission-wise separate from growth, model-training, experiment, and launch owners. Appointment and removal belong to a differently constituted oversight authority that includes affected-cohort representation. Changes that weaken a threshold or measurement obligation require that same outside authority and cannot take effect in the release cycle or experiment they govern. A hold always names scope, owner, deadline, exit evidence, and escalation; it never means continuing breached behavior without cohort expansion. Full rollout never converts approval into permanence. These adopted product-governance requirements are distinct from the standalone's proposed `OperationalAdoptionProfile` schema and Annex C realization.

## Terminology and closest prior art

Pairwise pseudonymous handles follow the risk-reduction pattern of [OpenID Connect pairwise subject identifiers](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0-18.html#PairwiseAlg); they do not claim absolute unlinkability or anonymity. Sender-constrained visitor credentials are closest to proof-of-possession mechanisms such as [OAuth DPoP (RFC 9449)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9449.html) and inherit their key/session-compromise limits. The Plan/Fact/Journal split is a product-domain authority boundary, not a novelty claim about event sourcing, CQRS, audit logs, or workflow systems.

## Historical seed: the MVP

The stream brief specified the intended smallest auditable action loop: an allowed owner expresses an intention in Telegram; Chiplog proposes a precise calendar action; explicit confirmation authorizes one intended-idempotent Google Calendar write; a journal links request, approval, and provider identity; and Chiplog returns a receipt. No implementation version, evaluation artifact, provider-condition test, or observed result is cited here, so this is historical design intent rather than evidence that the loop was built or validated.

The brief aimed to establish proposal before that write, explicit authority, idempotency, and a checkable trace. Even future evidence for that exact loop would validate only its named version and provider conditions—not the long-term action, receipt, authority, privacy, or rollout contracts in this vision. It does not freeze Google Calendar as the core model, explicit confirmation as the only authority mechanism, single-user operation as the target scope, or MVP exclusions as permanent exclusions.

## Open design issues

Accepted decisions leave a smaller set of material questions:

- which claim types and source/acceptance rules may support a proposed exact-node release, fulfillment, or withdrawal when reliance may have ended, and which authority may adopt that transition;
- the accessible designation/contest procedure and resolution authority for participant-facing fields when protected-data authorities disagree;
- how opaque receipt correlation can support authorized audit without becoming an ordinary-product linkage channel.

These are product decisions for later interview rounds, not implementation details to fill in silently.

They are tracked by a versioned blocker registry with closed applicability predicates, not only broad profile labels. `OPEN-CLOSURE-01` applies to every exact capability/version/cohort atom that can propose, authorize, execute, evidence, or render commitment release, fulfillment, withdrawal, or closure and binds the corresponding `SEM-COMMIT-01` atoms. `OPEN-PARTY-01` applies to every atom that can designate, contest, resolve, read, write, infer, or disclose participant-facing fields and binds its `SEM-COMMIT-01` and `SEM-PRIV-01` atoms. `OPEN-RECEIPT-01` applies to every atom that can issue, correlate, resolve, expose, or authorize audit/receipt linkage and binds its `SEM-PRIV-01` atoms. During the capability-surface fixed point, the independent attester evaluates every open record against every exact `ReleaseCapability` version and cohort/context atom; each match materializes a blocker binding containing the open ID, exact capability/version/cohort/context, exact requirement atoms, predicate version, and evidence. Every unclassified, disputed, or uncertain match yields `HOLD_ADOPTION`; a broad class name is navigation only and can never substitute for this binding. These records are owned by the product-design decision process with the affected-party and privacy representation required above; until an exact admissible resolution is adopted into the canonical body and all dependent bindings are recomputed, the bound capability remains absent or `HOLD_ADOPTION`.
