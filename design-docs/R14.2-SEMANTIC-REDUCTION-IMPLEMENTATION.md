# R14.2: selected semantic reduction

## Status

Open. The mounted owner preparation, writer with predecessor CAS, physical
schema, historical selector, and consumer reader for
`chiplog.loop.semantic-reduction.v1` are present. The existing mounted path covers
the initial no-late-evidence reduction. It does not yet prove a late R16 evidence
append followed by a successor reduction after reopen. A stale consumable
reduction must keep the next Turn on HOLD during that interval.

## Boundary

The resolver batch and semantic reduction are separate publications. The resolver
selects closure, recovered outcome, and resolver batch together. The reduction is
an appendable stream with its own predecessor CAS; later compatible evidence must
advance the current reduction while preserving `reduction_id`, semantic class,
and consumability. A rival or contradictory refinement must select a typed HOLD
head, so an older consumable head cannot remain current.

The selected R16 witness for a refinement can be a late `APPEND_EVIDENCE` record
whose obligation remains `CLOSED`. R14 accepts it only when the selected owner
journal, R14 loop journal, and physical publication rows form one complete,
contiguous tenant sequence from the current reduction to the new source cut.
Every intervening publication must be an exact same-intent, same-obligation
`APPEND_EVIDENCE`; gaps, forks, unknown rows, or other operations hold. The
current evidence list must extend the prior list, and the original resolver's
closure, recovered outcome, and batch anchors stay fixed. A neutral receipt can
refine provenance without duplicating child coverage; rival or contradictory
evidence selects a typed HOLD reduction.

The consumer joins the selected reduction to the current authenticated R16 cut.
The complete ordered physical evidence references and latest selected witness
must match. While they differ, the previous consumable reduction cannot authorize
the next Turn.

`Absent` is valid only after the broker has authenticated and completely
enumerated both selected loop decisions and physical loop reduction rows for the
tenant and stream. A hard-coded empty tuple or the Effects reader's closed schema
set does not prove absence from the loop-owned stream.

## Implemented in the current slice

1. One shared interval validator is used in source capture, reduction startup, and
   historical replay. It stitches owner and loop decisions by tenant sequence,
   checks their predecessor/result commitments and exact physical rows, and
   rejects any interval publication outside the R16 append allowlist.
2. The selected reduction reader retains a valid historical reduction across a
   verified R16 append suffix. The recovery consumer separately compares the
   reduction evidence and latest witness with the current cut; only a successor
   reduction with exact current evidence can clear HOLD.

## Remaining verification

## Required mounted evidence

- First reduction from a completely enumerated `Absent` stream; exact selected
  bytes and source references survive close/reopen and idempotent replay.
- Compatible evidence refinement selects the exact next predecessor and keeps
  the same reduction identity, semantic class, and consumable disposition.
- Sequence gaps, unknown or foreign interleavings, and evidence from another
  intent fail closed without selecting a successor.
- After reopen, the refined record is current and the old head fails the consumer
  join.
- Rival or contradictory evidence selects a typed HOLD reduction; the previous
  consumable record cannot authorize continuation.
- Missing, duplicate, reordered, foreign, partial, or orphan evidence and
  reduction rows fail closed without selecting a successor or issuing another
  SEND.

These checks use the mounted writer and reader with bounded fixtures. Load,
stress, and broad combinatorial tests remain deferred to final integration under
the user's 2026-09-28 instruction.

## Scope guard

reopen. Publishing a successor Turn after recovery is R14.3 and remains separate.
No R15–R17 implementation is part of this slice.
